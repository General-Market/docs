There is no deposit. There is no withdrawal. Your wallet is your account; the protocol moves funds in and out of escrow when you stake or claim. The vocabulary of "deposit" and "withdraw" exists here only because users expect it.

What this page actually covers: how to fund a wallet, how funds reach the protocol, how they come back, and what happens when something is sent to the wrong address.

## Testnet collateral

During testnet, bets are placed in **devnet SOL**. Devnet SOL has no monetary value — it exists for testing. The protocol treats it identically to mainnet SOL on the contract side; only the network differs. [FACT TBD: testnet collateral — SOL only, or USDC-devnet alongside]

For mainnet, the collateral asset is TBA — see [Audit & Mainnet Roadmap](/trust/audit-and-mainnet-roadmap).

## Fund the wallet (devnet)

Devnet SOL is free, dispensed by faucets. Most wallets link directly to one.

1. Open your wallet. Confirm it is on **devnet** — see [Wallet Setup](/help/wallet-setup).
2. Click **Receive** or **Faucet** in the wallet UI.
3. If a faucet button is present, request 1–2 SOL. If not, visit a Solana devnet faucet and paste your address.
4. Wait. The SOL appears within seconds.

Faucets rate-limit aggressively. One request per minute, sometimes per hour. If a faucet refuses you, try another.

## Fund the wallet (mainnet, when live)

When mainnet launches, you will fund the wallet by sending SOL — or whatever the mainnet collateral asset turns out to be — from another Solana wallet, an exchange withdrawal, or an on-ramp service. The mechanics are the same as any Solana transfer. nsgame does not run an on-ramp.

Before sending real funds:

- Confirm the **network**. Solana mainnet, not Ethereum, not BNB, not anything else. Cross-chain transfers to a Solana address are unrecoverable.
- Confirm the **address**. Copy from the wallet. Paste. Verify the first four and last four characters match.
- Send a **test amount** first if you are sending a large balance. The first transfer is the riskiest one.

## Place a bet

Funding the wallet is not depositing. The protocol takes nothing from your balance until you stake.

1. Browse the catalog. Pick a market.
2. Pick a side — A or B.
3. Enter a stake. Minimum **$0.10**. Maximum uncapped.
4. Click **Bet**. The wallet asks you to sign a transaction.
5. Approve.

The transaction transfers stake from your wallet to the program's escrow account on Solana. Two confirmations is enough — Solana finality is fast. The bet appears in your portfolio within seconds. Network fees are paid in SOL, separate from the stake.

## Claim a payout

If your side wins, the protocol holds your share of the pool until you claim it. Claims are not automatic by default — gas costs say claims should be batched in your head, not in the contract.

1. Open the market after settlement.
2. Click **Claim**.
3. Sign the transaction.
4. The payout — pool share minus 0.3% rake — lands in the wallet you placed the bet from.

Some void-and-refund flows auto-refund where the gas math makes claiming absurd. See [Refund Policy](/product/refund-policy) for which.

## Withdraw from nsgame

There is nothing to withdraw. The funds are already in your wallet. Move them where you want — another Solana address, an exchange, a hardware wallet. The protocol has no view of where they go.

## Network fees

Solana network fees are paid by the wallet, in SOL. Per-transaction cost is small — fractions of a cent — but the wallet must hold enough SOL to cover them. A wallet with stake but no fee buffer cannot transact.

Recommended buffer: **0.05 SOL** (devnet or mainnet). More than enough for several rounds of placing bets and claiming.

## Confirmations

Solana settles in ~1 second per slot. Two confirmations — about two seconds — is the practical threshold. The wallet UI shows the confirmation; the protocol UI updates within a slot or two of that.

If a transaction stalls past 60 seconds, it likely failed. Check the wallet's transaction history; it will say so.

## Common failures

- **Sent to a wrong address.** Solana transfers are irreversible. The funds are gone. There is no support flow that recovers them.
- **Sent on a wrong network.** A transfer to a Solana address from an Ethereum wallet does not arrive — the bytes go nowhere a Solana node will read them. Funds may be recoverable through the originating chain's tooling; nsgame cannot help.
- **Insufficient SOL for fees.** The transaction fails before signing. Top up SOL.
- **Wallet on mainnet, site on devnet (or vice versa).** Connection succeeds, transactions fail. Switch network.
- **Wallet locked mid-transaction.** Re-unlock. Re-sign. The transaction either confirms or expires.

## What we do not do

We do not custody funds. We cannot freeze accounts. We cannot reverse transactions. We cannot recover lost seed phrases. We cannot route funds back from the wrong chain. The chain is the disclosure; the chain is also the limit. See [Risk Disclosure](/legal/risk-disclosure).
