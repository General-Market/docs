A wallet is your account, your password, and your bank — collapsed into one file. Treat it accordingly.

nsgame is non-custodial. We never see your keys. We never hold your funds. The protocol talks to your wallet; the wallet decides what to sign. If you lose the keys, the funds are unreachable. Nobody at nsgame can recover them. Nobody anywhere can.

## Choose a wallet

Two wallets are tested against the site. Either works.

- **Phantom** — [phantom.app](https://phantom.app) — browser extension, mobile app. The default for most Solana users.
- **Solflare** — [solflare.com](https://solflare.com) — browser extension, mobile app, web vault. The alternative if Phantom misbehaves.

Both are open-source on the wallet side, both support Ledger hardware wallets, both work on Chrome, Firefox, Brave, and Safari. Pick one. Don't run both at once — they will fight over which wallet handles the connection prompt.

## Install

1. Visit the wallet's official site. Verify the URL — typosquatted wallet domains are a known attack.
2. Install the browser extension or the mobile app.
3. Create a new wallet. The wallet will display a **seed phrase** — twelve or twenty-four words.
4. Write the seed phrase down. On paper. Not in a screenshot. Not in a password manager that syncs to the cloud. Not in your email drafts.
5. Confirm the seed phrase when the wallet asks.

## The seed phrase

The seed phrase is your wallet. Anyone who reads it can drain every account derived from it. nsgame will never ask for it. Phantom will never ask for it. Solflare will never ask for it. If anyone asks for it — including someone claiming to be support — they are stealing from you.

We cannot recover a lost seed phrase. There is no password reset. There is no "forgot wallet" flow. The asymmetry is total: your only defense is that you wrote it down somewhere a fire wouldn't reach.

## Switch to devnet

nsgame runs on Solana devnet during testnet. You will need to point the wallet at devnet before connecting.

**Phantom:**
1. Open the extension.
2. Settings → Developer Settings → Change Network.
3. Select **Devnet**.

**Solflare:**
1. Open the extension.
2. Click the network selector at the top.
3. Select **Devnet**.

Mainnet wallets work with mainnet. Devnet wallets work with devnet. The two ledgers do not communicate. A balance on one is invisible to the other.

## Get devnet SOL

Devnet SOL is free. It is also worthless — that is the point of devnet.

Visit a Solana faucet (the wallet may link directly to one) and request a small amount. Faucets rate-limit; one request per session is usually enough. The SOL appears in the wallet within seconds. See [Deposits & Withdrawals](/help/deposit-withdraw) for the rest.

## Connect to nsgame

1. Open [nsgame.org](https://nsgame.org).
2. Pass the age gate.
3. Click **Connect Wallet** in the header.
4. Pick your wallet from the prompt.
5. Approve the connection in the wallet popup.

The wallet asks you to sign a message — not a transaction. Signing a message proves you control the address. It does not move funds. It does not authorise a trade. Read what each prompt says before signing; assume nothing.

## When something fails

- **Wallet popup never appears:** another wallet extension is intercepting the connection. Disable all but one.
- **"Wrong network" error:** the wallet is on mainnet. Switch to devnet.
- **"Insufficient funds" with non-zero balance:** the balance is on the wrong network. Re-check.
- **Connection hangs:** refresh the page. Disconnect from the wallet. Reconnect.
