A round closes. Somebody has to read the data, decide who won, and tell the chain. That somebody is not a person; it is a network of oracles signing collectively. The signature is what unlocks the pool. No signature, no payout. No quorum, no signature.

## Goldilock — the oracle network

Goldilock is the multi-oracle network that resolves nsgame markets. At mainnet, its operators are elected by the DAO. During testnet, its operators are the founding team. Same protocol, different roster. See [Goldilock Oracle Network](/trust/goldilock-oracle-network) for the full disclosure.

There is no single "the oracle." Settlement requires more than one signer to agree. A reading from one node is not a settlement; a quorum of signed readings is.

## What the oracles do

For each market, at lock time:

1. Each oracle node reads the configured data source independently — its own scrape, its own request, its own response. See [Resolution Sources](/product/resolution-sources).
2. Each node computes the result locally. F1 markets compute view-count delta. F2 markets compute the aggregated viewer total. See [Market Rules](/product/market-rules).
3. Each node signs a settlement message asserting the winner. The signature is over the pair index, the close timestamp, the readings, and the declared winner.
4. The signatures aggregate. When quorum is reached, the aggregate signature posts to the on-chain program. The program verifies the signature and unlocks the pool.

The exact quorum threshold is fixed in the program. Below it, the settlement transaction is rejected. Above it, the settlement is final.

## What the program does on receipt

The on-chain prediction-market program — `DQwMnwQGYuLDvciSFZNgUvcHkA3Buyhk3ejgbACvSydA` on Solana devnet — verifies the aggregated signature, marks the market resolved, records the winning side, and opens the claim path. The pool sits in a program PDA the entire time; settlement is the moment the PDA's authority changes from "frozen" to "claimable by winners."

The signature is not a hint. It is the only thing that flips the market state. The program does not trust IP addresses, hostnames, signed messages from a single key, or off-chain JSON. It trusts the aggregated signature against the registered oracle key set.

## What happens if quorum is not reached

Three failure modes, all with the same destination:

- Not enough oracles came back with a reading.
- Oracles disagreed on the winner — disagreement at the integer level, not noise.
- The aggregated signature failed verification on-chain.

In all three cases, the market does not resolve. After the resolution deadline passes, the market is forced into the void path. Stakes refund. See [Refund Policy](/product/refund-policy).

The protocol does not fall back to "best guess." The protocol does not fall back to "majority of available signers if a node was offline." Either the quorum signed cleanly or the round voids. There is no third state.

## What the user sees

Before lock — the market accepts bets.

At lock — the market shows "resolving."

After settlement — the winner is declared, the claim button activates for winners, the round shows "resolved" with the on-chain settlement transaction linked.

If the round voids — the market shows "voided" with a short reason ("data unavailable", "tied outcome", "quorum not reached", "source rate-limited"), and the refund path activates. See [Refund Policy](/product/refund-policy).

The settlement transaction is on Solana. Anyone can open the explorer and verify it. The readings the oracles used are published from the oracle node. Anyone can re-derive the winner from the same source data. That is the entire trust model — the chain says what happened, the oracle node says what it read, both are public, and the math is public.

## Operator framing — testnet and mainnet

During testnet: the founding team operates the Goldilock nodes. The signing keys are held by the team. The team can, in principle, refuse to sign or sign incorrectly. The chain would still verify the signature; the bettors would still be exposed to operator behaviour. This is acknowledged, not hidden. The mitigations are: every reading is published, every settlement is verifiable, and the audit path documented in [Audit & Mainnet Roadmap](/trust/audit-and-mainnet-roadmap) is the gate to mainnet.

At mainnet: Goldilock operators are elected by the DAO. The signing keys rotate to elected operators. The same multi-oracle quorum protocol runs; the roster of signers is no longer the team. See [Decentralization & DAO](/trust/decentralization-and-dao).

The protocol does not change between testnet and mainnet. The trust assumption does. We say so plainly because pretending otherwise would be the worse choice.
