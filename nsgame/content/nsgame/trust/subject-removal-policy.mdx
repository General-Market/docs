If you are a subject of a market on nsgame.org and you want to be removed, this is the page for you. Read the first paragraph carefully — it is the only paragraph that matters before you decide whether to file the form.

Why this policy exists, and what it does not claim, is in [Listing Posture](/trust/listing-posture). This page is the workflow; that page is the argument.

**The website is something we control. The protocol is something we don't.** We can delist a market from nsgame.org. The protocol does not delist it from the Solana program, because the program does not belong to us — it belongs to the chain, and the chain forgets nothing. A removal request, when granted, removes a subject from this website's catalog. Anyone running a different frontend can keep the market visible. Anyone reading the chain can keep transacting against it. We are honest about this because pretending otherwise would waste your time.

## What we can do

- **Remove the market from the nsgame.org catalog.** It will not appear on the homepage, the boards, the search, or any nsgame.org URL. New rounds for the removed subject will not be displayed.
- **Remove the subject from any imagery, copy, or feature module on nsgame.org.** Profile pages, leaderboards, hero sections — gone.
- **Make the removal permanent on nsgame.org.** Across rounds. Across redesigns. The website remembers, even though the protocol does not.

## What we cannot do

- **Delete the on-chain history.** The program has settled rounds. The settlements are on Solana forever. Anyone with a block explorer can find them.
- **Stop the protocol from continuing to settle that subject's market.** As long as the program runs and another frontend submits transactions, the market exists.
- **Delist you from a tube site.** We read xvideos, xnxx, pornhub, chaturbate. We do not operate them. If you want your performer profile removed at the source, you contact the source.
- **Remove you from a third-party indexer or archive.** Once data is on-chain, it is in the public record.

The honest sentence again: the website is something we control; the protocol is something we don't. If you need protocol-level removal, the answer is no, and it is no everywhere — there is no operator that can give you yes.

## How to request removal

A four-step workflow, plus a separate legal channel for non-ordinary cases. The order is non-negotiable.

1. **Submit the form** at `[FACT TBD: removal form URL — published when available]`. You provide the subject identity (the performer name as listed in the nsgame.org catalog, plus the source platform), a contact email or other channel you control, and a one-line reason. Anonymous submissions are accepted but cannot be processed past step 1, because step 2 needs a contact channel.
2. **We send a verification link** to the contact you provided. Click it within `[FACT TBD: verification link expiry — e.g. 7 days]`. This step exists to prevent third parties from filing on your behalf without your knowledge. We do not want to delist a subject because someone in a Discord server volunteered to delist them.
3. **You provide proof of identity.** One of:
   - Government-issued photo ID matching the performer name (or a documented stage-name link).
   - Verified-account proof from the source platform — a screenshot or post from the platform's authenticated account that we can cross-check, or a control message we ask you to publish briefly from the verified profile.
   - Both. Either alone is acceptable.
   The proof is reviewed by the founding team, used once for the verification, and deleted. We do not retain identity documents past the verification step. We are not in the KYC business.
4. **The website removes the market from the UI.** Removal SLAs, by report type, from receipt of verified proof:
   - **Standard subject removal**: target 7 calendar days from ID verification.
   - **NCII fast-track**: 48 hours from credible report, per the Take It Down Act compliance posture. The form is the same; the priority is different.
   - **Regulator notice or court order**: 24 hours from receipt.

   The removal is permanent across rounds. We will confirm the removal at the contact channel from step 2.

5. **Legal contact, for matters that bypass the form.** `[FACT TBD: legal@nsgame.org or designated agent email]`. Use this for cease-and-desist letters, court orders, regulator notices, and DSA Art. 16 notice-and-action submissions. The form is faster for ordinary removals. The legal channel exists for the cases that are not ordinary.

## What the form asks for

- The performer name, as listed.
- The source platform — xvideos, xnxx, pornhub, chaturbate.
- A contact channel you control.
- An optional reason.
- A confirmation that you are the subject, or are the subject's verified representative, or are reporting on behalf of someone who cannot represent themselves (a minor, a deceased person, a victim of non-consensual content). The third case routes to a different path — see "Other categories" below.

## Other categories

Some removal requests are not from the subject. We treat each differently.

- **Non-consensual content / privacy violation.** Treated with priority. **NCII fast-track**: 48 hours from credible report to UI delisting, per the Take It Down Act compliance posture. The subject does not need to verify identity in the same way; the verification is whether the depicted person was non-consensually depicted, and the proof is whichever proof you can provide. We err in favor of removal in this category.
- **Reports about a minor.** Removed immediately on report. We do not require verification before removing; the verification follows the removal. If the report is itself bad-faith, the market can be re-listed; in the meantime, no harm is done by the temporary removal.
- **Estate of a deceased subject.** Treated as an authorized representative request. Proof of representation (death certificate plus next-of-kin documentation) substitutes for identity verification. Permanent.
- **Court order or law-enforcement request.** Routed to legal review at `[FACT TBD: legal/abuse contact email]`. The website complies with valid orders and informs the requesting party of the protocol-level limitation that this page documents.

## The re-listing question

If a removed subject later wishes to be re-listed — because their situation changed, because they decided otherwise, because they want a market on themselves — they can request it through the same form, with the same identity verification. Re-listing is not automatic. The removal is the default; the request to undo it is also a request.

## What this policy will not do

- It will not erase the chain.
- It will not silence other websites.
- It will not change what the four source platforms publish.
- It will not enforce against operators we have no relationship with.

The chain is the chain. The website is the website. Removal here is real, and it is also bounded — bounded by the line between the protocol and the page. We are sorry about the bound. We are also unwilling to pretend it does not exist.
