A roadmap that cannot be missed is not a roadmap. So this one has no dates. It has a sequence, and a gate, and the honest sentence about what stands between the two.

## Today

nsgame is on Solana devnet. Real bets, test SOL, no audits. The founding team operates everything — the program deploys, the [Goldilock](/trust/goldilock-oracle-network) nodes, the data-node, the website, the multisig that holds the rake. This is the minimum viable centralization, named in [Decentralization & The DAO](/trust/decentralization-and-dao). Treat the testnet as a rehearsal that happens to use real wallets.

What is true today:

- The Solana program is deployed on devnet.
- The catalog is frozen at 25 PvP fights — 15 Stars, 10 Cams.
- The Goldilock network runs as multiple nodes under one operator (the founding team).
- The data-node reads the four sources documented in [Data Source Methodology](/trust/data-source-methodology).
- Settlements post on-chain. Refunds route per [Refund Policy](/product/refund-policy).
- The rake is 0.3% of each winning payout, held in a founding-team multisig until the DAO inherits it.

What is not true today: nothing has been audited.

## The gate

Three audits stand between testnet and mainnet. They do not run in parallel by accident. Each one looks at a different surface of the same protocol.

1. **The Solana program.** External review of the on-chain code. PDA accounting, signature verification, rake distribution, refund pathing, claim safety, admin paths. The audit is for the code that holds the money.
2. **The Goldilock oracle network.** External review of the multi-signature settlement pipeline. Quorum logic, key handling, signature aggregation, the program-side verifier, the failure modes that route to refund. The audit is for the bridge between the data and the program.
3. **The data-source methodology.** External review of the read. Endpoints, parsing, normalization, gap-handling, reproducibility. Less a security audit than a methodology audit — does what the oracle reads match what a third party would read against the same sources at the same time?

Auditors are not yet engaged. When they are, the names will be published. Anyone announcing audit results before the auditors do is announcing fiction.

## At mainnet

Mainnet happens after the three audits, and after the findings are addressed. There is no calendar date. There is a sequence:

1. Audits complete.
2. Findings are remediated. The diff is public.
3. Auditors confirm the remediation, or accept the residual risk in writing.
4. The DAO is in formation; details will be published when finalised.
5. Goldilock operators are elected by the DAO; the founding team's nodes are decommissioned or absorbed into the slate.
6. The treasury multisig hands its balance to the DAO-controlled treasury.
7. The Solana program is deployed to mainnet. The catalog is re-anchored. The website points at the new program.

Each step is a precondition for the next. We will not skip one because the calendar is impatient.

## What we will not promise

A date. A licensee. A regulator's blessing. A guaranteed refund schedule. A token allocation. A mainnet that is "just around the corner" — every protocol that promised that and missed it is a footnote in someone else's docs.

What we will promise: the audits, when they happen, will be public. The remediation, when it happens, will be public. The mainnet move, when it happens, will be announced before it happens, not after. The honest sentence applies again here — the DAO is in formation; details will be published when finalised.
