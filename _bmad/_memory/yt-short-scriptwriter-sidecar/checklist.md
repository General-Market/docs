# YouTube Short Script Framework

## Pipeline Overview

```
0. AGI ARENA LINK     → 1. FACT TYPE + ANGLE    → 2. RESEARCH        → 3. PAIN RESEARCH
        ↓                        ↓                       ↓                      ↓
  Does this problem       Pick primary type       Surface-level          Deep dive for
  lead to AGI Arena       from curated list +     facts + verify         the personal
  as the next step?       blocked type check      no viral Short         pépite that HURTS
                                                  exists

→ 4. RETENTION ARCHITECTURE  → 5. PRE-SCRIPT SCORE  → 6. PLAN  → 7. SCRIPT
           ↓                          ↓                   ↓           ↓
  Open loop, story arc,         10-point gate.       Architecture   Dialogue
  watch-through hooks,          Must hit 8+          (not script)   (after plan
  dinner table test             or KILL the idea                    approved)
```

Each step must be COMPLETED before moving to the next.

---

## 0. AGI Arena Link (GATE — must pass before anything else)

- Does this subject expose a systemic problem that AGI Arena solves?
- Would a viewer who cares about this problem naturally find AGI Arena as the answer?
- Is the connection genuine (not forced)?
- **If NO → reject the subject. Find a better angle.**
- **The Short NEVER mentions AGI Arena.** The link is implicit — the content is the funnel.

### Implicit Funnel Architecture

The Short is step 1 of a 2-step funnel: **Short → Profile → AGI Arena.**

For every Short, define:
1. **The Question Planted**: After watching, the viewer should be asking ONE specific question. Write it down.
2. **The Search Path**: If they Google that question, do they find AGI Arena? If not, the angle is wrong.
3. **The Profile Check**: When they tap Max's profile, does AGI Arena's bio/pinned content answer the question the Short planted?

## 1. Fact Type + Angle Selection (NEW — before any research)

### Curated Fact Types for AGI Arena

Before researching, choose ONE primary fact type. This is the #1 decision that determines performance.

| Fact Type | Why It Works for AGI Arena | View Multiplier |
|-----------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| **Counter-Take** | "Everyone thinks X about finance, but..." — fits insider POV perfectly | 2-5x |
| **Comparison** | "$100 in X vs $100 in Y" — diagrammable, visual, chart-native | 3-5x |
| **Money Fact** | Specific $ shock — fits "I build this stuff" credibility | 2-3x |
| **Corporate Autopsy** | Brand rise-and-fall — consumer-recognizable, narrative-native | 2-10x (high variance) |
| **Novel Concept** | Hidden term nobody knows — fits "hidden mechanics" DNA | 3-5x |
| **Nobody Story** | Unknown person + extraordinary market outcome — narration + visuals | 2-3x |

### BLOCKED Fact Types (never use as primary)

| Blocked Type | Why It's Blocked | Evidence |
|--------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Hack/Tip** | #1 most-produced AND #1 lowest-performing across 25 creators | Ali Abdaal 34K, Vincent Chan 14K, Codie Sanchez 28K |
| **Personal Experience** | First-person opinion is bottom-quartile for everyone except MrBallen | Johnny Harris 144K vs 1.8M for visual, Ali Abdaal 59K |
| **Prediction without data** | Abstract forecasts without evidence avg bottom quartile | Economics Explained predictions at 40K |
| **Technical Explanation without story** | Mechanism talk without narrative = scroll-past | Vincent Chan 5K, Economics Explained 32K |
| **Pure Statistic dump** | Data overload without narrative = info-dump death | Multiple stats per short = 5-40K. One killer stat with story = 1-5M |

### View Multiplier Stack (apply as many as possible)

After choosing primary type, stack these secondary multipliers:

1. **Counter-intuitive angle** (2-5x) — Every short should flip an assumption
2. **Specific dollar amount in hook** (2-3x) — First 3 seconds, concrete $ number
3. **Named entity** (3-5x) — Brand, person, fund, platform — NEVER "a company" or "a hedge fund"
4. **Visual anchor** (2x) — Chart, diagram, on-screen data (our substitute for physical objects)
5. **Absurd/WTF detail** (2-3x) — One "you can't make this up" element
6. **Humor or wit** (2-5x) — One funny, ironic, or memorable line

## 2. Subject Research (surface level)

- Research trending topics, facts, data in the niche
- Find 1-2 chocolate-chip facts (shocking stats)
- Verify no viral Short already exists on this exact angle
- **Fact-type-aware**: research specifically for the fact type selected in Step 1
  - Counter-Take: find the common belief first, then data that contradicts it
  - Comparison: find two things people assume are similar but are wildly different
  - Money Fact: find specific $ amounts that trigger surprise
  - Corporate Autopsy: find recognizable CONSUMER brands with rise-and-fall arcs
  - Novel Concept: find terms from academic papers, economics textbooks, Fed research
  - Nobody Story: find named individuals with extraordinary outcomes

## 3. Pain Research (deep dive) — THE CRITICAL STEP

This is where the Short gets its soul. Without it: "huh, neat." With it: "holy shit, this is about ME."

### What to search for
- **Personal cost**: How does this directly cost the viewer money, time, opportunity? Find the DOLLAR AMOUNT.
- **It's happening NOW**: Not hypothetical. IS happening, right now, on autopilot.
- **Invisible extraction**: Something taken without knowledge — no notification, no line item, no consent.
- **Broken trust**: Something the viewer believed was protecting them that is actually hurting them.
- **Scale of disconnect**: Numbers showing the gap between what the viewer thinks vs what's actually happening.
- **Institutional admission**: An insider saying the quiet part out loud (the betrayal source — the institution that PROFITS is the one admitting it's broken).

### What makes a good pépite
- It's about the VIEWER — THEIR money, THEIR retirement, THEIR trade
- It triggers: "wait, this is happening to me RIGHT NOW?"
- Verifiable with a credible source (bank, regulator, academic paper — not a blog)
- Can be said in ONE sentence
- After hearing it, the viewer CANNOT unsee it

### Output of this step
A ranked list of pépites per concept, each with:
- The fact (one sentence)
- Why it hurts personally
- Source (prioritize betrayal sources — show logo/name ON SCREEN)
- Where it could go in the Short (hook? knife? proof?)

### The hook IS the pain
The most devastating pépite becomes the hook. Don't separate "hook" from "stakes."

### Every fact must be a scene
A fact is not a statistic — it's an image. If the viewer can't SEE it, rewrite it.
- BAD: "Algorithmic trading has increased significantly over the past two decades"
- GOOD: "Goldman had 600 traders in 2000. Today — two. The room is empty."
Convert every pépite into something visual before using it.

## 4. Retention Architecture (NEW — the step that prevents info-dumps)

This is where the Short becomes something people STAY for, not just click on.

### A) Open Loop (mandatory)
What question does the first line plant that ISN'T answered until the last 10 seconds?

The viewer must think "wait, but HOW?" or "wait, but WHY?" within the first 3 seconds — and NOT get the answer until the end.

- **Bad open loop**: "Here's a shocking fact about banks" (answered immediately)
- **Good open loop**: "Goldman had 600 traders. Now they have two." (WHY? — answered in the flip)

Write the open loop question explicitly. If you can't articulate what question the viewer is holding, there is no retention.

### B) Story Arc (mandatory — 3 beats)
Every short MUST follow this structure:

```
SETUP:    What the viewer believes before watching (the comfortable lie)
TENSION:  The fact/event that breaks that belief (escalation — NOT the flip yet)
PAYOFF:   The flip + the unsaid (reality cracks, viewer fills the gap)
```

**Validation**: If your plan reads like "fact → bigger fact → biggest fact" — that's an info-dump, not a story. REJECT and rebuild.

The viewer should feel a progression of emotion (recognition → unease → shock), not a progression of data (number → bigger number → biggest number).

### C) The Tease (misdirection)
What does the viewer THINK they're about to learn vs what they ACTUALLY learn?

The best shorts set up an expectation in the first 5 seconds, then deliver something the viewer didn't see coming. This is what makes them watch again.

### D) Watch-Through Hooks (at second ~15 and ~30)
At each of these points, a new doubt must open to prevent exit. Think of them as mini-cliffhangers within the short.

- Second ~15: new information that recontextualizes what came before
- Second ~30: the escalation that makes the flip inevitable but still surprising

### E) The Dinner Table Test
Can you retell this short in one sentence?

- "Did you know Goldman went from 600 traders to two?"
- "Did you know your index fund votes on your behalf without asking?"

If you can't retell it as a conversation starter, the hook is too complex. Simplify.

## 5. Pre-Script Validation Score (NEW — gate before scripting)

Score the short idea. 1 point each. **Must score 8+ to proceed to script.**

| # | Criterion |
|---|-----------|
| 1 | Verifiable with a primary source (named source, date, specific number) |
| 2 | Contradicts a common belief ("wait, really?" moment) |
| 3 | Contains a specific dollar amount or percentage |
| 4 | References a named entity (brand, person, fund, platform) |
| 5 | Has a visual you can show (chart, diagram, on-screen data, screenshot) |
| 6 | Has a human character or recognizable figure (even briefly) |
| 7 | Passable as a dinner-table conversation starter |
| 8 | Has a narrative arc (not just info delivery) |
| 9 | Contains one absurd/WTF or humorous detail |
| 10 | Self-contained (no "part 2" needed) |

### Scoring:
- **9-10**: Strong viral potential. Script it immediately.
- **8**: Solid. Proceed.
- **6-7**: Risky. Rework the angle — add a multiplier or find a stronger fact.
- **0-5**: Kill it. Find a better subject.

Output the scorecard with pass/fail per criterion and total. If below 8, explain what's weak and suggest how to fix before proceeding.

## 6. Plan Format (before scripting)

A plan is the ARCHITECTURE, not the script. No dialogue. Defines what each part DOES, not what it SAYS.

### Plan must contain:

**a) Foundation (from Pain Research)**
1. **Concept** — One-line pitch
2. **Stakes/Enjeux** — The pépite. One sentence that hurts.
3. **Chocolate-chip facts** — 1-2 facts with source, validated in Pain Research.
4. **Primary Fact Type** — Which of the 6 curated types, and why.
5. **Multiplier Stack** — Which secondary multipliers are applied.

**b) Retention Architecture (from Step 4)**
6. **Open Loop** — The question planted in second 1-3 that isn't answered until the end. Written as a literal question.
7. **Story Arc** — 3-beat structure:
   - SETUP: The comfortable lie the viewer believes
   - TENSION: The escalation that breaks it
   - PAYOFF: The flip + the unsaid
8. **The Tease** — What the viewer thinks they'll learn vs what they actually learn.
9. **Watch-Through Hooks** — What new doubt opens at ~15s and ~30s.
10. **Dinner Table Sentence** — The one sentence someone would use to retell this.

**c) Narrative Architecture**
11. **Hook** — The most painful pépite IS the hook. Type (number / question / intriguing fact) follows naturally.
12. **ABT map** — One line each for And, But, Therefore
13. **Emotional arc** — The journey in one line (e.g. False safety → Violation → Disgust → Relief → Curiosity)
14. **The FLIP sentence** — The single line where the viewer's assumption breaks. This line gets the most production emphasis (sfx, visual change, pause before it). Not a gradual turn — a violent inversion.
15. **The Unsaid** — Identify the most devastating implication and do NOT say it. Let the viewer arrive there alone. The script must leave a wound open.
16. **The Loop Point** — The last line must make the first line mean something different on second viewing.

**d) Structure**
17. **Parts and sub-parts** — Table format:

| Sub-part | Does what (NOT dialogue) | Leaves what doubt |
|----------|--------------------------|-------------------|

18. **Transitions** — How each part launches into the next (the bridge idea, not a written line)

**e) Implicit Funnel**
19. **The Question Planted** — What the viewer asks themselves after watching.
20. **The Search Path** — Does Googling that question lead to AGI Arena?

**f) Direction notes**
21. **Visual direction** — Key visual concept per part
22. **Audio direction** — Music/SFX mood per part

## 7. Script (after plan approved)

### Voice / Persona
- **Insider STORYTELLER, not insider commentator.**
- The voice is: "Let me show you what I found" — not "Let me tell you what I think."
- Define WHO is talking before writing a single line. A friend who just connected two dots at 2am? A builder who walked the empty trading floor?
- Write 1 example line the persona WOULD say and 1 they NEVER would
- WOULD: "Goldman had 600 traders in 2000. I walked that floor last year. Two desks."
- NEVER: "Let me tell you why algorithmic trading is a problem for retail investors."
- The difference: **showing a scene you witnessed** vs **explaining a concept you understand**

### Structure Rules
- 3 Parts, with sub-parts that launch into each other
- Every sentence leaves a doubt → next sentence answers it BUT opens a new one → linked until the end
- Small sentences — extreme brevity
- Facts = chocolate chips in a cookie. 1-2 sentences max of pure facts, embedded in the story
- **The open loop from Step 4 must be visible in the structure**: first line plants the question, last section answers it

### Rhythm Rules
- Never write 3 sentences of similar length in a row. Vary deliberately: Long. Short. Short. Long — — — short. Punch.
- Rhythm IS emotion. Compression = tension. Expansion = relief. Use sentence length as a musical tool.
- Read it out loud. If it sounds metronomic, rewrite it.

### Tone Rules
- NOT a text — a SPEECH. Talking to the viewer.
- Mark intent/tone per line: interrogative? assurance? whisper? excitement?
- Include human quirks (pauses, self-corrections, asides)
- Alternate emotions: joy, hate, surprise, fear, excitement
- ABT: the "Therefore" is shown on screen as proof or chibi emotion

### Production Notes
- **Character**: Chibi with different emotions (not face-to-camera)
- **Format**: YouTube Short (60s to 3min)
- Prepare image, music, sfx (sfx always on something visual), and visual plan alongside script

### Error Avoidance
- NO word repetitions / redundancy / complex words — go to essential
- Play on viewer emotions — take them by surprise vs other YouTube content
- NOT a generic tip disguised as a story
- NOT an info-dump disguised as dramatic delivery
- The story arc must be FELT, not just structurally present
