# perps-en — niche map

## TL;DR (5 bullets max: biggest niche, best underserved niche, dominant format, dominant villain, one-line opportunity)

- Biggest niche: Hyperliquid / $HYPE identity content; the harvest has the densest repeated signal around Hyperliquid, $HYPE, ETF exposure, protocol revenue, and ecosystem expansion, with high-view posts from @HyperliquidX, @Grayscale, @CryptoHayes, @chameleon_jeff, @WSJ, and @brian_armstrong (https://x.com/HyperliquidX/status/2053970679846834202, https://x.com/Grayscale/status/2062207173208473772, https://x.com/CryptoHayes/status/2062411523243536428, https://x.com/chameleon_jeff/status/2055342031514599592, https://x.com/WSJ/status/2061833108488433768, https://x.com/brian_armstrong/status/2054895878318563693).
- Best underserved niche: risk-aware perp market structure; liquidation, open interest, and RWA shock posts perform, but the harvest shows them split across news, fear, and protocol updates instead of one calm explainer account (https://x.com/LLuciano_BTC/status/2062438363928338833, https://x.com/Jeremybtc/status/2062559821161718194, https://x.com/coinbureau/status/2060195517259481366, https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2057100263526138163).
- Dominant format: the dominant named format is `data_drop`, meaning a short post that leads with a number, market stat, revenue figure, open-interest figure, or funding fact; it shows up in Saylor’s macro framing and Hyperliquid’s $3B RWA open-interest post (https://x.com/saylor/status/2062500552705986704, https://x.com/HyperliquidX/status/2061673725502861406).
- Dominant villain: hidden leverage risk; the audience keeps returning to liquidation cascades, whales revenge-trading, visible positions, and public wallet exposure (https://x.com/AshCrypto/status/2062816814191501692, https://x.com/Jeremybtc/status/2062559821161718194, https://x.com/Americanfort_io/status/2059674955709907131).
- One-line opportunity: build the account that explains perps as market structure, not casino theatre, using numbers, plain risk language, and platform comparisons that a Hyperliquid-native trader trusts (https://x.com/Grayscale/status/2062207173208473772, https://x.com/KaikoHQ/status/2060726707984715965, https://x.com/Lighter_xyz/status/2056794887702339717).

## Audience psychology (max-marketing frame)

- Awareness level of the median audience member: solution-aware. They already know perps, Hyperliquid, $HYPE, liquidation, points, CEX/DEX, and RWA, and they engage with posts comparing products or explaining which venue wins rather than posts defining crypto from first principles (https://x.com/Grayscale/status/2062207173208473772, https://x.com/vikktorrrre/status/2056351753453420670, https://x.com/LLuciano_BTC/status/2062438363928338833).
- Market sophistication stage: Stage 4. Stage 4 means the audience has heard the broad claim before, so winning posts add mechanism, proof, or a sharper antagonist: Hyperliquid is not only “a perp DEX,” Lighter is not only “fast,” and RWA perps are not only “access” (https://x.com/PerpetualCow/status/2061220358686904348, https://x.com/Lighter_xyz/status/2056794887702339717, https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2057100263526138163).
- The median user wants asymmetry, but also fears being the exit liquidity; this tension shows up in $HYPE upside, airdrop math, whale sales, and liquidation warnings (https://x.com/Polymarket/status/2061181910374633550, https://x.com/Crypto_Pranjal/status/2052750997257339280, https://x.com/lookonchain/status/2062479917875646539, https://x.com/AshCrypto/status/2062816814191501692).
