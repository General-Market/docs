# Phase 3 — Angle d'attaque (the promise)

The angle B chosen in phase 1 — *Your Backtest Was Right. The Venue Lied.* — names the territory. Phase 3 names the promise. The promise is what the video makes to a stranger who has not yet clicked, and what the next twenty hours of work has to honour.

## Every plausible promise

Six candidates. Each is a real video; the differences are in what they make the viewer expect.

| # | Promise | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | *"You didn't lose because you were bad. You lost because the venue is rigged."* | Restorative. Blame-shifting. |
| P2 | *"Six numbers, peer-reviewed, that explain why your strategy died in production."* | Quantitative. Receipt-driven. |
| P3 | *"Your backtest measured the trade. It did not measure the venue. The venue is what killed you."* | Diagnostic. Two-clause reveal. |
| P4 | *"Walk-forward, slippage, regime shift — three wrong answers to one right question."* | Pedagogical. Names the folk explanations to demolish. |
| P5 | *"There's a market you backtested, and a market you traded into. They are not the same market."* | Philosophical. Most useful for the non-trader audience. |
| P6 | *"Eleven venues. Seventeen receipts. One reason your bot died."* | Investigative. Enumerative. |

## Scoring

| # | Curiosity force (indifferent viewer) | Honesty (can the script deliver?) | Specificity (vs saturated YouTube) |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Moderate — *"rigged"* is overused. The viewer has heard it. | Yes, with 17 receipts. | Low. *"Markets are rigged"* is the dominant frame on crypto YouTube. The video would be lost in a crowd. |
| P2 | Strong for traders. Weak for the indifferent viewer — *six numbers* is a list, not a question. | Yes — the bps math is on disk. | High. Number-led framing is not the dominant pattern in this space. |
| P3 | Strong. *"It did not measure the venue"* is the sentence that makes a non-trader pause. | Yes — the script can deliver this in every receipt beat. | High. The diagnosis is the differentiator. Nobody else is naming the venue as a hidden parameter. |
| P4 | Strong for traders, but it requires the viewer to know what walk-forward and overfit *are* before the title can land. | Yes, with care. The script has to demolish three folk explanations cleanly. | Moderate. The folk-explanation-takedown is fresher than P1, less novel than P3. |
| P5 | Strongest for the *non-trader*. *"They are not the same market"* opens the door for anyone. | Yes — the dead-market thesis on the page is exactly this. | High. Philosophical framing is rare in this niche; the non-trader audience is mostly untapped. |
| P6 | Moderate. Numbers are clean, but the *eleven / seventeen / one* structure reads as enumeration, which we already filtered out at phase 1. | Yes. | Moderate. List-shape videos saturate. |

## The pick

**Promise P3 — *"Your backtest measured the trade. It did not measure the venue. The venue is what killed you."***

It is the most honest sentence the video can keep. It diagnoses rather than complains. It does not reach for *"rigged"* (saturated) or *"corrupt"* (legally fragile) — it reaches for *did not measure*, which is precise enough to defend in court and visceral enough to land on a stranger.

The phrase that does the work is *the venue was a hidden parameter*. Every operator who has lost will recognise the shape of the claim even before they have the language for it. Every non-trader will recognise the *form* — *you measured the wrong thing*. That form is portable: it is the shape of half the good investigative journalism in the world.

P5 (*"two markets, not the same market"*) is the one I would put in reserve as a *secondary line* — the one the script uses as a chapter transition, the one the thumbnail can borrow if P3's wording falls flat. It is too philosophical to lead with, but too good to throw away.

## The video's one-sentence promise

> The gap between a profitable backtest and a losing live trade has a name, and it is not the one you've been told. It is the venue itself, and you can see it in the receipts.

## Beta-title

> **Your Backtest Was Right. The Venue Lied.**

Phase 4 will test this against 8–10 candidates. For now it carries the promise as written. The pair *"right / lied"* is the structural payoff — it tells the viewer *you didn't do anything wrong, and someone else did*. That is the restorative move the angle requires.

## What is now in scope

- The universal experience of the backtest-to-live collapse (the cold open)
- Six mechanisms by which a venue takes from retail before the trade resolves: PFOF, b-book, VIP fee-tier, order-flow visibility, MEV/sandwich, listing front-running. (One was cut from the seven; phase 5 will confirm which — currently colocation is the most expendable, since the bps cost is the smallest.)
- The receipts that anchor each — Robinhood, Wahi, Sigma Chain, Hyperliquid liquidation map, Solana sandwich data, Polymarket / Kalshi b-book mirror
- The anti-cheat fix as the closing answer, *not* the centerpiece

## What is now out of scope

- The dead-financial-market frame as a *standalone* theory. It will live as a *bridge* — one or two sentences inside the script, not the spine. Angle A would have made it the spine; we did not pick angle A.
- The full enumeration of all eleven venues. The video names some venues; it does not tour them. Angle C would have toured them; we did not pick angle C.
- Custodial hacks, AML/KYC failures, exchange outages. The page editorial rule already excludes them; the video inherits the rule.
- Product walkthroughs of Vision. The video closes on anti-cheat as a *property*, not a *product tour*. A separate video can do the product tour.
- The full thirteen mechanisms. Six is the cleaner number. The other seven exist; they don't make the cut.

## The price of this angle

Every angle has a retention tax. P3's tax is that it requires the viewer to *care about the diagnosis* — to want to know *why* their strategy died, not just *that* it died. A viewer who clicks for catharsis (*"yes, the system is rigged, validate me"*) will leave at minute three when the video starts naming basis points and citation indices.

We accept this. The replacement audience — the operator who wants the diagnosis, the non-trader who wants the *form* of the diagnosis — is the audience the page was already built for. The targeting list is twenty-two of them.

The angle drifts further from pure catharsis than P1 would have, and closer to instruction. That is the cost.

## Checkpoint

Is this the promise we keep for the next twenty hours of work?

If yes, phase 4 — *Titre et miniature* — opens the title-and-thumbnail design. The title is decided *before* the script is written; the script then has to honour it.
