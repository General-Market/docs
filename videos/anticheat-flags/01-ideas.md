# Phase 1 — Idée (the wall of fire)

The seed is large. A page that names eleven venues and thirteen mechanisms is, in video terms, three or four distinct videos sitting on top of each other. Phase 1 cuts one of them out cleanly.

## The domain tree

```

│
├── 1. The symptom — your strategy died in production
│      ├── The backtest → live gap (the universal operator experience)
│      ├── The 22 handles already on the targeting list
│      ├── Walk-forward, overfit, slippage as folk explanations
│      └── The wrong question: "what did I do wrong?"
│              └── The right question: "what did the venue do?"
│
├── 2. The mechanism — how the venue takes its cut
│      ├── PFOF — every retail trade sold to a wholesaler
│      ├── Internal book (b-book) — the broker as counterparty

│      ├── MEV / sandwiching — Solana, Ethereum, every public mempool
```

Three of these branches can carry a worked video on their own. The other two are too narrow (3 — list-shape only) or too dependent (5 — has to come after the diagnosis).

## Three candidate angles

Each angle is scored on the three lines the skill demands.

---

### Candidate A — *The Dead Financial Market*

The video frames itself the way the page does: as a sibling to the dead internet theory. Open with the parallel — most internet content is bots, most market activity is predators. State the thesis quickly. Then take three of the strongest receipts from the page — one from a household-name venue, one from a quant-favourite venue, one from a memecoin venue — and show the same mechanism playing out three different ways. Close on what a living market would look like.

- **Why it interests me:** the frame is culturally legible. "Dead internet theory" has tens of millions of touches across YouTube and Twitter. Mapping that frame to markets lets the video reach beyond crypto/trading audiences into a broader tech-aware crowd. It also gives the script its red thread for free — *what does "dead" mean here, and who is alive?*
- **Why anyone else would care:** anyone who has ever felt the suspicion that "the market is rigged" has now been given a name for that suspicion. The dead-internet framing turns a paranoid feeling into a thesis with peer-reviewed footnotes.
- **Risk it doesn't land:** this is a thinkpiece angle. If the editor does not give it urgency, it reads as a meditation. The thumbnail and title have to *promise a payoff*, not just a frame. The receipts have to be visceral, not statistical.

**Length estimate:** 22–28 min. **Tone:** essayistic, slow, building.

---

### Candidate B — *Your Backtest Was Right. The Venue Lied.*

A first-person investigative video aimed at the operator who has lived this. Open with the universal experience — *you backtested it. It made money. You went live. It died. You blamed yourself.* Then take the strongest mechanisms one at a time, each tied to a specific receipt, each ending on the line *that is why yours died.* Walk the viewer through six or seven of the thirteen — not all of them, just the ones that touch the largest share of retail. Close on anti-cheat as the answer to the question the viewer didn't know to ask.

- **Why it interests me:** the targeting work has already mapped this audience — twenty-two PRIME and STRONG handles. The video is a lead-magnet for those people and for the larger cohort behind them. It has a natural retention hook: the viewer is *waiting* to see which mechanism killed their bot. They will not click away until they hear the one that matches.
- **Why anyone else would care:** 100% of algorithmic traders have lived this story. The wider audience — anyone who has wondered why retail loses in markets — gets the same answer. The framing converts a personal failure into a structural one, which is the move that earns shares.
- **Risk it doesn't land:** the framing is too tactical for non-traders. The "I" in the framing has to be *performed* convincingly — the script must sound like a real operator talking, not a marketing voice. If the receipts feel like a feature list instead of confessions, the video collapses.

**Length estimate:** 18–25 min. **Tone:** confessional opening, investigative body, restorative close.

---

### Candidate C — *Eleven Casinos Pretending To Be Markets*

A documentary-style enumeration. Open with the three most viscerally damning incidents on the page — pick from Binance's Sigma Chain yacht, the Coinbase insider listing front-run, the Hyperliquid JELLY incident — then take a tour through all eleven venues, structured as a hit list. Each venue gets ninety seconds. The viewer is escorted past them like exhibits in a museum of theft. End at the door at the back of the building: the venue that has no exhibits because it has nothing to put inside one.

- **Why it interests me:** this angle maximises the page's own asset — the receipts. The video becomes the page in motion. It has the strongest visual surface of the three: incident cards, ribbon stats, the bleed chart, the venue pills. The editing is largely *built* — the page already designed the shots.
- **Why anyone else would care:** list-shaped videos are reliable. Viewers know what they are getting and can stop when they're satisfied. The enumeration is *comforting* in the same way a thread of receipts is. Each venue named is a chance to capture the part of the audience who trades on it.
- **Risk it doesn't land:** enumerative videos are saturated. Retention drops sharply when a viewer's specific venue is not in the first three. Eleven beats of the same shape risk monotone — every venue intro reads the same, every ribbon reads the same, every incident card reads the same. The editor has to fight the structure on every cut.

**Length estimate:** 28–40 min (the long end of the *worked video* band). **Tone:** investigative, escorting, fact-heavy.

---

## Recommendation

If forced to pick alone, I lean toward **B**. Three reasons:

1. The first-person frame gives the script a *character* — the operator who lost. Without a character, the receipts read like a list. Candidates A and C both lean on tone to substitute for character, which is harder.
2. The audience for B is already mapped. The 22 PRIME handles on disk are not a sample of the target audience; they *are* the target audience. The video lands on a prepared field.
3. B has a clear answer to *"why am I watching this and not closing the tab?"* — *"I am watching this because I am about to find out which mechanism killed my bot."* That is a retention promise. A's promise is intellectual; C's is enumerative. B's is personal.

But the call is yours. The next ten phases obey whichever angle you pick now.
