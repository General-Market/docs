# Angle G — Cohort Decay

The retail cohort of 2020-2024 underperformed at almost twice the rate of the cohort of 2015-2019. Same instruments, same indices, same English-speaking world. Different outcome. The gap doubled.

## The sharpest finding

Derek Horstmeyer (George Mason University, Costello College of Business) ran the dollar-weighted-vs-time-weighted timing gap on the universe of US-dollar-denominated mutual funds, split across two five-year windows:

| Window | Timing gap, all equity | Timing gap, small-cap |
|---|---|---|
| 2015 – 2019 (pre-Covid) | **0.53%/yr** | 0.62%/yr |
| 2020 – Oct 2024 (post-Covid) | **1.01%/yr** | 1.38%/yr |

Same fund universe. The post-Covid cohort burned through twice the alpha of the pre-Covid cohort by timing the same vehicles worse. Small-cap funds — where retail flows are loudest — more than doubled.[¹]

This is the cohort-decay smoking gun: the *same* asset class, *same* benchmark, two adjacent cohorts, one of them visibly worse.

## DALBAR — fifteen consecutive years of underperformance

DALBAR's 2025 QAIB report (covering 2024) puts the average equity investor at **16.54%** against the S&P 500's **25.05%** — an 848 bp gap, the second-largest of the past decade and the fourth-largest since DALBAR began tracking in 1985. The average equity investor has now underperformed the index for **fifteen consecutive years**. The last year they beat it was 2009.[²][³]

Withdrawals from equity funds happened in every quarter of 2024, with the largest outflows immediately before the largest rally. Fixed-income investors *added* money during a year of negative bond returns.

## Morningstar — the gap is structural, not noise

Morningstar's *Mind the Gap 2024* and *2025*: across the 10 years to 31 Dec 2024, the average dollar in US mutual funds and ETFs earned 7.0%/yr vs aggregate fund returns of 8.2%/yr — a 1.2% timing gap, persistent across every rolling 10-year window from 2020 through 2024. Investors forfeit ~15% of fund returns to their own timing.[⁴]

Sector equity funds: -1.5% gap. Allocation funds: -0.1%. The more retail-coded the vehicle, the wider the gap. The widest single-year gap in the series occurred in 2020.[⁵]

## Robinhood — the 2021 vintage is a graveyard

Robinhood's MAU went **21M → 10.8M** between Q1 2021 and Q3 2023, a 49% attrition.[⁶][⁷] The S-1 (filed July 2021) disclosed that the 2017 and 2018 cohorts roughly tripled their per-user revenue over 24 months. The 2020 cohort came in with cumulative deposits 3.3× higher at 12 months than earlier cohorts.[⁸] Then half of them left.

What's left in the *active* MAU pool by 2024 is the survivor subset. The reported cohort metrics are right-censored: the worst-performing accounts churned out and stopped contributing data.

## Bryzgalova, Pavlova, Sikorskaya — retail options are a wealth transfer

Tracking wholesaler-routed retail options orders Nov 2019 – Jun 2021: aggregate retail portfolio lost **$2.1B** at a 10-day holding horizon, with **$6.4B** more burned on bid-ask spreads. Average return: -5% to -9%, rising to -10% to -14% around high-vol earnings announcements. Published Journal of Finance, 2023.[⁹][¹⁰]

The 0DTE share of S&P 500 options went from ~5% (2016) → ~17% (2020) → ~47% (2024).[¹¹] Retail share of US options volume crossed 50% in 2025, up from 34-38% in late 2019.[¹²] The cohort of 2024 trades the worst product mix retail has ever traded.

## BaFin — Germany counted everyone

BaFin's product intervention study on turbo certificates (Jan 2019 – Dec 2023): **74.2% of German retail traders lost money**, averaging €6,358 per loser, **€3.4B** in total retail losses. Investors with >1,000 trades lost in **91%** of cases. Retail turbo participation grew ~110% from 2019 to 2023.[¹³]

This is not a sample. It is the entire German turbo population over five years.

## Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti — Brazil, 2013-2015 cohort

Of all individuals who began day-trading Brazilian equity futures 2013-2015 and persisted >300 days: **97% lost money**. 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage. 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary. Regression on the 300-day persisters: no evidence of learning over time.[¹⁴]

The Taiwan equivalent (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, 2014, JFM): <1% of day-traders earn statistically reliable abnormal returns net of fees, 1992-2006.[¹⁵] Chague replicated in Brazil 2013-2015. Outcome distribution stable across two decades and two continents.

## JPMorgan Chase Institute — risk goes up, returns unmeasured

500,000 JPMC investor accounts split into pre-pandemic (Jan 2019 – Feb 2020), pandemic (Mar 2020 – Jun 2021), and late-pandemic (Jul 2021 – Sep 2023) cohorts. The pandemic cohort ran portfolio beta **12.6%** higher than the pre-pandemic cohort and **11.7%** higher than the late-pandemic cohort. Two-thirds of the increase came from *active* allocation changes. Portfolio market risk in 2023 was still ~10pp above 2019.[¹⁶]

JPMC did not publish realized P&L. They published the risk numerator without the return denominator. The omission is itself a finding.

## Bitcoin — cycle multiples halve each cohort

Glassnode's Long-Term Holder MVRV peaked at **36.2×** during the 2017 bull run and **12.58×** during the 2021 cycle — a 2.9× compression in realized profit multiples per vintage. Each cohort buys higher, sells flatter.[¹⁷] Realized Cap HODL Waves show the 2021 vintage's realized price climbing through 2022-2024 only via dollar-cost-averaging the underwater position downward.[¹⁸]

Retail share of crypto exchange volume per Kaiko: **40% in 2021 → 18% in 2024**. The retail take-rate hit its lowest level since the Terra Luna collapse.[¹⁹]

## What this stack means

Skeptics say retail always lost. The data says retail lost *more* recently, in two independent ways:

1. **Same instrument, worse timing**: the Horstmeyer split (0.53% → 1.01%) shows the same fund universe being timed nearly twice as badly. The Morningstar gap has stayed structurally elevated across every rolling decade window since 2020.
2. **Worse instrument mix**: the new cohort routes into 0DTE options, turbos, perps, and meme equities — the worst-EV products ever offered to retail. BaFin's 91% loss rate for >1,000 trades and Bryzgalova's $2.1B options loss are the products this cohort actually trades.

The 2020-2024 cohort is the first to combine the worst timing in DALBAR's history with the highest exposure to negative-EV derivatives ever made available. Two compounding decays.

## Gaps in the public record

- **Calvet/Campbell/Sodini** Swedish population data ends in 2007 in published work. No post-2020 cohort update is publicly indexed.
- **Linnainmaa** Finnish work (Lee/Liu/Odean class) covers 1995-2006. No post-2020 update located.
- **DALBAR's full 1985-2024 year-by-year gap table** is paywalled behind qaib.com.
- **Vanguard "How America Saves 2024"** publishes deferral and TDF behavior but masks the realized-return distribution for self-directed brokerage accounts. The gap data sits inside Vanguard but is not externally disclosed.
- **Robinhood post-S-1 cohort metrics** stopped being reported in this granularity after the IPO.

The dream chart — "median PnL of 5-year retail cohort, by start year, going negative" — does not exist as a single published artifact. It has been assembled here from five datasets that all point the same direction.
