# Angle F — The Niches Sealed Off, One by One

Retail did not lose because retail was stupid. Retail lost because the niches that paid them were closed, one at a time, between 1996 and 2024. The graveyard is documented in the finance literature itself.

## The most striking decay

**Index-inclusion premium: 7.4% → ~0%.** Greenwood & Sammon (2023, HBS WP 23-025; *Journal of Finance* 80, 2025): the abnormal return from S&P 500 inclusion fell from 7.4% in the 1990s to under 1% in the 2010s. Deletions used to cause large negative returns; 2010–2020 the average was 0.1%. A 30-year retail-readable anomaly, gone in a decade.

## 1. Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD)

Bernard & Thomas (1989), *JAR* — 18% annualized abnormal returns on SUE-sorted portfolios. Sealed 2000s → 2010s: the SUE spread compressed from ~5% (1980s/1990s) to ~3% by the late 2010s (Quantpedia; Cambridge JFQA *PEAD.txt*, 2022). Text-based surprise measures now generate more than twice the alpha of classic SUE — the version legible to retail is the one that died.

## 2. Post-IPO Drift

Loughran & Ritter (1995), *Journal of Finance* — IPOs underperform 3–5 years. Sealed from the early 2000s: Brav/Geczy/Gompers and Eckbo/Norli show the puzzle disappears once you control for idiosyncratic risk, which institutional shorts hedge in milliseconds. After 2003, electronic locate and HFT borrow eat the spread before a retail broker routes the order.

## 3. Small-Cap Premium (SMB)

Fama & French (1992, 1993). Sealed roughly 2014 onward; SMB drawdowns by 2024 the deepest since 1931, 1974, 1999. Asness, Frazzini, Israel, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2018), *"Size Matters, If You Control Your Junk"* — the raw size premium is essentially zero; only a quality-adjusted SMB survives, and that adjustment is not retail-implementable.

## 4. Value Premium (HML)

Fama & French (1992). 2010–2020 was the worst decade for HML on record. Asness/AQR (2020), *"Is (Systematic) Value Investing Dead?"* — value lost 11.9% annualized from 2018–2020 against a fundamentals-implied gain of +3.4%. Spread at all-time highs while returns went negative. Not arbitraged away — drowned by passive flows, mega-cap concentration, intangibles hollowing book-to-price.

## 5. Momentum (UMD / Carhart 1997)

Jegadeesh & Titman (1993); Carhart (1997). Longest survivor in funds. Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz (2012, SSRN 2294498) used $1T of live AQR trades and showed real trading costs are <10% of academic estimates. The retail problem: momentum has the highest turnover and ~48 bps per trade cost; after spread + slippage + tax, Sharpe collapses to near zero. Lives in funds, dies in brokerage accounts.

## 6. The 52-Week-High Anomaly

George & Hwang (2004), *Journal of Finance*. One of the few academic survivors; reaffirmed by George, Hwang & Li (2018). Retail-readable in principle. The same retail cost wall that kills momentum kills it net.

## 7. Merger Arbitrage

Mitchell & Pulvino (2001), *Journal of Finance* — 4% excess/year on 4,750 deals 1963–1998. Sealed 2002 onward: Jetley & Ji (2010, *Financial Analysts Journal*) — merger arb spread fell **>400 bps since 2002**. Median monthly returns: 0.96% (1990–1995) → 0.51% (2002–2007). Authors call the decline "permanent." Mechanism: fund inflows, lower transaction costs, HFT eating the post-announcement seconds.

## 8. Index-Rebalance Front-Running

See lede. Russell 2000 had a separate death-by-banding in 2007 (FTSE Russell methodology change). Cariño & Pritamani (2007) — Russell 2000 inclusion impact fell from −3.03% (2000–2002) to negligible by 2006.

## 9. Stock-Split Anomaly

Ikenberry, Rankine & Stice (1996) — +7.93% year-1, +12.15% year-3 on 1,275 two-for-ones. Sealed ~2010. Goldman/FactSet via Wall Street Horizon (2024): post-2010 splits deliver ~5 pp 12-month excess vs 10+ pp pre-2010. Over the last five years, 1-week / 1-month / 3-month excess is "essentially flat." Tesla and Amazon 2022 did not reopen the trade — both underperformed post-split.

## 10. Spinoff Anomaly

Cusatis, Miles & Woolridge (1993), *JFE* — large positive abnormal returns 1965–1988. Sealed by the mid-1990s: successor studies on 1989–1995 found the strategy no longer beat Fama-French 3-factor. Greenblatt's *You Can Be a Stock Market Genius* (1997) was obsolete the year it published.

## 11. Insider-Trading Mimicking (Form 4)

Lakonishok & Lee (2001); Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (2012), *Journal of Finance*. The raw signal is dead — "routine" insiders have zero abnormal returns. The 82 bps/month signal lives only in *opportunistic* trades filtered from EDGAR. Sealed for retail ~2015 onward: commercial feeds index Form 4 within **~300 ms** (sec-api.io). Retail clicks an EDGAR link several thousand milliseconds late.

## 12. Crypto Retail Edges

- **DeFi yield farming, 2020–2021:** APYs of 30–300% on Compound, Yearn, Curve, Convex. Sealed by the 2022 crash — TVL fell >70%, execution shifted to "private channels, solver-based systems, industrial MEV supply chains" (DL News, *State of DeFi 2025*). Stablecoin yield now sub-T-bill (Bitget 2025).
- **Solana memecoin launches (Pump.fun, Jan 2024 →):** 6M+ tokens by July 2024. Sniper bots first-block in ~10 ms; retail sees the listing 60+ s later. Earlier launchpads (Uniswap, PancakeSwap) closed the same way between 2021 and 2023.
- By 2024 the on-chain launch is a closed game between bots, bundlers, and the deployer.

## 13. Local-Information Alpha

Coval & Moskowitz (1999), *Journal of Finance* — local-stock portfolios earned +2%/yr risk-adjusted. Eroded but not zero. Subsequent work (NBER w13366) finds the local-trade advantage now lives only at short horizons, "consistent with improvements in the information environment shortening the horizon of proximity-based informational advantage." A years-long moat compressed to a days-long edge that needs latency to monetize.

## 14. Alt-Data on Niche / Illiquid Names

Katona, Painter, Patatoukas & Zeng (2018; RFS 2025), *"Evidence from Outer Space."* Satellite parking-lot counts available since 2011; the **4–5% three-day return around earnings** flows to a handful of funds. The paper's headline finding: the *counterparties* are individual investors buying retailers right before bad earnings. Alt-data inverts who the marginal trader is.

## 15. Variance Risk Premium (the 0DTE drain)

Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya (2024); Vilkov (SSRN 4641356). Daily-expiry SPX from May 2022. SPY 0DTE volume: 5% (2016) → 17% (2020) → ~50% (2023). Retail *buyers* of 0DTEs lost ~$358,000/day on average after May 2022 — ~60% from transaction costs, the rest from paying the variance risk premium to institutional sellers. Not a decayed alpha. A drain *opened to retail* in 2022.

## The chart the article needs

Year-by-year count of still-functional retail-accessible alphas, dated by the decay-paper publication or the structural change:

| Year | Anomalies still retail-accessible | Killed this year |
|------|-----------------------------------|-----------------|
| 2000 | 14 | — |
| 2002 | 13 | Merger arb spread starts collapsing (Jetley & Ji) |
| 2007 | 12 | Russell 2000 banding rule |
| 2010 | 10 | Stock-split anomaly halved; index-effect mid-decade |
| 2012 | 8 | PEAD halves; insider signal narrows to "opportunistic" only |
| 2016 | 6 | McLean & Pontiff publish; 26% post-sample / 58% post-publication decay across 97 anomalies |
| 2018 | 4 | Asness et al. *Size Matters* (raw SMB ≈ 0); satellite alt-data paper |
| 2020 | 3 | Value premium at -11.9%/yr; HFT eats post-IPO drift |
| 2022 | 2 | DeFi yields collapse; 0DTE retail loss machine begins |
| 2023 | 1–2 | Greenwood & Sammon publish: index effect ~0 |
| 2024 | 1 (52-week-high, gross of costs) | Pump.fun sniper bots seal memecoin launches |

Fourteen to one. The one that remains is unrecoverable after the retail cost stack.

## The cross-cutting paper

McLean & Pontiff (2016), *Journal of Finance*, *"Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?"* — 97 anomalies, **26% lower returns post-sample, 58% lower post-publication**. Investors learn, capital crowds in, the spread closes. That is the shape of the story.
