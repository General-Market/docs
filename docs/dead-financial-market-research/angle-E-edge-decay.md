# Angle E — The Edge Decayed

The skeptic's reply to "retail lost in 2024" is "retail always lost." It's true. And it's not the argument. The argument is that the *ceiling* came down. The right tail of retail performance — the small subset of traders who used to extract real money — has been arbitraged, sped, and starved into a thinner and thinner shape.

Below: the studies that show it.

## The sharpest single finding — post-publication anomaly decay

R. David McLean and Jeffrey Pontiff, *Journal of Finance* (2016): tested 97 published return-predicting variables. **Returns drop 26 % out-of-sample and 58 % post-publication.** The 32-point gap between the two is the part attributable to investors reading the paper and trading it away.

Every retail-accessible edge — value, momentum, low-vol, PEAD, post-IPO drift, accruals — has the same shape: discovered, published, harvested, dead. The decay is mechanical. It doesn't care about you.

Source: McLean & Pontiff, ["Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)

## PEAD — the cleanest decay curve

Post-earnings-announcement drift was the most reliable retail anomaly of the late 20th century. Easy to trade. Long-only. No leverage required.

- High-SUE / low-SUE spread fell from ~5 % in the 1980s–90s to ~3 % by the late 2010s.
- For non-microcaps, **PEAD essentially disappeared after 2001.**
- For large-caps, **PEAD was zero by 2006.**
- Cause: decimalization (2001) + Reg NMS (2005) + HFT compressing the announcement-day adjustment to seconds.

Source: [UCLA Anderson Review summary](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/); [Wikipedia bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93earnings-announcement_drift).

## Momentum — the second cleanest

- 1990s: momentum factor returned ~10 % annually.
- Today: ~2 %.
- Long-short paper portfolio earned 5.7 % annualized; actual investor capture: **0.4 % per year.** A 5.2-point slippage to implementation.
- 2007–2015 and 2010–2015 sub-periods: alpha for the "winner" portfolio was *negative*.

Source: Research Affiliates, ["The Incredible Shrinking Factor Return"](https://www.researchaffiliates.com/publications/articles/604-the-incredible-shrinking-factor-return-unabridged); [MSCI on momentum crashes](https://www.msci.com/www/blog-posts/is-momentum-crashing-/0333608167).

## SPIVA persistence — the right tail of active managers evaporates

This isn't retail directly. It's a proxy: managers who *are paid full-time to do nothing else* can't hold the top quartile.

- Of 2020's top-quartile US large-cap active funds, **zero remained in the top quartile by year-end 2024.**
- Only **5 %** of above-median large-cap active funds in 2020 stayed above median for the next two consecutive years.
- Five-year top-half persistence: **4.2 % in the US, 6.1 % in Europe.**

If professionals with Bloomberg terminals can't persist in the top quartile, the retail right tail is structurally thinner.

Source: [S&P Dow Jones US Persistence Scorecard](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/us-persistence-scorecard/); [Evidence Investor commentary](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/spiva-persistence-scorecard).

## Taiwan day traders — the canonical right-tail study

Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean's *Cross-Section of Speculator Skill* (Taiwan, 1992–2006) established the ceiling shape that all subsequent studies replicate:

- **Less than 1 % of day traders** earn predictable profits net of costs.
- Survival rates: 44 % at 1 year, 24 % at 2 years, **15 % at 3 years.**
- The 1 % who survive *do* persist. Skill exists. It is just very rare and the rest is a slow grinder of attrition.

Source: [Barber & Odean, Berkeley page (Day Trading Skill PDF)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf).

## Brazil — the same shape, modern era

Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, *Day Trading for a Living?* (2013–2015, equity futures):

- **97 % lost money** among those who persisted ≥ 300 days.
- **0.4 % earned more than a Brazilian bank teller** (~US$54/day).
- The single best trader earned $310/day at $2,560 standard deviation. The top of the right tail was a coin flip.

Source: Chague et al., [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/).

## Hyperliquid — the same shape, 2024 vintage

ENVY Protocol analyzed 10,000 Hyperliquid wallets, 30-day window, November 2024:

- 73.8 % losing, 9.7 % flat, **16.5 % profitable.**
- **27.2 % lost more than 85 % of capital in 30 days.**
- 40.6 % of wallets stopped trading entirely within 30 days; 66.4 % of the quitters were unprofitable when they left.
- Median trader: −$67.

The number to flag: 16.5 % profitable on Hyperliquid in 2024 vs. ~25 % profitable in Barber-Lee-Liu-Odean's 1992–2006 Taiwan sample for the equivalent short window. The right tail is *not* expanding with technology. It is compressing.

Source: ENVY Protocol, ["I Analyzed 10,000 Hyperliquid Traders"](https://medium.com/@envyprotocol/i-analyzed-10-000-hyperliquid-traders-the-results-are-brutal-a29adcca8c2a); [BeInCrypto coverage](https://beincrypto.com/hyperliquid-traders-profitability/).

## Robinhood — the herd-as-counter-indicator

Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, *Journal of Finance* (2022):

- Top stocks bought by Robinhood users return **−4.7 % over the next 20 days** (abnormal).
- Robinhood's herd is not just losing — it is *systematically* tradable as a contrarian signal by anyone with a Bloomberg.
- The 2018 launch of zero-commission trading on Robinhood didn't lift the right tail. It widened the funnel into the loss zone.

Source: [Wiley/JoF paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13183); [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3715077).

## DALBAR — the average vs. the index, year by year

DALBAR's QAIB tracks the gap between the average equity-fund investor and the S&P 500 from realized-flow data:

- 2024: investor 16.54 %, S&P 25.02 %. **Gap: 848 bps** — second-largest in a decade.
- 2015–2019 (pre-COVID): 53 bps/year average drag from poor timing.
- 2020–2024 (post-COVID): **101 bps/year.** Nearly double.
- **15 consecutive years** of average-equity-investor underperformance versus the S&P. Last beat year: 2009.

Source: [DALBAR press release](https://www.dalbar.com/press-release/investors-missed-the-best-of-2024s-market-gains-latest-dalbar-investor-behavior-report-finds/); [PLANADVISER coverage](https://www.planadviser.com/investors-bad-behavior-led-sharp-underperformance-2024/).

## Crypto right-tail compression

Direct longitudinal data is thin, but two facts box the ceiling:

- 2024 crypto VC capital raised: **$5.1 billion across 79 new funds — the lowest since 2020**, average and median fund sizes lowest since 2017. The institutional bid has thinned and concentrated.
- Bitcoin retail inflows fell to **332 BTC (30-day MA) — the lowest level since Binance launched in 2017.**
- Closed-allocation private rounds at $5–50B FDVs are the new normal. The 100× retail-accessible round (ETH 2014, SOL 2020) has been replaced by structured ICOs with VC-pre-allocated unlock cliffs.

Source: [insights4vc 2024 crypto VC review](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/2024-crypto-venture-capital-trends); [CoinDesk on structured ICOs](https://www.coindesk.com/research/market-spotlight-the-structured-speculation-thesis-why-icos-are-back); [CryptoRank on retail Bitcoin inflows](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/5a89a-bitcoin-retail-inflows-nine-year-low).

## Mag-7 concentration as a retail-edge killer

- Top 10 S&P 500 names: **~70 % of the index's economic profit.**
- Magnificent Seven: **~33 % of S&P 500 market cap** (2026); **42 % of total 2025 return.**
- SPIVA US 2024 year-end: 65 %+ of large-cap active funds underperformed S&P 500 over 1-year, 85 %+ over 10-year, 90 %+ over 15-year horizons.

The stock-picking opportunity set has narrowed to a handful of names that institutions already own at scale. The retail edge from "find a small undervalued name" is structurally smaller than it was in 1992 when Fama-French published.

Source: [Russell Investments on concentration](https://russellinvestments.com/content/ri/us/en/insights/russell-research/2024/10/the-magnificent-seven-market-concentrations-and-complications-.html); [S&P SPIVA US Mid-Year 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us/).

## What's missing — the dream paper does not yet exist

No academic study has directly measured Robinhood top-decile PnL in 2018 vs. 2024 as a longitudinal series. No public Hyperliquid cohort study compares 2023 vintage to 2025 vintage at the right tail. The data exists — Robinhood holds it, Hyperliquid wallets are public — but nobody has done the right-tail compression chart. This is a research gap, not a refutation. The proxies (PEAD decay, momentum decay, McLean-Pontiff post-publication decay, SPIVA persistence, DALBAR widening) all point the same direction.

The right tail of retail performance is shorter than it was. Every retail-accessible alpha published has decayed by 32–100 %. The Mag-7 has eaten the dispersion that small-cap pickers used to feed on. The few who persist — the Taiwan 1 %, the Hyperliquid 16 % — survive on a structurally smaller pie than they would have inherited a decade earlier.

Retail always lost. It just used to lose less, more slowly, and with the occasional accidental winner. Now the loss is faster, deeper, and the accidental winner is statistically a memory.
