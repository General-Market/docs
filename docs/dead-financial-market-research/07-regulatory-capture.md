## B · Crypto industry lobbying — the 2024 surge

The crypto industry mounted the largest political spend of any new industry in US history during the 2024 cycle.

### Fairshake PAC (the umbrella)
- Funded primarily by Coinbase, Ripple, Andreessen Horowitz.
- 2024 cycle disclosed contributions: **$200M+**.
- Focus: defeat anti-crypto Senate candidates (notably Sherrod Brown OH); support pro-crypto candidates regardless of party.
- Outcome: 53/55 endorsed candidates won; widely credited with shifting Senate composition on crypto policy.
- Source: FEC filings, OpenSecrets, Bloomberg.

### Coinbase direct lobbying
- 2020: ~$0.2M federal lobbying
- 2024: **$4M+** federal lobbying
- Source: OpenSecrets.

### Coinbase political donations (post-Citizens United, via Coinbase Inc PAC + executives)
- 2024 cycle: tens of millions in donations + Fairshake contributions.

### FTX / SBF (pre-collapse 2022)
- Sam Bankman-Fried publicly disclosed plans to spend $1B in 2024 cycle.
- Donated ~$40M in 2022 cycle to Democrats; second-largest individual donor that cycle.
- Funds later subject to clawback claims after FTX bankruptcy.
- Source: FEC filings, prosecutorial filings in SDNY.

The collective implication: the regulated and the regulator increasingly share political infrastructure.

## C · Blocked SEC rules — the PFOF reform saga

### Timeline

- **December 2022**: SEC Chair Gary Gensler proposes four interlinked rules to reform retail trading:
  - **Order Competition Rule** (Rule 615) — would have required retail orders to be auctioned, not internalized to wholesalers.
  - **Best Execution Rule** (Reg Best Ex) — would have required brokers to demonstrate best execution by methodology.
  - **Tick Size / Access Fee** — would have changed exchange fee structures.
  - **Rule 605 amendments** — would have improved order-execution reporting.
- Industry response: SIFMA, Citadel Securities, Virtu, Robinhood, brokerage industry — all opposed Rule 615 specifically.
- Coordinated lobbying campaign 2023–2024.
- **March 2024**: SEC released final rules for tick size and Rule 605 reporting, **but dropped Order Competition Rule entirely**.
- **2024–2025**: Gensler's tenure ended January 2025; new commission has signaled rollback of remaining rules.

Source: SEC press releases 2022 and 2024; FT, WSJ coverage.

The page can cite this single arc to make the regulatory-capture point: the central proposal to reform retail flow internalization was lobbied to death over 16 months.

## E · Asymmetric enforcement — the disciplinary record

A defensible claim, requires careful sourcing: HFT firms and major market makers face **smaller, more often-settled fines** for market-structure violations than retail-facing firms face for customer protection violations.

Examples of HFT/MM settlements (post-2015):
- Citadel Securities — $700K SEC settlement 2017 (sub-pennying)
- Citadel Securities — $97.3M SEC settlement (in stages) 2017–2020 for various market-making and PFOF issues
- Virtu Financial — $5M SEC settlement 2024 for information barrier failures around customer data
- Tower Research — $67.4M CFTC settlement 2019 for spoofing
- Jump Trading — $25M SEC settlement July 2023 for Terra/Luna market manipulation allegations
- Sigma Chain — implicated in SEC v. Binance complaint 2023

Examples of retail-broker settlements:
- Robinhood — **$65M** SEC settlement 2020 for PFOF misrepresentation
- Robinhood — $70M FINRA settlement 2021 for outages, options approval
- Robinhood — $26M FINRA settlement March 2024
- Coinbase — facing SEC v. Coinbase litigation 2023–2025 ($billions exposure)
- Binance — **$4.3B DOJ settlement** November 2023 for BSA / AML violations
- FTX — fraud case 2022–2024 (criminal)

The pattern the article should note: HFT/MM violations get **single-digit million settlements without admission**. Retail-facing failures or fraud get **nine to ten-figure** settlements. The structural extraction is treated as misconduct; the customer-facing fraud is treated as crime.

## F · MEV — no regulator at all

The point worth making in one line: there is no SEC, no CFTC, no equivalent for MEV. The Ethereum and Solana extraction zones are entirely unregulated. The harm is structural, the perpetrators are pseudonymous, and the surface is not in any agency's jurisdiction.

## G · Margin / circuit-breaker asymmetries — the Robinhood-GME case

The single most visceral example for a retail audience:

- **January 28, 2021**: Robinhood disabled buy-side trading on GameStop, AMC, and other "meme stocks" while permitting sell-side trades to continue.
- Stated cause: NSCC clearinghouse capital call increased from ~$125M to **~$1.4B** overnight (CCO testimony to House Financial Services Committee).
- Robinhood subsequently raised $3.4B+ in emergency capital.
- Outcome of class-action against Robinhood: dismissed November 2022 (no breach of contract; margin agreement gave Robinhood discretion).

The structural reading: when retail demand caused HFT firms (notably Citadel, which was simultaneously the largest Robinhood PFOF customer AND a backer of Melvin Capital, the largest short of GME) to face material losses, the retail buy-side was halted while the sell-side remained open.

Source: Robinhood Margin Disclosure Statement; House Financial Services Committee hearings February 2021 and March 2021.

## H · The summary paragraph (proposed)

> *The regulators exist. They are funded, staffed, and competent. They are also outspent, out-lobbied, and out-revolved. The single rule that would have reformed retail order flow — the SEC's 2022 Order Competition proposal — died after sixteen months of coordinated industry opposition and a $200M crypto-industry election cycle. The HFT firms pay single-digit-million fines without admission. The retail-facing firms pay nine-figure penalties when they fail their customers. The structural extraction is treated as a misdemeanor.*
