## Adam Khoo — Analysis

- **Total shorts:** 50 | **Avg views:** 28,860 | **Median views:** 20,500
- **View range:** 7K to 143K

### Top 3 Shorts (by views)

1. **"Stocks that are on my Watchlist"** — 143K views
   - **Why it worked:** Watchlist videos are inherently high-intent content. Viewers want to know what a wealthy investor with $10M+ in the market is actually buying. The title uses the eyes emoji and "Watchlist" which signals insider knowledge. It names specific tickers (Amazon, ASML, Microsoft) with exact intrinsic values, creating both curiosity and actionable info.

2. **"The Stock Market is PANICKING..."** — 90K views
   - **Why it worked:** Fear-driven headline during a market downturn. The all-caps "PANICKING" creates urgency. Adam reframes the fear as opportunity, which gives contrarian reassurance. People flock to content during crashes seeking guidance.

3. **"Here's my SECRET to making $$ in the Stock Market"** — 79K views
   - **Why it worked:** The word "SECRET" combined with the money emoji triggers massive curiosity. The actual content is emotionally charged ("buy the effing dip" repeated multiple times) making it shareable and memorable. He shows his own P&L ($170K+ profits on Google) which provides social proof.

### Bottom 3 Shorts (by views)

1. **"Price is what you pay, Value is what you get!"** — 7K views
   - **Why it underperformed:** Generic Warren Buffett quote as title. No urgency, no specific ticker, no controversy. Pure educational content without a hook that creates curiosity or fear.

2. **"How NLP Changed My Life"** — 10K views
   - **Why it underperformed:** Completely off-topic from finance. NLP (neuro-linguistic programming) is a personal development topic that alienates the finance audience. Long biographical storytelling without a financial payoff.

3. **"The Investment Megatrend to watch for 2026"** — 11K views
   - **Why it underperformed:** Vague title. "Megatrend" is overused. No specific ticker or dollar amount. The content is about energy infrastructure which is educational but lacks the emotional charge of his top performers.

### Content Style
Educational stock analysis with a value investing lens. Adam uses his proprietary "StockOracle" tool as a recurring prop. He combines macro outlook with specific stock picks and always ties back to intrinsic value and economic moats. He has a "professor who trades" vibe, blending teaching with portfolio flexing.

| Statistic | 6 | 12% |

| Trend/Prediction | 4 | 8% |
| Historical Event | 2 | 4% |

### Hook Analysis
Best-performing hooks share these traits:
- **Emotional triggers:** "PANICKING," "SECRET," "LOSE Money," "CRASHING" — all-caps emotional words dominate top performers
- **Specificity:** Naming specific stocks (Duolingo, UnitedHealth, ASML) outperforms generic market commentary
- **Contrarian framing:** "Why this might NOT be a bubble," "NEVER trust the media" — going against the crowd drives clicks
- **Portfolio voyeurism:** Showing his own $10M+ positions creates aspirational curiosity

### View Multipliers Observed
- Naming specific popular stocks (NVDA, DUOL, UNH) in titles: ~2-3x baseline
- Fear/panic market context: ~3-4x baseline
- Showing personal P&L or portfolio size: ~2-3x baseline
- Using "SECRET" or "watchlist" framing: ~3-5x baseline

### Anti-patterns Observed
- Off-topic content (NLP, self-help): drops to bottom 10%
- Generic investing wisdom without specific tickers: underperforms
- Tool/product demos (StockOracle walkthroughs): below average unless paired with popular stock
- Vague "megatrend" language without naming companies: low engagement

### Unique Strengths
Adam's $10M+ portfolio gives him unmatched credibility for "what I'm actually buying" content. His consistent use of intrinsic value calculations provides a unique data-driven framework that most finance creators lack. He bridges the gap between retail and institutional thinking.

### Key Takeaway
Adam Khoo performs best when he combines market fear/panic context with specific stock picks and his own portfolio actions. Generic educational content about investing principles falls flat; his audience wants to know what the rich guy is doing with his money right now.

## Andrei Jikh — Analysis

- **Total shorts:** 50 | **Avg views:** 75,580 | **Median views:** 51,000
- **View range:** 9K to 281K

### Top 3 Shorts (by views)

1. **"The Carry Trade That Shaped Global Markets"** — 281K views
   - **Why it worked:** Massive macro topic (Japan carry trade) that was in the news cycle. Andrei explains it simply with an analogy-driven style. The title promises a grand narrative about what shapes the entire market, appealing to intellectually curious viewers.

2. **"Why The U.S. Just Invaded Venezuela"** — 266K views
   - **Why it worked:** Breaking geopolitical news with a provocative framing. "Invaded" is a loaded word. He connects it to oil, power, and US dollar hegemony, which are topics his audience deeply cares about. The conspiracy-adjacent framing (war sold through narratives) creates strong engagement.

3. **"Why Bitcoin Is Struggling"** — 211K views
   - **Why it worked:** Bitcoin content always pulls views, and the "struggling" framing activates concern from holders. He provides a technical explanation (OP return update) that most crypto content skips, adding real informational value.

### Bottom 3 Shorts (by views)

1. **"When AI Replaces Jobs, the Economy Breaks"** — 9K views
   - **Why it underperformed:** Vague title. AI job replacement is an oversaturated topic. The content is abstract philosophical musing rather than concrete analysis.

2. **"How The Economy Is Broken"** — 20K views
   - **Why it underperformed:** Extremely generic title with no specificity. "Economy is broken" has been said a million times. Short script with no concrete data or story.

3. **"The Immigration Problem is Economic"** — 26K views
   - **Why it underperformed:** Immigration is politically charged but the take is measured/centrist which doesn't generate strong engagement from either side. No shocking data or narrative hook.

He frequently discusses the dollar, world order shifts, commodities (silver, gold), and power dynamics between US/China/Russia. Andrei Jikh dominates when he tells grand geopolitical narratives connecting world events to financial markets. His audience craves "big picture" understanding of why the world works the way it does. Generic economic commentary or oversaturated AI content fails for him.

### Universal Patterns Across All Creators

**What drives views:**
1. **Fear/crisis context** — Market crashes, panics, recessions. Every single creator's top performers coincide with market stress events.
2. **Specific dollar amounts or named entities** — "$1,500 in passive income," "Japan Bonds," "Venezuela," "Nvidia." Specificity always wins over abstraction.
3. **Contrarian framing** — "NOT a bubble," "NEVER trust the media," "struggling" — going against the consensus narrative drives engagement.
4. **Grand narratives** — "New World Order," "The Last Phase of the Dollar," "How Did We Get Here?" — people want to feel like they understand the big picture.
5. **Personal transparency** — Showing real portfolios, real P&L, real salaries. Authenticity through specificity.

**What kills views:**
1. **Generic titles** — "Price is what you pay, Value is what you get" — proverbs don't work as hooks.
2. **Product promotion** — Anytime content feels like an ad, engagement craters.
3. **Off-topic content** — NLP, oysters, quantum physics — audience came for finance, not lifestyle.
4. **Podcast clip repurposing without editing** — Long, rambling clips without a clear hook or payoff.
5. **Saturation/fatigue** — Tariffs, AI job replacement — topics that have been covered to death.

### Fact Type Ranking by View Performance (across all creators)

1. **Historical Event + Geopolitics** — Highest view multiplier when paired with current events
2. **Counter-Take** — Consistent performer across all creators
3. **Money Fact** — Specific dollar amounts always outperform vague claims
4. **Technical Explanation** — Works well when wrapped in entertainment (Coding Jesus) or narrative (Kyla)
5. **Hack/Tip** — Moderate performer, works best with specificity
6. **Trend/Prediction** — Works for Andrei/Kyla, less so for others
7. **Statistic** — Supporting role, rarely drives views alone
8. **Personal Experience** — Works when combined with transparency (showing real money)
9. **Comparison** — Underutilized across all creators
10. **Celebrity/Brand Name** — Moderate performer, depends on the name
