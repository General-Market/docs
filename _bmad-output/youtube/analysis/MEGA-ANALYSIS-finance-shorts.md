## Executive Summary

721 shorts. 22 creators. View counts from 85 to 7.4M. The data reveals a brutal truth: **format and framing matter 100x more than expertise depth.** tastylive (founded by an options trading legend) averages 1.9K views. The Plain Bagel (solo creator, 4 shorts) averages 193K. Same industry. Same audience. Completely different packaging.

The single strongest predictor of performance across all 22 creators: **whether the content was designed natively for short-form with an emotional hook, or repurposed from longer content.**

## 7 Universal Laws of Finance Shorts (From 721 Data Points)

### Law 1: The Counter-Take is King
Every single top performer across all 22 creators uses some form of counter-intuitive framing. "No, BlackRock doesn't control everything" (333K). "Was Toyota right?" (3.7M). "Sam Altman never ran a successful startup" (20K from a tiny channel). The data is unambiguous: **challenging what viewers think they know is the #1 view driver.**

### Law 2: Named Entities Are 3-5x Multipliers
GoPro, Toyota, BlackRock, Buffett, FTX, Tata, Mumbai, Japan — every top performer names something recognizable. "A company went bankrupt" = nobody cares. "GoPro is going bankrupt" = 7.4M views. "A hedge fund dodged taxes" = skip. "Renaissance dodged $6.8B in taxes" = 215K. **Never use generic descriptors when you can name names.**

### Law 3: Specific Dollar Amounts Are Engagement Magnets
$285M salary. $6.8B in taxes. $48M Polymarket bet. $2M Roth IRA. $85K in one morning. $1.98 per share deduction. Every top performer includes at least one specific, shocking number. **Vague wealth references ("a lot of money") are invisible. Exact figures are magnetic.**

### Law 4: Native Shorts > Repurposed Clips by 100x
The data is devastating:
- tastylive (repurposed broadcast clips): median 1K views
- The Plain Bagel (designed-for-shorts content): median 153.5K views
- Dimitri Bianco (podcast clip excerpts): median 979 views
- Patrick Boyle (native short-form scripts): median 130K views

**Chopping a podcast into clips is not a Shorts strategy. It's a content graveyard.**

### Law 5: Humor Multiplies Everything
Socratica's Python shorts (with jokes): 25-41K. Socratica's options shorts (no jokes, same channel): 2-3K. Patrick Boyle's dry wit turns institutional finance into entertainment. Vivian Tu's "strip" hook on a savings tutorial. Sharan Hegde's comedy skits. **Humor is the great equalizer — it makes complex content shareable.**

### Law 6: Persona Beats Information
Vivian Tu ("Your Rich BFF"): 104K avg. The Trading Channel (pure information, no persona): 62K avg. Warrior Trading (Ross's transparent journey): 29K avg. SMB Capital (institutional but faceless): 15K avg. tastylive (expert but no persona for shorts): 1.9K avg. **The audience follows a character, not a lesson.**

### Law 7: Promotion Is Poison
Every single creator's worst-performing shorts are promotional:
- New Money event announcement: 8K vs 52K educational
- Vivian Tu podcast cross-promo: 12K vs 1.5M educational
- Austin Hankwitz affiliate-heavy content: 375 median
- Wall Street Millennial ZipRecruiter ad: 3K vs 28K editorial
- tastylive Inside Access plugs: consistently bottom quartile

**Self-promotion, podcast cross-promo, brand deals without educational value, and corporate announcements are the universal view killers.**

## Per-Creator Strategic Insights

### Tier 1: The Consistent Performers (Median >50K)

**The Plain Bagel** — Laser-focused "No, [viral claim] is wrong" formula. Highest floor in the dataset (133K minimum). Proves frequency is irrelevant when format-market fit is perfect. Only 4 shorts but every single one crushes.

**Patrick Boyle** — Makes institutional finance entertaining through dry British wit. Financial scandals + precise details + humor = his winning formula. Even "underperformers" break 65K.

**Money & Macro** — PhD-level macro analysis in 60 seconds. Country-specific economic crises with tangible human consequences (blackouts, ships idle, food prices doubling). The most journalistic creator in the dataset.

**Vivian Tu** — The most commercially sophisticated creator. "Your Rich BFF" persona turns personal finance into pop culture. Universal topics (leggings, hiring, productivity) as on-ramps to finance. Top 3 are sponsored (Adobe) yet outperform everyone — proving good sponsorships can enhance, not kill, engagement.

### Tier 2: The Strong Performers (Median 25-50K)

**Andrei Jikh** — Geopolitical macro storyteller. Venezuela, Japan carry trade, dollar hegemony. Grand narratives about "why the world works this way" pull consistently high views.

**Sharan Hegde** — Highest ceiling in entire dataset (5.8M). India-specific finance with comedy skits for viral reach. Tax hacks, real estate, and expert interviews are his bread and butter.

**Kyla Scanlon** — The intellectual's finance creator. Weaves Marshall McLuhan, Hyman Minsky, and movie references into economics journalism. Multi-topic news roundups during crises are her highest performers.

**The Trading Channel** — Named chart patterns are his goldmine ("Double Bottom Rules" at 574K). SEO-optimized, search-driven content for the massive beginner audience. Generic motivational advice underperforms by 3-5x.

**Coding Jesus** — Unique format: "American Idol for quant devs." Live mock interviews where people fail publicly = entertainment + education. The only creator making schadenfreude work in finance.

**Warrior Trading** — Day trading as reality TV. Radical P&L transparency ($85K wins, $38K losses, $2M Roth IRA). The audience follows the journey, not just the lessons.

**Ticker Symbol YOU** — Only 5 shorts but dot-com-era credibility gives authority on AI bubble questions. Needs to commit to the format.

### Tier 3: The Niche Players (Median 10-25K)

**Adam Khoo** — $10M+ portfolio gives him "what the rich guy is actually buying" credibility. Performs best during market panic when paired with specific stock picks. Generic wisdom drops to bottom quartile.

**Wall Street Millennial** — Contrarian corporate debunker with a cynical edge. Only 7 shorts — needs volume. Sam Altman and Kevin O'Leary takedowns are his best work.

**SMB Capital** — Institutional credibility from a real prop firm. "Simple" framing and warning/negative hooks ("Do NOT trade like this") outperform pure technical content by 3-5x.

**Logically Answered** — The wildcard. Median only 13K but two massive outliers (7.4M + 3.7M). When a corporate autopsy hits a universally recognized consumer brand, it's unmatched. When it misses (B2B, meta-content), it's mediocre.

**Socratica** — Proves humor is the multiplier. Python shorts (with jokes): 25-41K. Options shorts (no jokes): 2-3K. Same channel, same production quality. The only variable is funny vs. not funny.

### Tier 4: The Underperformers (Median <5K)

**QuantNomad** — Legitimate long-tail search strategy for Pine Script tutorials. Not designed for virality — functions as documentation. Respectable within its intent.

**tastylive** — World-class options expertise completely wasted by the repurposed broadcast clip format. The #1 candidate for a format transformation. Switching to native shorts would likely 10x their views.

**Dimitri Bianco** — Too niche (quant careers) and too unedited (raw podcast clips) for shorts. Best content targets career-seeking students.

**Austin Hankwitz** — Heavy affiliate monetization destroys organic engagement. Median 375 views suggests either tiny subscriber base or severe algorithm suppression from promotional content.

## The 5 Biggest Surprises in the Data

1. **Sponsored content CAN outperform organic.** Vivian Tu's top 3 shorts are all Adobe Acrobat sponsorships. The difference: the sponsored content was designed as educational content first, sponsorship second. The "phone a friend" format works as genuine entertainment.

2. **Publishing frequency has zero correlation with average performance.** The Plain Bagel (4 shorts, 193K avg) vs. tastylive (50 shorts, 1.9K avg). Quality and format fit > volume by orders of magnitude.

3. **The "Hack/Tip" format is simultaneously the most-produced AND the lowest-performing type.** 12 of 22 creators use it as their primary format. It consistently underperforms counter-takes, stories, and comparisons. The single exception: when the tip is hyper-specific with a named brand and exact dollar savings (Tata car at 5.8M).

4. **Humor matters more than credentials.** Socratica's Python jokes beat their options expertise. Patrick Boyle's wit beats Bloomberg-level analysis. Vivian Tu's "strip" hook beats Wall Street credentials. The audience is choosing entertainment first, education second.

5. **India is a sleeping giant for finance shorts.** Sharan Hegde has the highest absolute ceiling (5.8M) and a strong median (47K). India-specific real estate and tax content has an enormous addressable audience that Western-focused creators don't touch.

## What This Means For Content Strategy

### If you're starting from zero:
1. Pick the **Counter-Take / Myth-Buster** archetype (Plain Bagel model). Highest floor, lowest production cost.
2. Every short should challenge a common belief with specific evidence.
3. Name names. Use dollar amounts. Keep it self-contained.

### If you're an expert with low views:
1. You're probably in the tastylive/Dimitri Bianco trap — expertise without packaging.
2. Stop repurposing clips. Design every short natively.
3. Wrap every technical insight in a story, scandal, or counter-take.
4. Add humor. Even dry humor. Even one joke per short.

### If you want maximum viral ceiling:
1. **Corporate autopsy** (Logically Answered model) on universally recognized consumer brands.
2. **Comedy skit + finance education** (Sharan Hegde model) — highest ceilings but requires performance skills.
3. **Novel concept introduction** through universal topics (Vivian Tu model) — "productivity debt" through leggings.

### For trading-specific content:
1. **Named chart patterns** with "for beginners" in the title (The Trading Channel model) — search-driven, evergreen.
2. **Real P&L transparency** (Warrior Trading model) — the reality show approach.
3. **Warning/loss framing** over positive tips (SMB Capital model) — "Do NOT do this" beats "Do this."
4. **Never** publish pure technical explanations without a story, dollar amount, or emotional hook.
