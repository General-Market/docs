# The Cost of a CTA: View Penalty by Type
## Estimated View Impact of Every CTA Style Observed Across 950 YouTube Shorts

**Date:** 2026-02-12
**Source:** [Per-Creator Top vs Bottom Storytelling Analysis](./channels-top-vs-bottom-storytelling.md) (~950 Shorts, 19 creators)
**Method:** For each CTA type, compared view performance of videos using that technique vs the same creator's organic baseline. Estimates are directional, not precise -- based on pattern clustering, not A/B tests.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Tier 1: FREE (0% or positive impact)

---

#### 1. No CTA / End on Punchline
**Estimated cost: 0% (this IS the baseline)**

The content ends on its strongest beat. No ask, no redirect, no engagement prompt.

| Creator | Technique | Performance |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Fireship | Ends on joke/punchline. "Anti-CTAs," sarcastic PBS sign-offs | Top content: 900K-8.7M |
| ZackDFilms | Ends on final story beat. Sharp, ominous, darkly humorous | Top content: 7.9M-22M |
| Hormozi | Trails off mid-sentence. Viewer left wanting more | Top content: 96K-462K |
| How Money Works | Ends on wry observation | Top content: 96K-5.4M |
| Caleb Hammer | Clips end at peak tension | Top content: 806K-2.6M |

**Why it costs nothing:** The algorithm rewards retention. If your ending IS the content's peak moment, viewers watch to the end and often rewatch. No CTA means no retention drop at the close.

---

#### 2. Cliffhanger / Mid-Sentence Cutoff
**Estimated cost: +5% to +15% (actually HELPS)**

Video cuts mid-explanation or mid-sentence, forcing curiosity. Drives follows, rewatches, and comments.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Mark Tilbury | ~half of top 25 use deliberate mid-sentence cutoff | Consistent in both halves, but TOP half has more of them. Top: 1.8M-18M |
| Veritasium | Ends abruptly mid-explanation (intentional) | Top content technique. Drives curiosity to long-form |
| MagnatesMedia | "because..." = deliberate cliffhanger in top half | When accidental in bottom, feels like running out of time |

**Why it's positive:** The cut creates an information gap that can only be closed by following/subscribing. It converts the ending into a hook for the next interaction. Mark Tilbury's highest performers (18M, 16M, 12M, 11M) almost all use this technique.

**Caveat:** Only works when clearly intentional. Accidental trailing off (MagnatesMedia bottom half) reads as poor editing and has negative impact.

---

#### 3. Branded Sign-Off
**Estimated cost: ~0%**

A consistent, short, identity-reinforcing closer that becomes a signature.

| Creator | Sign-off | Evidence |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Nas Daily | "That's one minute. See you tomorrow." | Present in ALL his top-performing documentary/parable content (700K-14M) |

**Why it's free:** It's 2-3 seconds, it's branded, and it closes the loop without asking anything. It functions as a period at the end of a sentence, not an interruption. It signals consistency and professionalism.

**Caveat:** Only Nas Daily uses this in the dataset. Works because it's brief, warm, and habitual. A longer branded outro would likely cost views.

---

#### 4. Product-as-Proof (Tool in Tutorial)
**Estimated cost: ~0%**

The product appears as a natural step in solving the problem the video teaches. No format break, no pitch. The tool IS the content.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Ahrefs | Tool demo woven into case study / tutorial | Top 4 videos (43K, 19K, 18K, 18K) ALL feature the product. Product content = top content |

**Why it's free:** The viewer doesn't perceive it as selling. The product is the mechanism that delivers the promised result. "We got 1M visitors" is the hook; the Ahrefs tool is how they did it. Removing the product would make the content less valuable, not more.

**Key requirement:** Your product must genuinely be the best tool for the specific problem shown. If you're stretching to fit it in, viewers notice and it becomes a -30% sponsor read instead.

---

#### 5. Open Question / Philosophical Close
**Estimated cost: ~0%**

Video ends with a thought-provoking question rather than an answer. Stimulates comments and reflection.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Kurzgesagt | Open existential questions after science content | Present in top half organic content |
| Veritasium | Leaves the "why" hanging after demonstrating the "what" | Top content technique |

**Why it's free:** Functions like a No-CTA ending but with an engagement mechanism built in. The question IS the content's payoff, not an appendage.

---

### Tier 2: CHEAP (-5% to -25%)

---

#### 6. In-Narrative Character CTA
**Estimated cost: -5% to -10%**

A character within the story delivers the CTA as dialogue, not as a break in the fourth wall.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Erika Kullberg | Antagonist: "Who taught you this?" Protagonist: "Erica taught me. She's a lawyer and reads the fine print so I don't have to." | Present in her TOP organic content (120K-1.5M). The CTA IS the story's resolution |

**Why it's cheap:** The CTA is the punchline. The viewer has watched a narrative arc (helplessness > empowerment > triumph) and the CTA is the satisfying conclusion. It doesn't interrupt the story -- it completes it.

**Key requirement:** The CTA must function as narrative resolution. If the character says "go to erica.com/invest" it becomes an Explicit URL CTA (-40% to -60%) instead. Erika's organic skits use the character device; her promotional skits add the URL and that's where performance drops.

---

#### 7. Soft Engagement Prompt
**Estimated cost: -5% to -15%**

"Let me know in the comments," "What do you think?", "Drop a like if you agree."

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Ali Abdaal | Frequent engagement prompts across both halves | Consistent, no dramatic penalty. Top: 29K-125K |
| Humphrey Yang | "Let me know in the comments." Natural, conversational | Present in both halves. Minimal impact |

**Why it's cheap:** Brief, non-intrusive, doesn't break format. The cost comes from diluting the ending -- instead of ending on your strongest beat, you end on a generic ask. The shorter and more natural, the less it costs.

**Cost scales with intensity:** "What do you think?" = ~-5%. "Like, comment, subscribe, and hit the bell" = ~-15% or worse.

---

#### 8. Redirect to Longer Content
**Estimated cost: -10% to -20%**

"Watch the full video," "Link to the deep dive below."

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Economics Explained | Funnels to main channel long-form video | Consistent across both halves |
| Ahrefs | "Watch the video below to learn how..." | Top performer (43K) uses this. Minimal penalty |
| Veritasium | Some bottom videos point to long-form | Slightly more common in bottom half |

**Why it's moderate:** You're explicitly saying "this isn't the full value -- go elsewhere." This can reduce the perceived completeness of the Short. Works best when the Short already delivered a satisfying standalone payoff and the redirect is bonus, not required.

---

#### 9. Newsletter Plug (Integrated)
**Estimated cost: -10% to -15%**

Newsletter or subscription mentioned naturally mid-content or as a brief closer, matching the content's tone.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Patrick Boyle | Newsletter plug in ~1/3 of videos, integrated into content tone | Top half content: 130K-337K |
| Slidebean | Newsletter plug when woven into the analysis | Top half uses this (~17K-277K) |

**Why it's moderate:** When integrated ("I wrote about this in more detail in my newsletter"), it functions like a redirect to longer content. The cost is real but manageable because the plug matches the content's voice.

---

#### 10. Trusted Product Recommendation (Organic)
**Estimated cost: -15% to -25%**

Creator recommends a product they genuinely use, with personal endorsement. Not formally sponsored.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Ali Abdaal | Books, apps, tools he uses. "This $10 book made me $2M" | Top content when framed as personal discovery. ~60K average |
| Humphrey Yang | Affiliate links, natural mention of tools | Minimal penalty when organic |

**Why it's moderate:** Trust carries it, but the viewer still recognizes a recommendation as somewhat promotional. The cost depends on how personal and specific the endorsement is. "This specific book changed my business" = -15%. "Check out this cool app" = -25%.

---

### Tier 3: EXPENSIVE (-30% to -60%)

---

#### 11. Named Framework → Event/Workshop CTA
**Estimated cost: -20% to -35%**

Teach a framework, then sell access to deeper implementation (workshop, course, coaching).

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Codie Sanchez | Named frameworks (TRUST, "core four", "snake strike"), then "come to our event" | Most commercially-oriented creator. Top: 50K-196K, heavy-CTA bottom: ~30K |

**Why it's moderate-to-expensive:** The framework provides real value, which cushions the sell. But the transition from "here's knowledge" to "pay for more" is always detectable. Codie manages it better than anyone in the dataset, but even her CTA-heavy content drops ~35% from her best organic.

**Cost depends on ratio:** 90% value + 10% sell = -20%. 60% value + 40% sell = -35%+.

---

#### 12. Newsletter Plug (Appended)
**Estimated cost: -30% to -50%**

Newsletter mention bolted onto the end, clearly disconnected from the content's natural conclusion.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Slidebean | Same newsletter plug, but in bottom half it "feels more appended than integrated" | Bottom half: 7K-17K vs top half: 17K-277K |
| Patrick Boyle | Newsletter plug when it feels like an afterthought | Bottom half: 65K-129K |

**Why it's expensive:** The viewer feels the content ended and then got an ad tacked on. It signals "this content exists to sell you something" retroactively, devaluing the experience they just had.

**The difference between -10% and -50% is purely execution.** Same CTA, same product. Integrated = cheap. Appended = expensive.

---

#### 13. Explicit URL CTA
**Estimated cost: -40% to -60%**

"Go to erica.com/invest," "Click the link in bio," "Head to [domain]."

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Erika Kullberg | "erica.com/invest", "erica.com/future", "erica.com/state" | Workshop/URL videos: 7K-45K vs organic skits: 200K-1.5M |
| Codie Sanchez | Event URLs in some videos | Bottom half when URL is the focus |

**Why it's expensive:** Saying a URL out loud breaks the content's narrative reality completely. It's the verbal equivalent of a pop-up ad. The viewer's brain switches from "entertainment/education mode" to "I'm being marketed to" instantly.

**Escalates with repetition:** URL mentioned once at the very end = -40%. URL mentioned twice + "link in bio" = -60%+.

---

### Tier 4: VERY EXPENSIVE (-60% to -90%)

---

#### 14. "Comment [Keyword]" Farming
**Estimated cost: -60% to -80%**

Creator asks viewers to comment a specific word to receive a link, resource, or freebie.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Vincent Chan | "Comment [keyword]" on almost every video. Most CTA-heavy creator | Top: 10K-2.6M (identity content). Bottom: 2K-10K (keyword farming) |
| Nas Daily | "Comment NAS" repeated 2-3x in product promos | Promo content with keyword CTA: 34K-209K vs story content: 700K-14M |

**Why it's very expensive:** It reads as engagement manipulation. The algorithm may actively deprioritize content that solicits artificial engagement signals. Viewers have learned to associate "comment [word]" with funnel marketing, creating immediate distrust.

**Vincent Chan is the case study:** His identity-driven content ("I can clock if you didn't grow up with money") reaches 2.6M. His "comment [keyword]" videos consistently land at 2K-10K. Same creator, same audience, same production quality. The CTA is the variable.

---

#### 15. Sponsor Script (#ad)
**Estimated cost: -65% to -85%**

Creator reads a sponsor-provided script with #ad disclosure.

| Creator | Organic Avg | #ad Avg | Drop | Evidence |
|---------|-------------|---------|------|---------|
| Erika Kullberg | ~200K | 5K-7K (State Street ETF ads) | **-97%** | Three #ad videos = 3 lowest performers |
| Ali Abdaal | ~60K | 16K-29K | **-67%** | All 3 #ad videos land in bottom half |
| Humphrey Yang | ~200K | ~27K (Claude AI) | **-87%** | Sponsored demos = lowest performers |

**Why it's very expensive:** The format breaks in three ways simultaneously:
1. **Tone shift** -- the creator's natural voice changes to scripted delivery
2. **#ad disclosure** -- signals "this is an ad" before content begins
3. **Foreign product** -- viewer didn't come to learn about ETFs or AI assistants

**The triple penalty:** Each factor alone costs ~20-30%. Combined, they compound to -65% to -97%.

---

#### 16. Entertainment-as-Promo
**Estimated cost: -70% to -90%**

Creating entertainment/game content to promote a product in a niche where the audience expects utility.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Ahrefs | Brand Radar guessing games featuring Ahrefs data | 6 of 11 bottom videos. Games: 2K-4K vs case studies: 18K-43K. **-88%** |
| Kurzgesagt | Starbirds game promos mixed into science content | 561K-696K vs organic 1.5M+. **-75%** |

**Why it's very expensive:** Audience mismatch. People follow Ahrefs for SEO tactics, not game shows. People follow Kurzgesagt for existential science, not mobile games. The entertainment wrapper doesn't disguise the promo -- it confuses the audience about what the channel is.

**Counterintuitive:** You'd think entertainment = more accessible = more views. In practice, entertainment in a utility niche signals "this isn't for me" to the core audience while failing to reach a new one.

---

#### 17. Full Product Demo / Sales Video
**Estimated cost: -85% to -95%**

The entire video exists to showcase a product's features, pricing, or availability.

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Nas Daily | NAS.io feature announcements, product walkthroughs, "I built an AI that..." | Promo videos: 34K-91K vs story content: 452K-14M. **-90% to -95%** |
| Vincent Chan | "Free gift card" sponsored series | 3K-7K vs organic identity content: 50K+. **-90%** |
| Erika Kullberg | Pure workshop funnel videos ("Watch this before Monday") | 23K-45K vs organic skits: 200K-1.5M. **-85%** |

**Why it's the most expensive:** The content has zero standalone value. If you removed the product, there would be nothing left. The viewer gets nothing unless they buy. This inverts the social contract of content (value first, then maybe sell).

**Nas Daily is the clearest case:** His parable about drip irrigation (452K) and his NAS.io AI product launch (91K) are made by the same creator for the same audience. One tells a story. One sells a product. 5x difference.

---

#### 18. Multi-CTA Stacking
**Estimated cost: -90% to -95%**

Two or more CTAs layered in the same video: "Comment NAS" + "Go to NAS.io" + "Follow for more."

| Creator | Technique | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Nas Daily | Product promo + "Comment NAS" repeated 2-3x + "Work for yourself" + URL | Bottom half promos: 34K-91K vs stories: 700K-14M. |
| Vincent Chan | "Comment [keyword]" + "Follow" + URL + engagement prompt | Bottom half: 2K-10K |

**Why it's catastrophic:** Each additional CTA compounds the penalty. CTA #1 signals "this is promotional." CTA #2 confirms it. CTA #3 makes the viewer feel marketed AT rather than spoken TO. The content becomes a funnel, and Shorts viewers have zero tolerance for funnels.

## The One Rule

Every CTA type clusters along a single axis: **how much does the CTA break the content's format?**

- **0% cost:** The CTA IS the content (product-as-proof, punchline, cliffhanger)
- **-5% to -25%:** The CTA matches the content's format and tone (in-narrative, soft prompt, integrated plug)
- **-30% to -60%:** The CTA is visible but contained (framework → sell, appended plug, URL)
- **-65% to -95%:** The CTA replaces the content (sponsor script, product demo, multi-CTA stack)

The cost isn't about selling. It's about **format rupture.** Erika Kullberg sells in her top content ("She follows Erica") at near-zero cost because the sell IS the story's ending. She sells in her bottom content (erica.com/invest, #ad) at -97% because the sell breaks the story.

Same creator. Same product. Same audience. The only variable is integration.
