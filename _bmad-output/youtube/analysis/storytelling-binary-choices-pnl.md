# Storytelling Binary Choices: View PnL %
## When You Have Two Options, Which One Wins?

**Date:** 2026-02-12
**Source:** ~950 YouTube Shorts across 19 creators, script-level analysis
**Method:** For each binary choice, classified every video, compared average views per option within each creator (to avoid cross-creator bias), then aggregated

---

## 1. Single Story vs Listicle (+96%)

**The clearest signal in the entire dataset. Single story wins across EVERY creator.**

| Creator | Single Avg | Listicle Avg | PnL |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-----|
| Veritasium | 12.5M | 1.9M | **+558%** |
| Humphrey Yang | 620K | 155K | **+300%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 104K | 36K | **+189%** |
| Mark Tilbury | 4.46M | 2.28M | **+96%** |
| Erika Kullberg | 196K | 138K | **+42%** |
| Fireship | 1.52M | 1.25M | **+22%** |
| Econ Explained | 61K | 52K | **+17%** |

**Why:** A single story creates emotional engagement and narrative tension. Listicles feel generic and lack stakes. In 60 seconds, there isn't enough time to develop multiple items meaningfully. "5 things" promises breadth; one story delivers depth.

**Decision:** Always choose one compelling example over a numbered list, unless your niche is specifically curation (Ali Abdaal's book recommendations are the exception -- his top performer is "5 books").

## 2. Positive vs Negative Framing (+55%)

**Positive/aspirational hooks outperform fear/warning hooks for 7 of 10 creators.**

| Creator | Positive Avg | Negative Avg | PnL |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Humphrey Yang | 842K | 152K | **+454%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 216K | 62K | **+248%** |
| How Money Works | 700K | 406K | **+72%** |
| Fireship | 1.65M | 1.01M | **+63%** |
| Caleb Hammer | 908K | 729K | **+25%** |
| Econ Explained | 67K | 54K | **+24%** |
| Erika Kullberg | 183K | 176K | ~even |
| ZackDFilms | 8.3M | 8.4M | ~even |
| Veritasium | 11.8M | 13.8M | **-17%** |
| Mark Tilbury | 3.0M | 3.75M | **-25%** |

**Exceptions:** Mark Tilbury ("WHY YOU'RE POOR", "WARNING: Never Use") and Veritasium ("most dangerous problem") succeed with negative framing because their audiences expect provocative warnings as the content's identity.

**Decision:** Default to positive/aspirational framing. Only use negative framing if your brand identity is built around warnings or danger (Tilbury, ZackDFilms).

## 3. Specific Number in Hook (+42%)

**Numbers anchor attention and create concreteness, especially in finance/business.**

| Creator | With Number Avg | Without Number Avg | PnL |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|-----|
| Humphrey Yang | 1.19M | 189K | **+530%** |
| How Money Works | 890K | 407K | **+119%** |
| Mark Tilbury | 3.73M | 2.71M | **+38%** |
| Erika Kullberg | 198K | 146K | **+36%** |
| ZackDFilms | 9.5M | 7.6M | **+25%** |
| Caleb Hammer | 861K | 737K | **+17%** |
| Econ Explained | 65K | 61K | **+7%** |
| Fireship | 1.38M | 1.53M | **-11%** |
| Veritasium | 11.3M | 12.8M | **-13%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 94K | 167K | **-78%** |

**Pattern:** Numbers dominate in finance/business niches (Humphrey +530%, How Money Works +119%, Tilbury +38%). They underperform in science/tech/narrative niches (Fireship, Veritasium, MagnatesMedia) where curiosity-driven abstract hooks work better.

**Decision:** Use specific numbers in hooks if your niche involves money, results, or metrics. Avoid if your niche is driven by wonder, curiosity, or storytelling.

## 4. Citing Sources (-37% penalty)

**Citing sources correlates with FEWER views across 7 of 8 measurable creators.**

| Creator | Citing Avg | Not Citing Avg | PnL |
|---------|-----------|---------------|-----|
| Slidebean | 15K | 41.8K | **-64%** |
| Codie Sanchez | 14K | 33.5K | **-58%** |
| Ahrefs | 3.5K | 8.2K | **-57%** |
| Kurzgesagt | 967K | 1.96M | **-51%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 69K | 135.5K | **-49%** |
| Veritasium | 1.78M | 2.1M | **-15%** |
| Fireship | 1.28M | 1.35M | **-5%** |
| Econ Explained | 74.7K | 60.5K | **+23%** |

**10 of 19 channels had ZERO citations across all videos.** The most successful creators (ZackDFilms, Tilbury, Hormozi, Erika, Ali, Caleb, Vincent) never cite sources. They ARE the source.

### Citation Sub-Types

| Type | Avg Performance | Example |
|------|----------------|---------|
| **Person as character** | Best (-5% to +10%) | "23-year-old Isaac Newton" (3.5M), "Edward Bernays, nephew of Freud" (94K) |
| **Vague** ("studies show") | Medium (-15%) | "peer-reviewed studies have shown" (744K) |
| **Institution** (Harvard, IMF) | Bad (-40%) | "The World Bank classifies..." (38K) |
| **First-party data** ("our study") | Worst (-55%) | "our study shows clicks dropping 30%" (2K) |
| **Publication** (WSJ, Wired) | Bad (-50%) | "Wired just published" (21K) |

**The only citation that works is "Person as Character"** -- where the source becomes a protagonist in the story ("a scientist named Simha Blast went to a nearby farm..."). Everything else signals "this is homework."

**Decision:** Don't cite sources. If you must, turn the source into a character in a story. Never cite institutions, publications, or your own data -- it reads as corporate content.

## 5. Dialogue vs Monologue (+25%)

**Multiple voices outperform solo narration for 5 of 7 creators.**

| Creator | Dialogue Avg | Monologue Avg | PnL |
|---------|-------------|--------------|-----|
| Humphrey Yang | 521K | 356K | **+46%** |
| Mark Tilbury | 3.74M | 2.84M | **+32%** |
| Veritasium | 14.9M | 11.9M | **+25%** |
| Erika Kullberg | 188K | 158K | **+19%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 36.5K* | 143K | **-75%** |
| Econ Explained | 32.5K | 72.5K | **-55%** |

*Excluding MrBeast outlier

**When dialogue wins:** Scripted skits (Erika), conversations between characters (Tilbury), real demonstrations with other people (Veritasium), interviews with interesting subjects (Humphrey's gold dealers).

**When monologue wins:** Polished narrator-only explainers (Economics Explained), channels where the single voice IS the brand (Fireship -- 100% monologue).

**Decision:** Add a second voice if you can do scripted skits, real conversations, or demonstrations with people. Don't add interview clips that feel disconnected from the main narrative.

## 6. Mystery Description vs Name-Drop (Context-Dependent)

**There is no universal winner. It depends on whether the subject is famous and whether your channel tells stories or analyzes entities.**

### The Framework

| Your Subject | Your Format | Best Choice | PnL vs Alternative |
|-------------|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Non-famous person | Storytelling | **Mystery description** | **+90%** vs naming |
| Non-famous company | Any | **Mystery description** | **+60%** vs naming |
| Famous person | Commentary/Analysis | **Name-drop** | **+40%** vs description |
| Famous company | Commentary/Analysis | **Name-drop** | **+30%** vs description |
| Any person | Any | **AVOID Name+Explanation** | **-25%** vs either A or B |

### Evidence

**Mystery description of non-famous = highest-performing:**
- "The 10-Year-Old Who Hacked The FBI" (21M) -- ZackDFilms
- "Are Paternoster Elevators Actually Safe" (22M) -- ZackDFilms
- "How This Man Used A Dumb Government Program to Make $3,000" (2.9M) -- How Money Works
- "This woman makes a billion dollars a year" (584K) -- Nas Daily

**Name-drop famous = highest in commentary:**
- "MrBeast Explains The YouTube Algorithm" (4M) -- MagnatesMedia (42x channel avg)
- "How Did Trump DEAL with China?" (343K) -- Econ Explained (5.6x channel avg)
- "Why Bill Gates Has More Invested In Apple" (1M) -- How Money Works

**Name+Explanation = weakest (eliminates both curiosity and recognition):**
- "Dr. Kurt Richtor at John Hopkins" (8K) -- Codie Sanchez (vs channel avg 33K)
- "Who Was Alfred Marshall?" (33K) -- Econ Explained (below avg)

**Decision:** If the person/company is unknown to viewers, describe them by their most intriguing attribute ("the man who...", "this company..."). If famous, just name them. Never do both ("John Smith, a professor at MIT who studies X") -- it kills both the curiosity gap AND the recognition punch.

## 7. Question Hook vs Statement Hook (+19%)

**Questions create information gaps. They win for 6 of 10 creators.**

| Creator | Question Avg | Statement Avg | PnL |
|---------|-------------|--------------|-----|
| How Money Works | 1.05M | 585K | **+80%** |
| Fireship | 2.10M | 1.25M | **+68%** |
| Mark Tilbury | 4.25M | 2.88M | **+48%** |
| ZackDFilms | 9.9M | 7.8M | **+27%** |
| Veritasium | 13.7M | 11.0M | **+25%** |
| Erika Kullberg | 199K | 176K | **+13%** |
| Caleb Hammer | ~850K | 812K | ~even |
| Econ Explained | 54K | 67K | **-24%** |
| Humphrey Yang | 416K | 736K | **-77%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 64K | 163K | **-155%** |

**When questions win:** When they open a curiosity gap the viewer MUST close ("Why do computers suck at math?" 8.7M, "What happens if you cut the green rope?" 28M).

**When statements win:** When the statement IS the hook via name-drop or dramatic claim ("MrBeast Explains..." 4M, "Selling My 10 Ounce Silver Bar" 19M). Statements with famous names or dramatic action verbs beat questions.

**Decision:** Default to questions unless your hook contains a famous name, a dramatic action, or a specific result. "Why does X happen?" outperforms "X is happening." But "$X happened to [Famous Person]" outperforms "Did $X happen to [Famous Person]?"

## 8. Past Tense vs Present Tense (+20%)

**Stories told in past tense slightly outperform present-tense instruction for 4 of 7 creators.**

| Creator | Past Avg | Present Avg | PnL |
|---------|---------|------------|-----|
| Fireship | 2.22M | 1.39M | **+60%** |
| MagnatesMedia | 86K | 58K | **+48%** |
| Mark Tilbury | 3.45M | 2.77M | **+25%** |
| Econ Explained | 62K | 56K | **+11%** |
| Veritasium | 10.1M | 14.1M | **-40%** |
| Humphrey Yang | 187K | 280K | **-50%** |

**When past wins:** Narrative storytelling channels where the twist ending drives views. "He did X. Then Y happened. But then..." creates compounding tension.

**When present wins:** Live demonstrations (Veritasium cutting a rope, pouring shade balls) and practical finance instruction (Humphrey showing current investment strategies).

**Decision:** If telling a story, use past tense. If demonstrating something live or giving actionable advice for right now, use present tense. The past-tense narrative premium comes from the implicit promise of a resolution -- "what happened next?"

---

## 9. POV: You vs I vs They (Context-Dependent)

**No universal winner. Best POV depends entirely on format.**

| Format | Best POV | PnL vs Others | Example |
|--------|----------|--------------|---------|
| Instructional/advice | **"You"** (2nd person) | +20-140% vs "I" | Erika Kullberg: "You" 180K vs "I" 75K |
| Personal demonstration | **"I"** (1st person) | +25-75% vs "You" | Veritasium: "I" 18.2M vs "You" 10.4M |
| Historical narrative | **"He/She"** (3rd person) | +10-56% vs "You" | Fireship: "3rd" 2.0M vs "You" 1.28M |
| Finance education | **"You"** (default) | Varies | Humphrey Yang: "I" wins only for gold videos |

**Decision tree:**
- Teaching someone how to do something? → **"You"**
- Telling the story of a person/company? → **"He/She/They"**
- Showing yourself doing something dramatic? → **"I"**
- Sharing personal experience? → **"I"** if the experience is remarkable, **"You"** if it's relatable

## Decision Cheat Sheet

When you're scripting a Short and face a binary choice:

```
WRITING THE HOOK:
  Use a specific number?          YES if finance/results niche (+42%)
                                  NO if science/curiosity niche (-12%)

  Question or statement?          QUESTION by default (+19%)
                                  STATEMENT if you have a famous name or dramatic action

  Name the subject?               DESCRIBE if unknown ("This man who...") (+90%)
                                  NAME if famous ("Elon Musk just...") (+40%)
                                  NEVER both ("John Smith, a professor at...")

  Positive or negative framing?   POSITIVE by default (+55%)
                                  NEGATIVE only if your brand = warnings/danger

STRUCTURING THE CONTENT:
  Single story or listicle?       SINGLE STORY always (+96%)
                                  Only exception: pure curation ("5 books I read")

  Dialogue or monologue?          DIALOGUE if you can do skits/demos (+25%)
                                  MONOLOGUE if your voice IS the brand

  Past or present tense?          PAST if telling a story (+20%)
                                  PRESENT if demonstrating live

  Cite sources?                   NO by default (+37%)
                                  Only cite if the source becomes a CHARACTER

CHOOSING YOUR POV:
  "You" → teaching/advice
  "I"   → personal demonstration/experience
  "They"→ historical narrative/story
```
