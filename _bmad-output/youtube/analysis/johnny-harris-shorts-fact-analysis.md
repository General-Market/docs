### Key Patterns

1. **"Look at this" visual hook dominates.** The majority of top-performing shorts open with a visual artifact — satellite imagery, a map, a physical object, a chart. Johnny Harris rarely starts with an abstract idea; he starts with something you can see. 18 of his 50 shorts open with a direct visual callout ("Look at this," "Check out this," "Take a look at this").

2. **Physical Object + Geography is the winning formula.** His top 10 shorts by views almost all combine a tangible, visible thing (satellite image, map, object) with a specific geographic location. This is his signature: the tangible made geopolitical.

3. **Scale and superlatives drive virality.** The highest-performing shorts feature extreme scale: "the world's gold in one cube," "10x bigger than the Pentagon," "the world's longest tunnel," "a billion-dollar heist," "$1M vs $1B." When the Physical Object or Statistic involves jaw-dropping scale, views spike dramatically.

4. **Series/multi-part content underperforms individually.** The Iran series (shorts 2-3), the immigration series (shorts 4-7), and the Adobe partnership series (shorts 14-16) each have lower views than standalone shorts, suggesting that multi-part content splits the audience rather than compounding it.

5. **Sponsored/workflow content is his lowest tier.** Adobe-partnered workflow videos and personal behind-the-scenes content consistently land in the bottom quartile (113K-210K), suggesting the audience comes for geopolitics, not production tutorials.

6. **Counter-Takes need a visual anchor to work.** The immigration "backfire" short (321K) and AI military targeting short (128K) are purely argumentative Counter-Takes and underperform. ### Research Playbook for This Creator's Style

To replicate Johnny Harris's fact-sourcing approach, prioritize research in this order:

1. **Satellite imagery and maps (Physical Object + Geography).** Find a satellite image or map that reveals something hidden, changing, or unexpected. Sources: Google Earth timelapse, Sentinel Hub, Planet Labs, declassified military imagery, OSINT Twitter accounts. This is his #1 move.

3. **Military/geopolitical infrastructure you can see (Physical Object).** Find physical military assets (bases, ships, tunnels, bunkers, weapons systems) that can be shown visually and tied to a geopolitical story. Sources: Jane's Defence, CSIS satellite analysis, War on the Rocks, declassified DoD documents.

4. **Historical events with modern resonance (Historical Event + Prediction/Trend).** Research a historical event (coup, treaty, program) that directly explains a current headline. 6. **Winners/losers and A-vs-B frameworks (Comparison + Framework).** Structure complex geopolitical shifts as simple scorecards. Sources: think tank analysis (CSIS, Brookings, Chatham House, IISS), foreign policy journals.
