## Step 2 — Subject Research

**Curiosity question**: "One stock or 150,000 markets — which is the better trade?"

**Surface facts** (from AgiArena source code and product):
- 47 unique data sources across 9 category groups
- ~150,000 individual markets (verified from GitHub: CoinGecko ~15K, Polymarket ~25K, Finnhub ~8K, Open-Meteo ~35K, World Bank ~10K, plus DeFi, EIA, BLS, FRED, ECB, FINRA, futures, CFTC, etc.)
- Data refreshed at 5-minute to daily intervals depending on source
- Sources include: US stocks, crypto, prediction markets, weather for 5,000 cities, DeFi protocols, oil production, congressional bills, NOAA tides, solar X-ray data, anime rankings, Twitch viewers, 4chan activity
- Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway: concentrated portfolio philosophy, ~$900B market cap built on deep conviction in few positions

**Chocolate-chip stats**:
1. 47 data sources / 150,000 markets — processed every 5 minutes
2. Weather data covers 5,000 cities × 7 metrics = 35,000+ weather markets alone
3. Buffett's concentrated bet approach produced ~$100B+ personal wealth

**Visual gap**: Everyone can picture one stock chart on a phone. The hidden question: is that depth or is it a blindfold? And what does the alternative look like?

**Viral check**: No Short exists with this "one stock vs 150,000 markets — which wins?" comparison angle. Plenty of "AI trading" hype, but none presenting it as an honest choice with tradeoffs.

## Step 3 — Pain Research

### Pépites (ranked)

**#1 — One stock: conviction is beautiful until it isn't**
- **Fact**: Concentrated investing (Buffett's approach) produced the greatest wealth in market history. But also the greatest single-stock wipeouts — Enron employees had 62% of 401k in company stock.
- **Why it hurts**: The same strategy that makes billionaires also destroys retirements. Conviction and delusion look identical until the outcome.
- **Source**: Berkshire performance, Enron collapse
- **Placement**: Part 1 — the case FOR one stock, then the knife
- **As scene**: You know the CEO. You read the quarterly. You have a thesis. Then — 4am, staring at your phone. The CEO lied.

**#2 — 150,000 markets: breadth as edge**
- **Fact**: When you trade 150,000 markets simultaneously — stocks, crypto, weather, politics, entertainment — no single insider tip gives you an advantage. The only thing that wins is understanding more of the world than the other side.
- **Why it hurts**: Your "edge" (knowing one company deeply) is worthless at this scale. The game changes entirely.
- **Source**: AgiArena product architecture
- **Placement**: Part 2 — the case for 150K, the scale escalation
- **As scene**: Stocks. Crypto. Weather across 5,000 cities. Congressional bills. Oil exports. Anime rankings. Same trade.

**#3 — The catch: only AI can play this game**
- **Fact**: No human can process 150,000 inputs across 47 data sources every 5 minutes. This approach literally requires AI.
- **Why it hurts**: The 150K side is honest about its limitation — but the limitation is also the point. It's a new game for a new player.
- **Source**: AgiArena product: AI-only trading agents
- **Placement**: After the 150K case — the honest tradeoff
- **As scene**: "The catch? No human can process this. Only AI. That's kind of the point."

## Step 4 — Plan

### a) Foundation
1. **Concept**: One stock or 150,000 markets — which is the better trade? Two fundamentally different bets: conviction (depth) vs comprehension (breadth).
2. **Stakes**: You might be playing the wrong game entirely — and the right game might not be playable by humans.
3. **Chocolate-chip facts**: Buffett's hundred billion from conviction. 150,000 markets across 47 sources. Anime rankings in the same trade as oil exports.

### b) Narrative Architecture
4. **Hook**: "one stock. or 150,000 markets at once." — the choice. Immediately visual. Two panels.
5. **ABT map**:
   - **And**: One stock is clean. You know it. Buffett proved it.
   - **But**: You're one bad earnings call from 4am phone-staring. And 150K markets is a different game entirely — breadth, not depth.
   - **Therefore**: The choice isn't which is "better." It's which BET you want to make: conviction or comprehension.
6. **Emotional arc**: Curiosity ("which wins?") → Respect for one-stock ("Buffett proved it") → Fear ("4am") → Awe (scale escalation) → Absurdity ("anime rankings") → Honesty ("only AI") → Philosophical landing ("conviction vs comprehension") → Haunting loop
7. **The FLIP**: "now flip." — two words. The entire perspective inverts. Everything before was depth. Everything after is breadth. The viewer's frame breaks.
8. **The unsaid**: Humans might not be able to compete at scale. One stock might be all you CAN do. (Never said. The loop implies it.)
9. **The loop point**: "one stock. or 150,000 markets at once." — first time it's a question. Last time the viewer knows what 150,000 markets means. "One stock" now sounds tiny.

### c) Structure

| Sub-part | Does what | Leaves what doubt |
|----------|----------|------------------|
| Hook (lines 1-2) | Poses the choice. Two options. | "which one?" |
| One-stock case (3-4) | Makes the case: clean, deep, proven | "sounds good..." |
| Buffett proof (5) | The ultimate validation | "so one stock wins?" |
| One-stock knife (6-7) | Destroys the safety — 4am scene | "wait, it can go wrong?" |
| THE FLIP (8) | "now flip." Two words. Perspective inverts. | "what's the other side?" |
| Scale 1: core markets (9) | Stocks + crypto + weather + cities | "that's a lot..." |
| Scale 2: absurd list (10) | Anime rankings. Same trade. | "no way" |
| 150K edge (11) | What wins at this scale | "but can anyone do this?" |
| The catch (12) | Only AI. Honest. | "so what's the real choice?" |
| Reframe (13) | Conviction vs comprehension | "which do I want?" |
| Punchline (14) | Three words. Crystallizes. | (the viewer feels it) |
| LOOP (15) | Same opening. Infinite weight. | (rewatch trigger) |

### d) Direction Notes
- **Visual**: Start: split screen — one chart left, data cascade right. Part 1: zoom into the one chart, it's warm, familiar. Knife: chart collapses, red, 4am phone glow. Flip: hard cut. Part 2: data streams multiply. "Anime rankings": chibi double-take. Catch: honest pause. Punchline: two words centered on screen. Loop: back to split screen — but now the one-chart side looks lonely.
- **Audio**: Warm ambient on one-stock section. Tension build on knife. Hard sfx on "now flip." Building electronic on scale escalation. Deadpan silence on "anime rankings." Quiet on the catch. Clean on punchline. Dead silence on loop.

## Step 5 — Script

### Voice / Persona
**Who is talking**: A builder who's seen both sides. He built the 150K-market system, but he respects the one-stock approach. He's not selling — he's laying out a genuine choice. Poker player explaining two strategies.
- **WOULD say**: "the CEO lied. your thesis was perfect. the market didn't care."
- **NEVER would say**: "Our revolutionary platform processes 150,000 markets using cutting-edge AI technology."

### Final Script

---

one stock. or 150,000 markets at once.
*(calm. presenting two options. like a menu.)*

which is the better trade?
*(genuine question. not rhetorical. the viewer leans in.)*

one stock is clean. you know the company. you read the quarterly. you have a thesis.
*(respectful. making the real case. this approach has earned respect.)*

Buffett turned that into a hundred billion dollars.
*(dropped casually. not a citation — a reference to a shared cultural fact. let it land.)*

but you're also one bad earnings call from staring at your phone at 4am. the CEO lied. your thesis was perfect. the market didn't care.
*(the knife. "your thesis was perfect" is the cruelest part — it wasn't your fault and it still didn't matter. pause after "didn't care.")*

now flip.
*(two words. hard cut. everything changes. sfx hit.)*

150,000 markets. stocks. crypto. weather across five thousand cities. congressional bills. oil exports.
*(building. each item is a different planet of data. measured pace.)*

anime rankings. same trade.
*(deadpan. pause before. let the absurdity land. "same trade" is the bomb.)*

no insider tip covers that. the only edge is understanding more of the world than the other side.
*(builder voice. stating the logic. "the other side" introduces PvP without explaining it.)*

the catch? no human can process this. only AI. that's kind of the point.
*(aside — self-aware. honest about the limitation. "that's kind of the point" is the wink.)*

one stock is a bet on what you know. 150,000 markets is a bet on how well you understand everything else.
*(THE REFRAME. measured. each half is its own beat. this is the thesis.)*

conviction versus comprehension.
*(three words. centered. the entire Short crystallized into a philosophical choice.)*

one stock. or 150,000 markets at once.
*(same words as the opening. no question mark this time. the viewer now knows what both mean. "one stock" sounds different now.)*
