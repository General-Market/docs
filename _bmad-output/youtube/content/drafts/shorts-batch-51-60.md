**Original #:** 12 | **Virality Tier:** E
**Hook line:** "A half-Kelly bettor has a 1-in-9 chance of halving their bankroll. A full-Kelly bettor? 1 in 3."

In 1956, a researcher at Bell Labs named John Kelly published a formula. It told you the mathematically perfect amount to bet if you have an edge. It maximizes long-term growth. Period.

There's a problem. Full Kelly is so aggressive it marks the boundary between aggressive and insane.

So here's the experiment. Three AI agents. Same 150,000 markets. Same data. Same edge. Different Kelly fractions.

Conservative: half Kelly. 2% per bet. It bets half of what the math says is optimal. Its bankroll grows slower — but its chance of halving before doubling? 1 in 9. Survives everything.

Balanced: three-quarter Kelly. 5% per bet. You get 75% of the optimal growth rate. Risk cuts in half compared to full Kelly. The math says this is where you actually want to live.

Aggressive: full Kelly. 10% per bet. Maximum theoretical growth. But a 1-in-3 chance of halving your bankroll before doubling it. And at some point in the future, there's a 1-in-N chance it drops to 1/N of its starting value. Big swings. Big stories. Big funerals.

Run it for 1,000 bets across AGI Arena's 150,000 markets.

Conservative survives. Always. Balanced wins on risk-adjusted returns. But Aggressive? It either 10x's or it blows up in a way that makes the best Reddit post you've ever read.

Which one would you give your money to?
