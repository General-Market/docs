## PROPOSITION 1 — Humphrey Yang (8/10)

**Original**: Should You Pay Off Your Mortgage Early or Invest Instead? (78K)

**Original script**:
> Should you pay off your mortgage early or invest instead? Everyone seems to have an opinion on this, but the math changes a lot based on one number, and that is your interest rate. If you got your mortgage back when interest rates were 3% mathematically, you're going to be a lot better off investing instead of paying off your mortgage. That's because the S&P 500 averages about 8% annually. So, if you paid off your mortgage early, you would get a tax-free and risk-free 3%. But if you took your extra dollars and invested them, you would roughly get around seven to eight% after tax, which is higher than the 3%. But at a six to 7% mortgage interest rate, the decision gets a lot tougher because a tax-free and risk-free 6% return is amazing because even if you did invest, not every year is going to return positively. And there's some inherent risk when you take your money and invest it. So if you have a mortgage rate of 6 to 7%, that's when you may want to consider paying off your mortgage early instead of investing. Mathematically speaking, the lower your interest rate, the better it is to invest. And the longer your time horizon on a mortgage, the better it is to invest because your money is compounding harder by investing. But paying off a house is not always a financial decision. So, what would you do? Let me know in the comments.

**Word-swap version**:
> Should you trade one stock or 150,000 markets at once? Everyone seems to have an opinion on this, but the answer changes a lot based on one thing, and that is your edge. If you've got deep knowledge of one company, read the quarterly, have a thesis, you're going to be a lot better off trading a single stock. That's because Warren Buffett averaged about 20% annually doing exactly that. So, if you concentrated on one name, you would get clean, high-conviction returns. Buffett turned that into a hundred billion dollars. But at the level of real-world uncertainty, the decision gets a lot tougher because one bad earnings call, the CEO lied, you're staring at your phone at 4am, your thesis was perfect, the market didn't care. And there's some inherent risk when you put everything into one name. So if you could trade 150,000 markets instead — stocks, crypto, weather across 5,000 cities, congressional bills, oil exports, anime rankings, same trade — that's when you may want to consider breadth instead of depth. No insider tip covers all of that. The only edge is understanding more of the world than the other side. And the wider your coverage across markets, the harder your edge compounds because you're capturing signal everywhere. But here's the catch: no human can process 150,000 markets. Only AI can. And that's kind of the point. One stock is a bet on what you know. 150,000 markets is a bet on how well you understand everything else. Conviction versus comprehension. So, which would you trade?

**Why 8/10**: Opens with binary question, "everyone has an opinion but it depends on one variable," presents Side A with numbers, pivots with "but the decision gets tougher," transitions to Side B, builds the mathematical principle, lands with philosophical reframe. Rhythm, transitions, emotional arc all preserved beat for beat.
