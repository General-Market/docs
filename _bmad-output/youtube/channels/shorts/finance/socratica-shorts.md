## 2. Covered Calls - Option Strategy for Earning Income
**Channel:** Socratica | **Views:** 3K | **Date:** N/A | **ID:** ZYC-MSLEK_o
**Link:** https://youtube.com/shorts/ZYC-MSLEK_o

### Script:
[Music] The straddle and strangle are both strategies that combine two options. But options can also be paired with stocks themselves, opening up even more strategies. A classic strategy for stock owners is the covered call. It's an example of using options to generate income. Suppose you own 100 shares of stock. You plan on holding the stock for a while. You don't expect any big price jumps in the near term, which presents an opportunity to make some extra money. So, you sell a call option on the shares you already own. This means you collect a premium, the price you're paid for that option. In return, you agree to sell your stock at a set price if the option is exercised. What's your hope? That the stock stays below the strike price. If it does, the option expires worthless and you keep both the premium and your shares. What's the risk? If the stock shoots up past the strike, the option will likely be exercised. That means you have to sell your stock at the strike price, even if the market price is higher. Can you see why the name covered call is fitting? You're selling a call, but you're covered because you already own the shares.

### Script:
Meet the protective put, also known as the married put. Suppose you own 100 shares of a stock. You'd like to hold on to it, but you think there's a chance it might drop soon. To prepare for that possibility, you buy a put option on that same stock. If the stock price drops, the put increases in value, offsetting the losses on your shares. The put option is literally protecting your stock from downside risk, hence the name protective put. The payoff diagram for the protective put helps you visualize this strategy. The protective put recreates the behavior of a long call by combining other components. In finance, we call this a synthetic position. A synthetic asset is built from a collection of different parts in order to resemble another kind of asset.

## 5. Straddle - Stock Options Trading - Betting on Volatility
**Channel:** Socratica | **Views:** 4K | **Date:** N/A | **ID:** l9tUHwBl14k
**Link:** https://youtube.com/shorts/l9tUHwBl14k

### Script:
[Music] To build a straddle, you buy two options at the same time. A long call and a long put. Same strike, same expiration. Why would you do this? Because you think something big is going to happen. You just don't know which direction. Could be an earnings call. Could be an FDA ruling. You don't need to guess the outcome. You just need the stock to move. The straddle is a bet on volatility. Let's look at the payoff diagram. First, the long call by itself. You lose the premium if the stock drops, but if it rises past the strike, the payoff increases with no cap. Now, the long put. This one profits if the stock drops below the strike. If it rises, the most you lose is the premium. To get the straddles payoff, we add them together. The result is a V-shaped diagram. If the stock moves up, the call takes over. If the stock moves down, the put takes over. And if the stock stays at the strike, neither option is in the money. you lose both premiums. This shape straddles the strike price. That's where the name straddle comes from. But keep in mind, you paid for two options. The stock has to move far enough to cover both premiums before you're in the black. You're positioned on both sides, ready to profit if the stock makes a move in either direction.
