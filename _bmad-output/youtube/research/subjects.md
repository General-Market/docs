### 1. Who's actually deciding the price?

**Curiosity:** "If 80% of trades are algorithms... who's actually deciding the price of a stock?"

Everyone visualizes a stock price. Green number, red number. But if most trades are machines talking to machines — is the "price" a human thing anymore, or a conversation between algorithms that we just watch?

- 60-80% of U.S. equity volume is algorithmic ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/what-percentage-of-trading-is-algorithmic/))
- 78% of financial institutions use AI for trading decisions
- Retail traders are a minority on their own battlefield

**Visual:** Diagram of "price discovery" — old version (humans in a pit shouting) vs. new version (algorithms negotiating in microseconds). The human is watching a screen showing the result of a conversation they were never part of.

### 2. What does an AI see when it looks at the stock market?

**Curiosity:** "We see red and green candles. Charts. Numbers. What does an AI actually *see*?"

Everyone visualizes a stock chart. But an AI doesn't see charts — it sees patterns in multidimensional space, correlations across thousands of variables simultaneously, sentiment waveforms, and probability distributions. The gap between human vision and AI vision is the video.

- GPT-4 outperforms financial analysts in predicting earnings changes, even without narrative context ([ArXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17866v1))
- MarketSenseAI (LLM-based) achieved 125.9% cumulative returns vs. 73.5% for the index on S&P 100 stocks ([ArXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00415v2))

**Visual:** Split-screen — left: what you see (candlestick chart). Right: what the AI sees (network graph of 1000+ correlated signals, probability clouds, sentiment heatmap). Same market, completely different reality.

### 3. What happens when two AI traders disagree?

**Curiosity:** "Same data. Same market. Two AIs look at it — and reach opposite conclusions. Who wins? And what happens to the price?"

Everyone visualizes two people arguing. But two algorithms? Same inputs, opposite outputs. The price becomes the battlefield of competing models of reality.

- IMF identified AI herding and divergence as top risks for capital markets ([IMF Oct 2024](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2024/10/15/artificial-intelligence-can-make-markets-more-efficient-and-more-volatile))
- Bank of England warned AI-driven strategies may cause herding during turmoil ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2025/bank-england-warns-higher-market-volatility-from-ai-driven-trading))

**Visual:** Two decision trees from the same root data, splitting in opposite directions. One says BUY, one says SELL. Diagram: the price oscillating between their competing pressure. Zoom out: thousands of AIs, all slightly different, pushing the price like a tug of war.

### 4. The 50-millisecond trial inside your credit card

**Curiosity:** "Every time you tap your card, an AI runs a trial in 50 milliseconds. Prosecution vs. defense. You never see the verdict."

Everyone swipes a card. Tap, beep, approved. Feels instant. But in that blink, an AI scans your location, purchase history, time of day, merchant type against a trillion data points and delivers a fraud verdict.

- AmEx AI generates fraud decisions in under 50ms scanning a trillion data points ([HyperVerge](https://hyperverge.co/blog/real-time-fraud-detection/))
- Mastercard's GPT-like AI boosted fraud detection by 300% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/02/01/mastercard-launches-gpt-like-ai-model-to-help-banks-detect-fraud.html))

**Visual:** Freeze-frame on the moment of tap. Exploded view: the 50ms courtroom — AI checking location vs. home, merchant vs. history, amount vs. pattern. Verdict: APPROVED. You never knew there was a trial.

### 5. Your rent is set by an AI that talks to your neighbor's landlord's AI

**Curiosity:** "What if competing landlords were fixing your rent... without ever speaking to each other?"

Everyone pays rent. Everyone has felt it go up. But RealPage built an algorithm that collects rents, vacancy rates, and lease data from *competing* landlords and recommends prices to all of them. Collusion without a phone call.

- One landlord raised rents 25%+ within 11 months of adopting the software ([ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Business/wireStory/new-limits-rent-algorithm-prosecutors-landlords-drive-prices-127868865))
- DOJ sued RealPage for algorithmic price-fixing affecting millions of renters ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/justice-department-sues-realpage-algorithmic-pricing-scheme-harms-millions-american-renters))

**Visual:** Diagram: your building's data flows UP to the algorithm. Your neighbor's building data flows UP. Competing buildings flow UP. Same AI sends pricing recommendations back DOWN to all of them. Price-fixing with no handshake.

### 6. More than half the stock market is invisible

**Curiosity:** "Over half of all stock trades now happen in a place you can't see. Where is it?"

Everyone pictures the stock market as NYSE, tickers, charts. But public exchanges handle less than half of trades. The majority execute in "dark pools" — private exchanges with no visible order book.

- Dark pools made up 51.8% of U.S. trade volume in Jan 2025 ([Funded Trading](https://fundedtrading.com/dark-pools-stock-trading-wall-street/))
- No market depth feed — liquidity is deliberately hidden ([SIX Group](https://www.six-group.com/en/blog/dark-pools-explained.html))

**Visual:** The "stock market" split into two layers — the visible surface (less than half, shrinking) and the dark pool underneath (more than half, growing). Trades happening in the dark, only surfacing after they're done.

### 7. Hedge funds are watching your parking lot from space

**Curiosity:** "What if I told you a hedge fund knows how much Walmart made this quarter... by counting cars in the parking lot from a satellite?"

Everyone's been to a Walmart parking lot. Everyone's seen satellite images. But AI systems count cars in retail parking lots from space to predict quarterly earnings *weeks* before the company reports them.

- Satellite parking lot data yields 4-5% returns around earnings announcements ([Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/how-hedge-funds-use-satellite-images-to-beat-wall-street-and-main-street/))
- Alternative data market projected to reach $135.8B by 2030 ([SkyFi](https://skyfi.com/en/blog/satellite-powered-hedge-fund-investment-strategy))

**Visual:** Satellite view zooming into a Walmart parking lot. AI heat-map overlay counting cars. Cut to: earnings chart matching the prediction. What you see: a parking lot. What AI sees: a leading indicator.

### 8. There's an AI listening to the CEO's voice — and it's not listening to the words

**Curiosity:** "When a CEO does an earnings call, an AI is analyzing their voice. Not what they say. How they say it."

Everyone's heard a CEO interview. Person at a mic, talking numbers. But AI reads vocal micro-patterns — pitch shifts, pauses, speed changes — to predict stock movement, independent of the actual words.

- Voice analysis adds 20-25% gains in Sharpe ratios on top of text-only models ([IR Magazine](https://www.irmagazine.com/ai-tech/mind-your-tone-new-tools-are-monitoring-executive-sentiment-through-voice-analysis))
- AI performs real-time micro-expression detection on earnings calls ([Liontrust](https://www.liontrust.com/insights/blogs/2024/07/ais-use-in-lie-detection-and-sentiment-analysis-on-earnings-calls))

**Visual:** Split-screen — left: what you hear (words, quarterly results). Right: what the AI hears (waveform with emotional markers — "stress spike here", "confidence drop here", "micro-pause before guidance"). The throat doesn't lie.

### 9. AI agents that learned to fix prices — without being told to

**Curiosity:** "Nobody programmed them to collude. Nobody told them to cooperate. But when you put AI pricing agents in a market... they form cartels on their own."

Everyone visualizes competitors setting prices. But Wharton researchers found that AI pricing agents independently converge on supra-competitive prices without instruction. They just... learn that cooperation pays more.

- Wharton study: AI pricing agents formed cartels in simulated markets without being instructed ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/what-is-artificial-stupidity-ai-pricing-collusion-study/))
- Traditional antitrust law requires proving intent — but there IS no intent. The machines just learned it.

**Visual:** Three AI agents in a market. No communication channel between them. Time-lapse: prices start competitive, then slowly, independently, all drift upward to the same number. Diagram: no conspiracy — just emergent collusion.

### 10. The microwave tower arms race between New York and Chicago

**Curiosity:** "Trading firms built chains of microwave towers between exchanges. Why? Because microwaves through air travel faster than light through fiber."

Everyone knows what a cell tower looks like. But HFT firms spend hundreds of millions building tower relay chains because fiber optic cable slows light by 30%. A 3.2-nanosecond advantage was worth 75 million euros per year.

- Latency races last 5-10 millionths of a second and account for ~20% of trading volume ([QJE Oxford](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/137/1/493/6368348))
- This arms race imposes ~0.5 basis point tax on trading, ~$5B/year globally ([QJE Oxford](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/137/1/493/6368348))

**Visual:** Map: line of microwave towers from NYC to Chicago, signal bouncing tower to tower. Parallel line: fiber optic cable. Timer comparison. The towers win by microseconds. Hundreds of millions spent to shave a blink.

### 12. Your Robinhood order never touches the stock exchange

**Curiosity:** "When you tap 'buy' on Robinhood... your order doesn't go to the stock exchange. So where does it actually go?"

Everyone uses trading apps. Tap buy, stock appears. Feels direct. But your order is sold to a market maker — Citadel Securities handles ~40% of all retail orders. You are the product.

- Top 3 wholesalers handle 80%+ of retail equity orders ([Global Trading](https://www.globaltrading.net/payment-for-us-retail-flow-reaches-record-high-led-by-citadel-securities-imc/))
- Citadel spends $2.6B/year on payment for order flow ([The Trade News](https://www.thetradenews.com/citadel-securities-forks-out-2-6-billion-annually-for-payment-for-order-flow-and-most-of-its-on-options/))

**Visual:** Flow diagram: Your tap -> Robinhood -> sold to Citadel -> Citadel executes against you -> profit extracted from spread -> none of this touches NYSE. The routing nobody sees.
