# YouTube Short Script Framework

## Pipeline Overview

```
1. SUBJECT SELECTION → 2. SUBJECT RESEARCH → 3. PAIN RESEARCH → 4. PLAN → 5. SCRIPT
         ↓                      ↓                     ↓               ↓          ↓
   Pick the topic       Surface-level facts    Deep dive for the    Architecture  Dialogue
                        + verify no viral      personal pépite      (not script)  (after plan
                        Short exists           that HURTS the                     approved)
                                               viewer personally
```

Each step must be COMPLETED before moving to the next.

---

## 1. Subject Selection Criteria
- a/ In my niche (AGI Finance, CEO of agiarena.org)
- b/ No one made a video but everyone can visualize what it is
- c/ Have things to say about it

### Subject DNA
- **Hidden mechanics**: Reveal the invisible layer behind something everyone interacts with daily
- **Curiosity-driven**: Frame as "have you ever wondered..." not "did you know..."
- **Visual gap**: Everyone can picture the surface — the hidden layer underneath is the revelation
- **Not a lecture**: The Short should WONDER out loud, not teach. Builder curiosity, not professor energy
- **Diagrammable**: If you can't draw the "before/after" — the subject is too abstract

### Subject bank
- Maintain a running list in `youtube/subjects.md`
- Each subject needs: curiosity question, why it's visual, the hidden layer, 1-2 verified facts with sources, visual concept

---

## 2. Subject Research (surface level)
- Research trending topics, facts, data in the niche
- Find 1-2 chocolate-chip facts (shocking stats)
- Verify no viral Short already exists on this exact angle

---

## 3. Pain Research (deep dive) — THE CRITICAL STEP

This is where the Short gets its soul. Without it: "huh, neat." With it: "holy shit, this is about ME."

### What to search for
- **Personal cost**: How does this directly cost the viewer money, time, opportunity? Find the DOLLAR AMOUNT.
- **It's happening NOW**: Not hypothetical. IS happening, right now, on autopilot.
- **Invisible extraction**: Something taken without knowledge — no notification, no line item, no consent.
- **Broken trust**: Something the viewer believed was protecting them that is actually hurting them.
- **Scale of disconnect**: Numbers showing the gap between what the viewer thinks vs what's actually happening.
- **Institutional admission**: An insider saying the quiet part out loud (the betrayal source — the institution that PROFITS is the one admitting it's broken).

### What makes a good pépite
- It's about the VIEWER — THEIR money, THEIR retirement, THEIR trade
- It triggers: "wait, this is happening to me RIGHT NOW?"
- Verifiable with a credible source (bank, regulator, academic paper — not a blog)
- Can be said in ONE sentence
- After hearing it, the viewer CANNOT unsee it

### Output of this step
A ranked list of pépites per concept, each with:
- The fact (one sentence)
- Why it hurts personally
- Source (prioritize betrayal sources — show logo/name ON SCREEN)
- Where it could go in the Short (hook? knife? proof?)

### The hook IS the pain
The most devastating pépite becomes the hook. Don't separate "hook" from "stakes."

### Every fact must be a scene
A fact is not a statistic — it's an image. If the viewer can't SEE it, rewrite it.
- BAD: "Algorithmic trading has increased significantly over the past two decades"
- GOOD: "Goldman had 600 traders in 2000. Today — two. The room is empty."
Convert every pépite into something visual before using it.

## 4. Plan Format (before scripting)

A plan is the ARCHITECTURE, not the script. No dialogue. Defines what each part DOES, not what it SAYS.

### Plan must contain:

**a) Foundation (from Pain Research)**
1. **Concept** — One-line pitch
2. **Stakes/Enjeux** — The pépite. One sentence that hurts.
3. **Chocolate-chip facts** — 1-2 facts with source, validated in Pain Research.

**b) Narrative Architecture**
4. **Hook** — The most painful pépite IS the hook. Type (number / question / intriguing fact) follows naturally.
5. **ABT map** — One line each for And, But, Therefore
6. **Emotional arc** — The journey in one line (e.g. False safety → Violation → Disgust → Relief → Curiosity)
7. **The FLIP sentence** — The single line where the viewer's assumption breaks. This line gets the most production emphasis (sfx, visual change, pause before it). Not a gradual turn — a violent inversion.
8. **The unsaid** — Identify the most devastating implication and do NOT say it. Let the viewer arrive there alone. The script must leave a wound open.
9. **The loop point** — The last line must make the first line mean something different on second viewing.

**c) Structure**
10. **Parts and sub-parts** — Table format:

| Sub-part | Does what (NOT dialogue) | Leaves what doubt |
|----------|--------------------------|-------------------|

11. **Transitions** — How each part launches into the next (the bridge idea, not a written line)

**d) Direction notes**
12. **Visual direction** — Key visual concept per part
13. **Audio direction** — Music/SFX mood per part

---

## 5. Script (after plan approved)

### Voice / Persona
- Define WHO is talking before writing a single line. A friend at a bar? A whistleblower? A curious kid?
- Write 1 example line the persona WOULD say and 1 they NEVER would
- Without this, AI defaults to "enthusiastic YouTube host" — the most generic, skippable tone on the platform

### Structure Rules
- 3 Parts, with sub-parts that launch into each other
- Every sentence leaves a doubt → next sentence answers it BUT opens a new one → linked until the end
- Small sentences — extreme brevity
- Facts = chocolate chips in a cookie. 1-2 sentences max of pure facts, embedded in the story

### Rhythm Rules
- Never write 3 sentences of similar length in a row. Vary deliberately: Long. Short. Short. Long — — — short. Punch.
- Rhythm IS emotion. Compression = tension. Expansion = relief. Use sentence length as a musical tool.
- Read it out loud. If it sounds metronomic, rewrite it.

### Tone Rules
- NOT a text — a SPEECH. Talking to the viewer.
- Mark intent/tone per line: interrogative? assurance? whisper? excitement?
- Include human quirks (pauses, self-corrections, asides)
- Alternate emotions: joy, hate, surprise, fear, excitement
- ABT: the "Therefore" is shown on screen as proof or chibi emotion

### Production Notes
- **Character**: Chibi with different emotions (not face-to-camera)
- **Format**: YouTube Short (60s to 3min)
- Prepare image, music, sfx (sfx always on something visual), and visual plan alongside script

### Error Avoidance
- NO word repetitions / redundancy / complex words — go to essential
- Play on viewer emotions — take them by surprise vs other YouTube content
