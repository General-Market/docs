# The Business Model

## We expand the market, we don't slice it

Every venue caps the number of markets it can run by the price of liquidity. A paid market maker costs upward of $1M to seed a single market, burned whether or not it trades. So the venue can afford a few thousand markets, and no more. The number of markets that can *exist* is set by the cost of liquidity.

General prices a whole block at once. Because the block is netted, the collateral it requires collapses far below the naive sum, and the marginal cost of one more market falls to near zero. The cap lifts. Markets that were never economical — thin, niche, long-tail — become viable. We do not take a slice of the existing market. We expand it toward a billion markets.

> Liquidity per market bankrupts. Liquidity per block scales.

## How we make money

A flat take on every settled pool. No order book to maintain, no maker to pay, no inventory to hedge.

- **Primary:** a fee on settled volume, the same order of magnitude as a Binance perp — ~0.05% of volume, taken from the pool at resolution.
- **Marginal cost of a new market:** near zero — two users make a market, so supply grows without us buying it.

One mechanism drives the model. Everything else is downstream of settled volume.

## Unit economics

The economics are unusual because the cost of supply is near zero. A market that two people use can run profitably for years.

> *Modeled from stated assumptions — a Binance-perp-level fee, near-zero marginal market cost. Live cohort data will sharpen these as it accrues.*

| Metric | Today (testnet) | At scale (target) |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | ~0.05% of volume | ~0.05% of volume |
| Revenue source | Pre-revenue | Settled volume |
| Marginal cost per new market | ~$0 | ~$0 |
| Gross margin | No revenue yet | 85%+ — infra-only cost of goods |

## Revenue projections

Revenue scales with one variable: how fast settled volume ramps. The fee is fixed and thin; distribution is the lever.

> *Modeled from stated assumptions. Not yet validated by live revenue.*

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** — organic only | $0.1M | $0.6M | $1.8M |
| **Base** — distribution from month 6 | $0.3M | $1.5M | $6M |
| **Aggressive** — a venue integrates behind us | $0.6M | $5M | $20M+ |

The differentiator between scenarios is distribution, not technology. The machine is already built.

## Cost structure

Low and flat. Three production VPS carry the whole system today.

| Category | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering payroll | ~70% | Two to three hires post-raise |
| Infrastructure (RPC, oracles, hosting) | ~10% | Three VPS, BLS quorum — cheap to run |
| Legal / regulatory | ~10% | Jurisdictional analysis, offshore-first wrapper |
| Growth | ~10% | Begins month 6, into data-native channels |

## Path to profitability

The burn is small and the marginal cost of supply is near zero, so breakeven is a question of distribution, not unit economics. In the base case, settled volume crosses the level that covers the team inside the 18-month window. We could choose profitability early. We choose growth instead, because the market we are expanding is far larger than the one we already serve.
