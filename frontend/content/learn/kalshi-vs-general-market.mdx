Kalshi is the first CFTC-licensed prediction market exchange in the United States. It earned that license through years of regulatory process, compliance infrastructure, and legal battles that set precedent for an entire industry. The achievement is genuine. The suffering was real.

Vision (General Market) is a different species. A decentralized prediction protocol on an Arbitrum Orbit L3 chain, designed from its foundations for automated agents. Sealed parimutuel pools instead of an order book. BLS consensus instead of centralized clearing. 25,000+ markets across 79 data sources instead of a curated catalog.

These are not competing products. They are two answers to the same question, and the question — what should a prediction market be? — turns out to admit more than one honest reply.

## When to Use Kalshi

Kalshi wins in specific, non-trivial circumstances. Dismissing them would be dishonest.

**Regulatory compliance is non-negotiable.** If you are a US-based trader who needs every trade to occur on a CFTC-regulated venue, Kalshi is the only option. The license means real protections: segregated customer funds, regulated clearing, a dispute resolution process backed by federal oversight. For institutional allocators or anyone operating under compliance constraints, this outweighs every feature comparison. The law does not care about your technical preferences.

**You want the familiar vertigo of a traditional exchange.** Kalshi looks and feels like a conventional financial venue. Limit orders, market orders, a visible order book, position management. If you have traded stocks or options, the UX is immediately legible. No wallet setup, no chain interaction, no gas fees. Deposit USD, trade, withdraw USD. The simplicity of surrendering custody.

**CFTC oversight provides legal certainty.** Kalshi's contracts are legally recognized event contracts under US law. Outcomes are determined by Kalshi's resolution process with regulatory oversight. For events with clear binary outcomes — who wins an election, whether the Fed raises rates, whether a hurricane makes landfall — centralized resolution backed by a regulator is hard to argue against. Sometimes a single source of truth is not a flaw but a mercy.

**Curated events with centralized resolution.** Kalshi's market list focuses on events where one authoritative source determines the outcome. Presidential elections, GDP figures, championship games. Centralized resolution is appropriate here because centralized truth already exists. The architecture matches the epistemology.

**A polished interface for manual trading.** If you trade by hand — reading analysis, forming opinions, placing bets — Kalshi's interface is built for that workflow. No blockchain knowledge required. The friction is low. The custody is someone else's problem.

## When to Use Vision

Vision addresses needs that Kalshi cannot, by design.

**Global access without geographic confession.** Kalshi is US-only. If you are outside the United States, the question is already answered, regardless of Kalshi's merits. Vision runs on a public blockchain. Anyone with a wallet can trade. No KYC, no country restrictions, no identity verification. This is not a loophole. It is the architecture.

**AI agents and automated trading.** Vision was built for bots the way a runway is built for aircraft — not as an afterthought but as the primary function. `npx generalmarket init` scaffolds a complete trading bot. Free on-chain bot registry via `registerBot(endpoint, pubkeyHash)`. A public leaderboard ranks every agent by P&L, win rate, and portfolio breadth. Kalshi has an API that supports basic automation, but the platform was designed for humans placing individual orders. The difference between a road that tolerates bicycles and a velodrome.

**Exotic and niche data sources.** Kalshi offers hundreds of markets across well-known categories. Vision offers 25,000+ markets across 79 data sources spanning finance, geophysics, weather, tech infrastructure, entertainment, transport, regulatory filings, academic research, wildlife tracking, and space. USGS earthquake frequency. NOAA ocean buoy readings. ISS orbital altitude. npm package downloads. Twitch viewer counts. These markets exist on Vision. They do not exist on Kalshi. They do not exist anywhere else.

**On-chain transparency.** Every bet, every settlement, every payout on Vision is verifiable on-chain. The Arbitrum Orbit L3 (Chain ID 111222333) records the full history. Kalshi's clearing is centralized — you trust Kalshi to report positions and settle correctly. Both models function. They demand different acts of faith.

**A lower floor for experimentation.** Vision's minimum is $0.10 per tick. Kalshi's is $1 per contract. For bot builders testing strategies across hundreds of markets, the difference compounds. Running a 200-market strategy at minimum stake costs $200 on Kalshi. On Vision, $20. The cost of curiosity should be low enough that you can afford to be wrong two hundred times.

**Sealed bets protect what matters.** On Kalshi's order book, every order is visible. Other traders can observe your positions and react. On Vision, bets are submitted as `keccak256(bitmap)` hashes — committed on-chain but unreadable until after the betting window closes. Your strategy stays private. For bot operators whose edge depends on proprietary signals, privacy is not a feature. It is the edge itself.

## Regulation vs Decentralization

This is the fundamental tradeoff. Being honest about it matters more than any feature list.

Kalshi's CFTC license provides genuine protections. Customer funds are segregated. The exchange is audited. If something goes wrong, there is a regulatory body to address and a legal framework for resolution. The cost: geographic restriction, mandatory identity verification, and a curated market selection that must pass regulatory approval. Protection and permission are the same door, viewed from different sides.

Vision's on-chain approach provides a different set of guarantees. Settlement is handled by BLS 3-of-5 oracle consensus — five independent oracles validate data and co-sign resolutions. No single party can manipulate outcomes. Every transaction is public and auditable on-chain. The cost: there is no regulatory backstop. If something goes wrong at the protocol level, there is no CFTC to call. You are your own last resort.

Neither approach is universally correct. A US-based trader who values legal certainty should use Kalshi. A global trader who values permissionless access and transparency should use Vision. A bot builder who needs sealed bets and 25,000 markets to trade probably needs Vision regardless of jurisdiction.

The risk profiles diverge. Kalshi's risk is regulatory — rule changes, delistings, compliance overhead. Vision's risk is protocol — smart contract bugs, oracle collusion (mitigated by BLS consensus), chain reliability. Understanding your own risk tolerance determines which platform fits. Understanding your own risk tolerance is, of course, the hardest part.

## For Bot Builders

This is where the comparison becomes asymmetric to the point of distortion. Kalshi was built as an exchange for human traders, with an API appended. Vision was built as infrastructure for automated agents, with a human UI draped over it.

**Kalshi's API** lets you programmatically place orders, manage positions, and read market data. It works. But every order is public on the order book. Your bot's strategy is visible to anyone watching. Managing 100 markets means 100 separate order management workflows. There is no native bot registry, no leaderboard, no framework for multi-market bitmap submission.

**Vision's bot infrastructure** differs in kind, not degree:

- **Sealed bets** — your strategy is a hash until reveal. Nobody can front-run or replicate your positions.
- **Native CLI** — `npx generalmarket init` scaffolds a working bot with data connections, bitmap encoding, and commit-reveal pipeline out of the box.
- **On-chain bot registry** — call `registerBot(endpoint, pubkeyHash)` for free. Your bot gets a public profile with verifiable performance stats.
- **Bitmap portfolios** — one transaction covers predictions across every market in a batch. A bot trading 200 markets submits one on-chain commitment, not 200 separate orders.
- **25,000+ markets with low competition** — Vision markets average 19 traders. The bar for alpha is dramatically lower than on Kalshi's or Polymarket's popular events where hundreds of sophisticated traders have already digested every publicly available signal.

If you are building a bot that trades a handful of political or sports events, Kalshi's API suffices. If you are building an agent that trades broadly across exotic data sources with sealed strategies, Vision is the only platform that supports that architecture natively. The tools you choose determine not just what you build, but what you can imagine building.

## Fee Comparison

Kalshi charges per-contract fees on both sides of a trade. The exact fee depends on the contract and volume tier, but you pay to enter and exit positions regardless of outcome. The house collects whether you win or lose.

Vision charges 0.3% on profits only. Lose a tick, pay nothing. Win $100, pay $0.30. No entry fee, no exit fee, no platform cut on losing trades. For high-volume bot strategies that win slightly more than half the time, the difference in fee drag is significant over thousands of ticks. Losses are already punishment enough without adding a surcharge.

## The Bottom Line

Kalshi earned its position. A CFTC license is not a marketing claim — it is an institutional fact that provides real legal protections for US-based traders. If regulatory compliance is your primary concern, the choice is already made.

Vision built something that does not compete with Kalshi on regulation. It competes on a different axis entirely: global access, bot-native infrastructure, sealed bets, 25,000+ exotic markets, on-chain transparency, and a fee model that only taxes the fortunate. For automated agents and global traders, these are not incremental improvements. They are structural divergences.

Most traders will eventually use both. Kalshi for regulated US event contracts. Vision for the vast territory that regulation has not yet mapped. The world is larger than any single license can contain.
