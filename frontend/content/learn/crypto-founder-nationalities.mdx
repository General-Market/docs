There's something perfectly absurd about a global, decentralized, borderless technology that reproduces the exact geographic biases of traditional finance. American VCs fund American founders. European funds prefer European deals. Everyone ignores everyone else. The blockchain doesn't care about passports. The capital does.

But this particular absurdity has a price tag. And the price tag is 1.6x of lost returns.

## Key Takeaways

- **All-US teams** reach ATH quickly (88 days) but return only 2.1x. The market prices American founders correctly because every fund sees the same pipeline.
- **Mixed-nationality teams** (Has EU, Has CN) return **3.7x** — the highest multiplier. Geographic diversity breaks the mimetic cycle.
- **All-CN teams** are the fastest to ATH (70 days, 3.0x) but the founder pool is shrinking post-regulation.
- **Regional composition** has barely moved in five years. Americas ~50%, Europe ~25%, Asia ~20%. The market is frozen.
- The **Middle East** grew from 1% to 4% of Top 100 founders — a region almost no crypto VC covers.

## 1. The American Paradox

All-US teams reach ATH in 88 days with a 2.1x multiplier. The second-fastest speed. The lowest return.

There is no mystery here. The American founder is the default model for every crypto fund in the Valley, in New York, in London. Stanford. Y Combinator. The same incubators, the same signal network, the same conferences. When a founder raises from a16z, the information propagates instantly. Every fund that sees the announcement wants in. The valuation adjusts upward. The return adjusts downward.

2.1x is the return of a fully transparent asset. No information asymmetry. No edge. Just a well-known project priced correctly by a market that has been staring at it since the seed round. The American founder builds brands fast. The market recognizes them fast. And there is nothing left for anyone who arrives after recognition.

It is almost touching, the way capital follows capital in a perfect circle, each fund reassured by the presence of the others, each one imagining they have an edge. Nobody has an edge. They have proximity. Proximity to the same deal is not an advantage. It is the definition of competition.

## 2. The International Edge

The data on mixed-nationality teams is the most interesting pattern in the geography slice:

| Configuration | Days to ATH | Multiplier | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| All US | 88 | 2.1x | 250 |
| All EU | 106 | 2.7x | 217 |
| All CN | 70 | 3.0x | 104 |
| Has US (mixed) | 113 | 2.9x | 164 |
| Has EU (mixed) | 188 | 3.7x | 53 |
| Has CN (mixed) | 185 | 3.7x | 26 |
| Other | 108 | 3.1x | 379 |

Mixed teams — those with at least one European or Chinese founder alongside founders of other nationalities — return **3.7x**. They take longer (185-188 days), but the multiplier is 76% higher than All-US.

The reason is structural. A founding team that spans three continents doesn't form through the usual mimetic channels. They didn't meet at the same accelerator. They don't share the same investor network. They don't build what the Valley expects because they've never been in the Valley's echo chamber. The product that emerges from this heterogeneity is harder to classify, harder to value at entry, and therefore harder for the market to price correctly on day one.

The information asymmetry exists because the network asymmetry exists. Nobody has pipeline on these teams. Nobody is competing for the allocation. The deal sits there, underpriced, waiting for someone who doesn't rely on mimetic signals to find it.

## 3. All-CN: Fast and Fading

Chinese founding teams reach ATH fastest of all: 70 days. Multiplier: 3.0x. An attractive profile by any measure.

But the pool is shrinking. Chinese founder representation in the Top 500 has declined from 14% (early 2021) to roughly 12%. Regulation, capital controls, and the general hostility of the post-2021 Chinese regulatory environment have pushed founders to Singapore, Dubai, or pseudonymity.

The talent hasn't disappeared. The access has. And reduced access means reduced competition for deals, which means lower entry valuations, which means — if you can find the founders — better returns. The market punishes difficulty of access as if it were difficulty of quality. These are not the same thing.

## 4. Geography Is Frozen

Americas ~50%. Europe ~25%. Asia ~20%. These numbers barely move across five years of data. The projects that entered the top tiers early are still there. Geographic rotation is near zero.

Two trends are visible:

**American share is rising** — from 23% in 2022 to 28% in 2026 in the Top 500. The dominance is intensifying. This means the mimetic premium on US deals will only increase, and returns will continue to compress.

**Chinese share is declining** — from 14% to ~12%. Regulatory pressure. Capital flight. The gap this creates is structural: fewer Chinese deals in the pipeline means the remaining ones face less competition.

The market has calcified geographically. This is not surprising. Markets calcify. What is surprising is that anyone expects otherwise from an industry that calls itself decentralized while reproducing every pattern of geographic concentration that traditional finance perfected decades ago.

## 5. The Middle East Blind Spot

Here is a pattern that almost nobody discusses. The Middle East's share of Top 100 founders grew from 1% (2021) to 4% (2026). Dubai and Abu Dhabi have built regulatory frameworks faster than the market has noticed. Founders are relocating. Infrastructure is being built.

Almost no crypto VC has a Middle East office. Almost no fund has local pipeline. The region is not in anyone's model of where founders come from, so the capital doesn't flow, so the valuations stay low. This is the textbook anatomy of a mispricing: an asset class that grows while the capital that should price it looks elsewhere.

Whether this becomes interesting depends on whether the growth continues. The data so far says it does. The market so far says it hasn't noticed. There is a name for the gap between reality and perception. Depending on your temperament, you call it opportunity or you call it futility. The data doesn't care which.

## The Bottom Line

Based on 4,675 founders and 1,193 tokens:

1. **Mixed international teams** return 3.7x. Geographic diversity is the strongest nationality signal. Not because diversity is virtuous — because it breaks the mimetic pipeline.
2. **All-US teams** return 2.1x. The most competed-for deals have the lowest returns. This should surprise nobody. It surprises everybody.
3. **All-CN teams** return 3.0x in 70 days. Fast, profitable, shrinking. Access is the bottleneck.
4. **The Middle East** is the clearest emerging blind spot — growing share, zero VC coverage.
5. **Geography is a proxy for competition.** The more VCs cover a region, the less alpha remains. This is not insight. It is arithmetic.

There is something almost noble about the persistence of geographic bias in an industry built to transcend it. We had the technology to be borderless. We chose the same borders anyway. It's not anyone's fault. It's just how desire works — we want what we see others wanting, and we see others wanting what's close to us. The international team is far away, unfamiliar, difficult to diligence. Also: 3.7x.

## Methodology

**Data sources:** 2,741 companies, 4,675 founders (nationality known for 90%). CoinGecko market cap January 2021 to March 2026. 1,193 tokens with complete TGE-ATH data.

**Categories:** "Has EU/CN" = at least one founder of that nationality in a mixed team. "All US" = every identified founder is American.

**Limitations:** Small sample sizes (Has CN n=26). Nationality based on public identification. Correlation is not causation.
