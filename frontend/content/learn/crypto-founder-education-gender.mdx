The credential is the most efficient destroyer of alpha ever invented. It reduces uncertainty — which is its purpose — and in doing so, it reduces the information asymmetry that creates returns. The PhD is trusted, priced correctly, and delivers exactly what the market expects. Nothing more. This is not a flaw in the PhD. It is a flaw in expecting alpha from something the market already understands.

But the data doesn't stop there. It gets worse. Or better, depending on your willingness to be uncomfortable.

## Key Takeaways

- **All-PhD teams** reach ATH in 66 days at 1.7x — the fastest and lowest return. The market trusts credentials and prices them immediately. No asymmetry, no alpha.
- **No-education teams** return 3.5x. Most fail. The survivors are the founders the entire VC pipeline rejected. That rejection is the mispricing.
- **Mixed-gender teams** return **5.3x** vs 2.8x for all-male. This is the single strongest demographic signal. The sample (n=103) is modest but the gap is too large to dismiss.
- **Intergenerational teams** (10-19 year age spread) return **5.9x** — the highest multiplier of any category in the dataset. These teams don't form through the social cascades that produce mimetic homogeneity.
- **All Masters** teams hit 3.1x in 108 days — the pragmatic optimum between credibility and ceiling.

## 1. The PhD Ceiling

All-PhD teams: 66 days to ATH, 1.7x multiplier.

The 66 days is instructive. The market prices PhD-founded protocols almost instantly. The auditors trust the code. The researchers validate the approach. The VCs recognize the pedigree. Within two months, every relevant actor in the market has formed a view, and that view is accurate. The price reflects reality. There is no gap between perception and value.

This is what efficiency looks like. It is also what zero alpha looks like. They are the same thing.

The PhD is venture capital's comfort blanket. It de-risks the investment committee meeting. It makes the LP letter easy to write. It does everything a credential is supposed to do. And it costs you exactly the difference between 1.7x and what you could have made if you'd been willing to be wrong about someone.

## 2. The Dropout Lottery

No-education teams: 101 days to ATH, 3.5x multiplier.

This is the memecoin bracket. Community projects. Anonymous founders. Wild variance. Most of these die. The ones that survive return 10x or more. For the median outcome, it's a disaster. For the tail, it's the best risk-adjusted bet in the dataset.

The mechanism is not mysterious. The founder without a degree is the one the investment committee won't consider. Not because the data says they're worse — the data says the opposite — but because the committee's incentive structure penalizes unconventional bets. Being wrong on a Stanford PhD is forgivable. Being wrong on a dropout is career-ending.

This asymmetry of professional consequence creates a systematic mispricing. The founders nobody will fund are, by definition, the founders nobody has priced. The market expels them — collectively, reflexively, without examining the data — and in doing so, creates the exact inefficiency it claims to exploit.

There is a Girardian precision to this: the scapegoat mechanism applied to allocation. The community expels the unworthy, the violence of rejection restores order to the portfolio, and the expelled party turns out to have been innocent all along. We've been doing this for ten thousand years. We're very good at it.

## 3. The Education Map

The full picture, for those who prefer tables to philosophy:

The All Masters bracket deserves attention: 3.1x in 108 days. This is the pragmatic optimum. Enough credential for institutional trust. Not enough to trigger the PhD ceiling. If you're building a thesis around education and want the simplest heuristic, this is it.

Mixed Higher Ed teams (some PhDs, some lower) return only 2.1x — suggesting that the PhD's ceiling effect contaminates the entire team. The credential's gravitational pull toward correct pricing is stronger than the non-degreed founder's pull toward variance.

## 4. Gender: The Largest Signal

Mixed-gender teams return **5.3x**. All-male teams return **2.8x**.

This is the largest single demographic alpha in the dataset. Larger than age, nationality, or education. The gap is 89%. The sample is n=103 for mixed teams — modest, but the effect size is too large for sample noise to explain.

Mixed teams take longer (145 days vs 97 for all-male). They build for sustained growth rather than quick pumps. The market prices them more slowly because the market's pricing apparatus — VCs, analysts, KOLs — is overwhelmingly male, and overwhelmingly connected through male-dominated networks. The mixed team is not in these networks. It is not at these conferences. It is not in these group chats.

93% of crypto founding teams are all-male. The capital is concentrated on 93% of the supply. The remaining 7% absorbs almost none of the mimetic capital and returns nearly twice as much.

This is not a statement about gender. It is a statement about pipeline homogeneity. When every fund draws from the same network, every fund sees the same deals, every deal gets the same valuation, and the returns converge to the mean. The mixed-gender team is the team that broke free of the network — not by intention, but by exclusion. And exclusion, in a mimetic market, is the precondition for alpha.

All-female teams return 2.4x (n=29). The sample is too small for conclusions, but the pattern is consistent: it's not gender that creates alpha. It's the mix. The mix breaks the network.

## 5. Age Spread: The Anti-Mimetic Structure

Teams with 10-19 years of age spread between founders return **5.9x**. 20+ years: 4.7x. Same-age teams (0-4 year spread): 2.9x.

5.9x is the highest multiplier of any category in this study. Age, nationality, education, gender — nothing beats the intergenerational team.

The explanation is mechanical. A 50-year-old founder and a 28-year-old CTO did not meet at the same bootcamp. They are not in the same Telegram groups. They do not follow the same people on Twitter. They formed a team because they share a problem, not because they share a network. This is the opposite of how mimetic teams form.

Mimetic teams form through proximity: same school, same cohort, same accelerator class. They produce same-age, same-education, same-nationality founding teams. These teams are easy to find, easy to diligence, and easy to price. Which is why they return 2.9x.

The intergenerational team is hard to find because it doesn't emerge from the channels VCs monitor. It's hard to diligence because the founders don't fit the usual pattern. And it's hard to price because there's no comparable in the portfolio. All of which is exactly why it returns 5.9x.

## The Bottom Line

Based on 4,675 founders and 1,193 tokens:

1. **Mixed-gender teams** — 5.3x vs 2.8x. The largest alpha signal. Systematically underpriced because the pipeline is structurally homogeneous.
2. **Intergenerational teams** (10-19y spread) — 5.9x. The highest return of any category. Anti-mimetic by construction.
3. **All-PhD teams** — 66 days, 1.7x. Not bad investments. Just not alpha. The credential eliminates the asymmetry that makes venture capital work.
4. **No-education teams** — 3.5x. The scapegoat of the pipeline. Most die. The survivors are mispriced because nobody wanted them.
5. **All Masters** — 3.1x in 108 days. The practical optimum for institutional mandates.

It would be comforting to believe that the best returns come from the best founders. They don't. The best returns come from the founders the market has systematically failed to price — because they don't fit the pattern, because they make committees uncomfortable, because they weren't in the right network at the right time. This is not a market failure. It is the market working exactly as mimetic systems always work: crowding the familiar, ignoring the strange, and leaving 5.9x on the table for anyone willing to pick it up.

Nobody will pick it up, of course. The incentive structure forbids it. That's the beautiful thing about structural alpha — it persists precisely because the structure that creates it is also the structure that prevents its exploitation. We are, as always, our own obstacle.

## Methodology

**Data sources:** 2,741 companies, 4,675 founders. Education known for ~60%. Gender: 99% confirmed. CoinGecko market cap January 2021 to March 2026. 1,193 tokens with complete TGE-ATH data.

**Limitations:** Gender data based on public identification. Education data has ~40% gaps. Small samples for some categories (All Female n=29). Correlation is not causation.
