# Risks

Every way you can lose money on a Dex Traded Fund (DTF — a tokenized basket of crypto assets, like an on-chain ETF). Read before committing capital.

**Risk categories:**
- [Market risks](#market-risks) — price exposure to the underlying basket.
- [Structural risks](#structural-risks) — DTF lifecycle and protocol design.
- [Smart contract risks](#smart-contract-risks) — code, oracle, and bridge.
- [Lending risks](#lending-risks-morpho) — borrowing against DTF shares on Morpho.

Each risk below follows the same shape: what it is, how it can affect you, how to mitigate it.

---

## Market Risks

### NAV fluctuation

**What it is.** The NAV (Net Asset Value — portfolio value per share) tracks the prices of the underlying assets:

```
NAV = sum(qty[i] * price[i]) / 1e18
```

**How it can affect you.** If the basket drops 50%, your DTF is worth 50% less. There is no floor and no guaranteed value.

**How to mitigate.** Size your position to what you can afford to lose. Diversify across uncorrelated DTFs. Read the holdings before buying.

### Concentration risk

**What it is.** A DTF with few tokens concentrates exposure into a small number of bets. Market-cap-weighted DTFs naturally concentrate into the largest assets.

**How it can affect you.** A single asset collapsing has an outsized impact on your position. A three-token DTF puts ~33% at risk per asset.

**How to mitigate.** Use equal-weight or capped-market-cap strategies. Compare concentration vs. diversification via [Backtesting](/index/guides/backtesting).

### Drawdown

**What it is.** Peak-to-trough decline. Crypto markets routinely see -50% to -80% drawdowns.

**How it can affect you.** Your position can drop further than the broader market if the basket holds altcoins. Historical drawdowns are a lower bound, not an upper bound.

**How to mitigate.** Check `max_drawdown_pct` from backtests before deploying or buying. Size positions for the worst historical case.

### Delistings

**What it is.** An asset is removed from supporting exchanges and becomes illiquid.

**How it can affect you.** The DTF still holds the delisted asset; its price feed may freeze or drop to zero. The position cannot be cleanly exited.

**How to mitigate.** Prefer large-cap assets with broad exchange coverage. Monitor `total_delistings` in backtests — high values signal a flaky category.

## Structural Risks

### No auto-rebalance

**What it is.** Deployed DTFs do **not** auto-rebalance. Quantities are fixed until the deployer triggers a manual rebalance.

**How it can affect you.** Effective weights drift from target weights as prices move. The DTF you bought may no longer match its stated thesis.

**How to mitigate.** Check rebalance history on the DTF detail page before buying. Avoid DTFs with no rebalance activity over long periods. If you are the deployer, see [Rebalancing](/index/guides/rebalancing).

### Rebalance costs

**What it is.** Each rebalance incurs trading fees and gas. The AP (Authorized Participant — the off-chain fill agent) pays exchange fees on every delta trade.

**How it can affect you.** Frequent rebalancing in volatile markets erodes returns. Total cost shows up in the `total_fees_pct` backtest metric.

**How to mitigate.** Rebalance on drift thresholds (5–10%) rather than tight calendar cadences. Verify the deployer's stated cadence before buying.

### Price feed risk

**What it is.** NAV depends on price feeds from CoinGecko, Binance, and other sources aggregated by the data-node.

**How it can affect you.** A stale or manipulated feed produces an incorrect NAV. Trades execute at the wrong price.

**How to mitigate.** Oracle nodes enforce a 0.1% price tolerance, which catches most deviations. Larger deviations can still slip through — prefer DTFs with major assets that have many independent price sources.

## Smart Contract Risks

### BLS consensus dependency

**What it is.** All DTF operations require BLS (Boneless–Lynn–Shacham — a multi-signature consensus scheme) signatures from at least 2/3 of the oracle set.

**How it can affect you.** If fewer than 2/3 of oracles are online, trades and rebalances stall. Funds are not lost, but they are not movable until quorum returns.

**How to mitigate.** Avoid time-sensitive trading during oracle outages. Monitor protocol status before placing large orders.

### Contract bugs

**What it is.** Smart contracts are deployed code. Bugs in deployed code can be exploited.

**How it can affect you.** A bug in the contract logic can result in partial or total loss of funds in affected positions.

**How to mitigate.** Review the [contract architecture](/index/architecture/contracts). Limit exposure to amounts you can afford to lose entirely. Bug impact is capped at the deposited amount.

### Bridge risk

**What it is.** The cross-chain bridge between L3 (the protocol's own Layer 3 chain) and Settlement has its own trust assumptions and attack surface.

**How it can affect you.** A bridge exploit or failure can depeg collateral, freezing or destroying value held in transit.

**How to mitigate.** Prefer native L3 operations where possible. Avoid keeping large amounts in transit across the bridge longer than necessary.

## Lending Risks (Morpho)

Morpho is the lending protocol used for borrowing USDC against DTF collateral on Settlement.

### Liquidation

**What it is.** Forced sale of your collateral when the borrowed position's health drops below threshold.

**How it can affect you.** If the DTF used as collateral drops in value, the position is liquidated automatically. You lose collateral at a discount to market.

**How to mitigate.** Keep a health factor buffer well above 1.0 (1.5x or higher). Monitor positions continuously. Crypto can move faster than you can react.

### Health factor

**What it is.** The ratio that determines whether your borrow position is safe:

```
health_factor = (collateral_value * LTV) / debt
```

LTV = Loan-To-Value, set per Morpho market.

**How it can affect you.** When `health_factor < 1.0`, the position is immediately liquidatable. No grace period, no warning.

**How to mitigate.** Borrow conservatively — target a health factor of at least 1.5x. Set price alerts on the collateral DTF.

### Oracle risk (Morpho)

**What it is.** Morpho uses oracle prices to compute collateral value and trigger liquidations.

**How it can affect you.** If oracle prices diverge from real market prices, liquidations can fire incorrectly — or fail to fire when they should. Either case can cost you.

**How to mitigate.** Use larger health factor buffers for DTFs with volatile or thinly traded assets. Avoid extreme LTVs.

## Checklist: Before Buying a DTF

Five steps. ~10 min total.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1: Read the holdings">
    Open the DTF's detail page. Read every asset in the basket. The name describes intent; the holdings describe reality.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2: Check the backtest">
    Run or review a backtest with the same category and weighting strategy. See [Backtesting](/index/guides/backtesting).

    Focus on max drawdown and Sharpe ratio — not total return.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3: Understand the rebalance schedule">
    Check rebalance history on the detail page. A DTF with no recent rebalance activity has drifted from its target allocation.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4: Size your position">
    Do not put more into a DTF than you can afford to lose entirely. Start small. Scale up after observing the product across market conditions.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5: Monitor after buying">
    Check NAV periodically on the **Portfolio** page (current value, PnL, asset breakdown). Set your own stop-loss — the protocol does not enforce one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Checklist: Before Creating a DTF

Five steps. ~30 min total. DTF creation is irreversible — verify each step before deploying.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Step 1: Backtest the strategy">
    Use [Backtesting](/index/guides/backtesting) to simulate performance. Run a sweep across weighting strategies and `top_n` values.

    A backtest does not predict the future. It surfaces weaknesses in the thesis.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 2: Check asset availability">
    All assets must have active price feeds. Hit `/sim/categories` to see the tradeable universe. Untradeable assets cannot be deployed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 3: Set realistic fees">
    Use `spread_multiplier: 2.0` for illiquid altcoins. The default 0.1% underestimates real-world slippage on thin order books.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 4: Plan your rebalance cadence">
    Decide upfront how often you will rebalance.
    - Monthly: simple to maintain.
    - Threshold-based (drift > 5%): more efficient, requires monitoring.

    Choose a cadence you will actually hold.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Step 5: Communicate to buyers">
    Write a clear name and description. Buyers need to understand the thesis without reading the contract.

    Next: [Create a DTF](/index/guides/create-itp).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Risk Summary

Eight rows. Severity = potential impact. Mitigation = the action that reduces it.

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| Market downturn | **High** | Diversify, check max drawdown in backtests |
| Concentration risk | Medium | Use equal-weight or capped strategies |
| Delistings | Medium | Prefer large-cap assets, monitor holdings |
| No auto-rebalance | Medium | Set reminders, check weight drift periodically |
| Price feed staleness | Low | Oracle 0.1% tolerance catches most issues |
| Contract bug | Low | Limited to deposited amount |
| Lending liquidation | **High** | Monitor health factor, keep buffer above 1.5x |
| Bridge risk | Medium | Prefer native assets where possible |
