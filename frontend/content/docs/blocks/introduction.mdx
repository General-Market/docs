# Blocks

We thought a German train would be late. It was. We made nothing from it, because we had told three people first. Blocks is the venue we built afterwards, mostly out of embarrassment.

A sealed bet is a small mechanism. Pick a number, pick a side, seal the commitment. Nobody sees the bet until the market closes. Nobody runs ahead of it. Nobody fades it on entry. Nobody buys the decision from a broker. The mechanism is older than electricity. We did not invent it. We just noticed nobody else was using it.

## Each market thins the predators

Spot is the open field. Every extraction works there. Cross-venue arbitrage, frontrunning, stop hunting, orderflow buying, spoofing, latency races, insider trading, toxic-flow making — eight strategies, all profitable, all paid for by someone who showed up to take a directional view. We have been that someone, more than once.

Perpetuals refuse one of the eight. Options refuse two more. Blocks refuses all of them. Not by policy. By construction. Policy is what a venue promises in its terms of service. Construction is what the bytecode allows.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/blocks/predator-matrix.svg" alt="Predator extraction matrix — nine strategies across four venues (SPOT, PERPS, OPTIONS, BLOCKS). Spot is vulnerable to all eight predator strategies; perps refuses cross-venue arbitrage; options refuses stop-and-liquidation hunting in addition; Blocks refuses all eight by construction. The final row, directional bets, is the only honest activity and is possible at every venue. Eight ways to lose, one bet that still works." />
</Frame>

The bets are sealed before they exist on-chain. No orderbook to spoof, no mempool to scan, no broker to sell the flow. Resolution comes from external data verified by an oracle network with BLS aggregate signatures. Winners split the losers' stakes minus a 0.05% fee. The pool is parimutuel. There is no counterparty to fade the bet, because there is no counterparty.

## How a block resolves

Four steps separate the conviction from its consequence.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/blocks/four-steps.svg" alt="The four steps — Browse, Seal, Resolve, Claim. Step 1 Browse: pick a batch from 90+ source categories like Crypto, Weather, Trains, Quakes. Step 2 Seal: bet UP or DOWN per market, sealed as keccak256(bitmap) on-chain so nobody can see your block until the tick closes. Step 3 Resolve: tick ends, oracles fetch real data, winners split the losers' stakes under 2-of-3 consensus. Step 4 Claim: withdraw winnings with a BLS-verified balance proof, any time, no lockup." />
</Frame>

<Info>
Blocks are **sealed** — a hash of the bitmap goes on-chain first, the bytes go to the oracles after the tick closes. Nobody sees the bet in advance. The error, when it comes, stays private until it is settled.
</Info>

## Why this and not the alternatives

Most venues let a trader take a directional view, then quietly sell the right to copy it. Blocks does not, because the act of placing a block is encrypted. The architecture refuses the abuse instead of promising not to commit it. The difference matters more than we expected.

## What can you bet on?

The breadth is the point. Anything that produces a number can become a block. Each one a small confession that somebody, somewhere — usually us, on a Tuesday — believes they know what happens next.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/blocks/example-questions.svg" alt="Twelve example questions paired with their source identifiers — Will Bitcoin be higher in 5 minutes (crypto_BTC-USD), will it rain more in Tokyo tomorrow (weather_tokyo), will an earthquake hit California (earthquake_calif), will the Northern Line have disruptions (tfl_tube_northern), will Counter-Strike 2 gain players (steam_cs2), will npm react downloads increase (npm_react), will McDonald's ice cream break in Chicago (mcbroken_chicago), will this GitHub repo get more stars (github_rust-lang), will Deutsche Bahn trains be late in Berlin (db_trains_berlin), will Palo Verde nuclear output change (nrc_palo-verde-1), will wave height increase at buoy 41002 (ndbc_41002), will LA air quality worsen (airnow_los-angeles)." />

Every block runs the same mechanic. A data source produces a number. A bet picks UP or DOWN. The outcome is read from real-world data. The universe does not care about the block. It moves, or it does not. The pool pays accordingly.

## The data pipeline

Ninety-eight APIs feed numbers into a Rust process that feeds them into a database that feeds them into smart contracts that redistribute money between strangers. The architecture is sound. The premise is absurd. We spent a year arguing which of the two was the bigger problem before deciding it did not matter.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/blocks/data-pipeline.svg" alt="Data pipeline — external APIs (CoinGecko, USGS, TfL, GitHub, NDBC, and 85 others) feed into a single Rust data node that collects via HTTP, validates with bounds and staleness checks, and stores time-series rows in Postgres. Three independent oracles read snapshots, resolve ticks, BLS-sign their result, and reach 2-of-3 consensus. Smart contracts: VisionPool.sol holds sealed bitmaps, VisionClaim.sol verifies BLS claims, VisionReserve.sol custodies the parimutuel USDC pool." />
</Frame>

<Info>
The on-chain contracts keep their original `Vision*` names from the protocol's earlier identity. The product is Blocks; the bytecode remembers what we used to call it.
</Info>

## Who is Blocks for?

Anyone who has looked at a number and felt, with irrational certainty, that they knew which way it would go. We feel this most mornings. It is rarely correct, and never less interesting for being wrong.
