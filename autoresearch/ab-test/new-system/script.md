# Vision — VC Presentation Video Script

## Context
- Product: Vision by General Market — sealed parimutuel prediction market
- 98+ data sources, 400,000+ markets
- Testnet Point Season 1 Live
- URL: GeneralMarket.com

## Tone
Cioran method. Short declarative sentences. No corporate fluff. Setup → pivot → knife. The last sentence carries all the weight.

## Format
- 16:9 landscape, 45 seconds. Landscape because this is infrastructure, not entertainment — the aspect ratio should feel like a terminal, not a TikTok.
- Primary delivery: VC cold outreach attachment (must work in a Gmail embed with no sound). Secondary: Twitter/X embed, website hero.
- The 45-second constraint is absolute. A 46-second cut means a line was wasted.

## Visual Rhythm
The montage accelerates on BOTH tracks. Footage cuts: first pair (Twitch, Steam) 2.5s, middle pair (PumpFun, 4chan) 2s, final trio (McDonald's, Military, Solar) 1.5s. "Exclusive Trade" cards: 1.5s → 1s → 0.7s. The cards shrink to almost-subliminal — the viewer barely processes them before the next source hits. Then "And 400,000 more" lands in stillness — 3 full seconds. The deceleration is the argument.

## Sound Design
No voiceover. No music during montage — only the mechanical THUD of each card cut (a single low-frequency hit, like a stamp on paper). Silence is the texture. After "And 400,000 more," a low sustained drone enters and holds through the mechanism lines. It fades to nothing during the 2-second black beat. The final card appears in absolute silence — no thud, no drone, no ambience. The absence of sound on the thesis is the loudest moment in the video.

## Typography
All text: Inter Bold, white on pure black (#000). One weight, one font, no variation — the monotony is deliberate. The system doesn't perform personality. "Exclusive Trade" cards: 48pt centered (institutional, not playful). Market descriptor text: 24pt, left-aligned, appearing 0.3s after the visual (the delay forces the viewer to process the image first). Final card: 64pt — the only size escalation, because only the thesis earns emphasis.

## Visual Argument
Every footage shot must show DATA IN MOTION — numbers changing, graphs updating, feeds scrolling. Never a static screenshot. The visual argument is: the world is generating data constantly, and every fluctuation is a tradeable event. The motion itself is the product demonstration.

## Color
Monochrome throughout. The only color comes from the data source footage (departure boards, stream overlays, radar screens). The contrast between vivid source footage and black-and-white cards creates the visual argument: the real world is colorful, the trading layer is austere.

## Running Counter
Bottom-right corner of each footage shot: a small white counter showing the CUMULATIVE number of markets so far. Inter Regular (not Bold — the counter is ambient, not declarative), 14pt, 60% opacity. First shot (DB trains): "847 markets". After Twitch: "10,847". After Steam: "11,200". It keeps climbing. The counter never resets. By the time "And 400,000 more" appears, the viewer has been watching a number climb for 25 seconds — the text confirms what they already felt.

## Production Handoff

**Audience brief**: Crypto-native VCs who evaluate 50+ deals a month. They have pattern-matched every pitch format. The only way in is disorientation — show them something they have never seen positioned as a financial product. The script works by making them feel the scale before explaining it, and by treating the product's absurd breadth not as a feature but as an inevitability they arrived at too late. The voice is the product: austere, precise, unsentimental. If the video feels like a pitch, it failed.

**Three lines they will quote**:
1. "Liquidity was never the problem. Imagination was."
2. "Their viewer counts are markets. Nobody told them."
3. "Every one of these markets exists. None of them have a counterparty."

**One known weakness**: The video assumes the viewer knows what parimutuel means. A non-crypto VC (traditional finance, generalist) might stall at "Sealed parimutuel" and lose the mechanism section. For those audiences, this script needs a 60-second cut with one additional explanatory beat.

**One thing the rubric didn't measure**: Whether the video creates URGENCY. The script explains what exists and why it matters, but it doesn't convey why NOW — why a VC should act on this deal in the next 48 hours rather than bookmarking it. The closing insight ("Imagination was") is memorable but not urgent. Urgency would require a market-timing argument or a competitive threat, which would compromise voice. This is a deliberate trade-off.
