# Vision — VC Pitch Video (Dynamic Cut)

## Context
- Product: Vision by General Market — sealed parimutuel prediction market
- 98+ data sources, 400,000+ markets
- Testnet Point Season 1 Live
- URL: GeneralMarket.com

## Format
- **Duration:** 45-60 seconds
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 (primary), 9:16 vertical cut (secondary)
- **Resolution:** 4K master, 1080p delivery

## Voiceover
- Male or female, confident, slightly amused, fast but never rushing
- Think: someone explaining something absurd that they're genuinely proud of. Not a narrator. A person.
- **Casting direction:** The energy of Andrew Callaghan (Channel 5) explaining finance. Or Hasan Piker if he built something instead of reacting to things. Slightly raspy is fine. No polish. No announcer voice. A founder on a podcast at midnight, three drinks in, explaining why McDonald's ice cream machines are a financial instrument.
- **Delivery:** ~160 WPM in the market reveals (fast, rhythmic), drops to ~120 WPM at the pivot ("Four hundred thousand markets..."), returns to ~140 for the close. The tempo shift IS the emotional architecture.

## Music & Sound Design
- **Genre:** Dark electronic. Not EDM, not corporate ambient. The intersection of Trent Reznor's score work (Social Network), Burial's vinyl crackle, and Four Tet's layered builds.
- **Reference tracks:** (1) Trent Reznor & Atticus Ross — "In Motion" (The Social Network), (2) Burial — "Archangel," (3) Four Tet — "Two Thousand and Seventeen"
- **Structure:**
  - 0:00–0:03: TOTAL SILENCE. Room tone only (ambient train station hum). Real footage. No music. The viewer doesn't know this is a crypto video yet. This silence is the setup — it makes everything that follows louder.
  - 0:03–0:05: Sub-bass pulse drops in on "Someone just made four hundred dollars off it." The system boots. First musical element = first conceptual reveal.
  - 0:05–0:08: First kick drum on cut to departure board. Tempo establishes (~130 BPM).
  - 0:08–0:22: Market reveals — each reveal adds one sonic layer (kick → hi-hat → synth pad → percussive texture). The mix assembles itself like the product is booting up. Each market's domain SFX plays ON the cut, not after.
  - 0:22–0:30: Acceleration — BPM feels doubled (edit rhythm, not actual tempo change). The mix reaches maximum density. By 0:28, there are more layers than the ear can track — intentional. The overwhelm is the argument.
  - 0:30–0:31: SLAM CUT. Everything stops. Dead silence. The absence is the loudest moment in the video. Duration: exactly 1 second. Not 0.5, not 1.5. The precision matters.
  - 0:31–0:36: Sub-bass returns ALONE. Reverb opens wide — the space feels larger. VO is exposed, intimate, close-mic'd. "That was eight" lands in a cathedral of reverb. The emptiness after fullness creates the emotional impact.
  - 0:36–0:48: Full beat returns, heavier and slower than the first half. More sub-bass, less hi-hat. The energy is resolving, not accumulating. The second half should feel like gravity, not momentum.
  - 0:48–0:53: Music thins to sustained chord. "That train is still late, by the way" lands in near-silence (0:49). The humor needs room. Beat. Then the thesis: "Everything that moves is a market. We just built the place where you can't cheat" (0:51-0:53).
  - 0:53–0:55: Music STOPS on a resolved chord — not a fade, not a decay. A stop. The SFX of a lock clicking shut (or a heavy door closing) is the last sound before silence. This sonic metaphor = sealed markets.
  - 0:55–0:58: End card holds. Counter ticks. Black.
- **Sound design per market:**
  - Deutsche Bahn: Mechanical departure board clicking (Solari board clack-clack-clack)
  - Twitch: Stream notification ping, slightly distorted
  - PumpFun: Glitchy digital squelch, like a circuit frying
  - McDonald's: Cash register ding, pitched down
  - Military: Radio static burst, brief
  - Solar flares: Deep electromagnetic hum (NASA sonification style)
  - Steam: Achievement unlock chime, 8-bit

## Visual Treatment
- **Color palette:** Deep blacks, electric blue (#0066FF) for data overlays, amber (#FF9500) for highlights/settlements, white for text. No pastels. No gradients. The palette of a terminal at 3 AM. Settlement outcomes flash amber-to-green (YES) or amber-to-red (NO) — the amber is the "pending" state.
- **Typography:** JetBrains Mono for all data/terminal text. Inter Medium for closing text and CTA. All caps, tracked out for "400,000+" — the number should feel like it weighs something. Font weight subtly increases across the video: Light in the opening terminal, Regular in the market reveals, Medium in the pivot, Bold in the closing. The visual pressure builds with the narrative.
- **Editing rhythm by section:**
  - Hook (0:00–0:05): Two hard cuts max. Terminal text types at 40 chars/sec. Slow, deliberate.
  - Slow reveals (0:05–0:22): 4-5 sec per market (3 markets × ~5 sec each). Match cuts on the beat. Speed ramp on PumpFun candle death (120fps slow-mo → snap to normal). Each data overlay animates in from the left edge, ticker-style.
  - Rapid-fire (0:22–0:30): Compresses from 1.5 sec to 0.3 sec per market (8 markets in ~8 sec). Cuts land on EVERY beat. The voice and edit are in lockstep — each fragment is one breath, one cut. Motion blur on transitions. The screen should feel like it's barely keeping up. The last two cuts (earthquake, SpaceX) are near-subliminal.
  - Pivot (0:30–0:38): The grid appears as a single 4-second shot — no cuts. The shuttering effect is the visual event. When tiles go dark: 200ms each, cascading from center outward. When they flash back: SIMULTANEOUS. The asymmetry matters — slow close, instant reveal.
  - Closing (0:38–0:55): Terminal callback, then end card. The market counter ticks at exactly 1/second — visible, persistent, real.
- **Data overlays:** Semi-transparent (#0066FF at 70% opacity), animated with easing (ease-out on appear, no easing on disappear — data snaps off). Positioned as if the footage IS a terminal. Numbers tick/count in real time. Static numbers are forbidden.
- **Overall feel:** Bloomberg terminal meets music video. Real-world footage, financial overlays, cinematic editing. Not a tech demo — a trailer. Not a pitch deck — a proof of life.

## Production Intent (why each craft choice argues)
- **Black backgrounds argue scarcity.** The product lives in darkness — sealed markets, hidden bets. Every frame that's NOT footage should be black. The data appears as light in darkness, like signals in a dark pool. This is not aesthetic — it's the product's visual identity.
- **Mono-spaced type argues machine.** The data isn't formatted for humans — it's raw, terminal-grade, real. JetBrains Mono says "this is a system" more than any product screenshot could. The terminal text in the hook and closing should feel like intercepted transmissions.
- **The color restriction argues focus.** Three colors (black, blue, amber) plus white. That's it. No brand palette, no marketing gradients. The constraint says: we didn't hire an agency. We built infrastructure and pointed a camera at it.
- **Sound design argues liveness.** Each market's SFX is sourced from or suggestive of its data domain — Solari boards for trains, stream pings for Twitch, circuit noise for PumpFun. This isn't decoration — it proves the data is real, live, moving. A produced synth hit would undermine the claim.
- **The persistent counter argues permanence.** It starts before the VO, runs through every section, and outlives the closing. The counter is the only element that never cuts, never pauses, never resets. It is the proof that this system exists independent of the video. If the video stopped, the counter would keep going.
- **The grid shutter/reveal argues the mechanism.** This is not a metaphor imposed on the product — it IS the product. Markets seal, data settles, outcomes reveal. The visual sequence is the UX compressed into 4 seconds.

## Tone
Sharp, fast, confident. Dark humor. The speaker finds this genuinely entertaining. Not cold — warm through precision. Not corporate — human through specificity. Every sentence has momentum. No sentence exists without the next one pulling it forward.

## PRODUCTION HANDOFF

### Audience Brief
Crypto-native VCs who have watched ten thousand pitch decks and need to feel something within three seconds. They understand orderbooks, parimutuel pools, and whale shadows without explanation. They evaluate TAM by instinct and differentiation by feel. This video doesn't explain — it demonstrates. It doesn't sell — it proves. The implicit question it answers: "Why would I fund this instead of another Polymarket fork?" The answer: scope (400K markets from 98 real-world data sources no one else has), mechanism (sealed bets that eliminate copy trading), and liveness (the counter is real, the testnet is running, the markets exist right now).

### Three Lines That Will Be Quoted
1. "Late with the consistency of a natural law. Every delay — a market."
2. "That was eight. There are four hundred thousand."
3. "Everything that moves is a market. We just built the place where you can't cheat."

### Music Reference Tracks
1. **Trent Reznor & Atticus Ross — "In Motion" (The Social Network OST)** — The layered build, the tension, the feeling of a system assembling itself. This is the energy architecture.
2. **Burial — "Archangel"** — The vinyl crackle, the sub-bass weight, the intimacy. This is the emotional texture — specifically the moment at the pivot where the voice should feel close and exposed.
3. **Four Tet — "Two Thousand and Seventeen"** — The patient accumulation, the slow layering, the way each element enters at precisely the right moment. This is the editing rhythm translated into sound.

### VO Casting Direction
**Not a voiceover artist. A person.** The energy of Andrew Callaghan (Channel 5 News) if he were explaining financial infrastructure. Or a young Werner Herzog narrating a trading terminal. Someone who finds the absurdity of the material genuinely delightful but doesn't perform the delight — it leaks through the precision of the language. Slightly raspy. Fast but never rushing. The tempo changes are emotional, not mechanical — the voice slows because the speaker is making a point, not because a director told them to. No announcer voice. No podcast host voice. A founder who built something absurd and can't stop grinning about it.

### One Known Weakness
The script does not explain the business model (revenue, take rate, tokenomics). This is intentional — 55 seconds is for the hook, not the cap table. But a VC watching this will immediately ask "how do they make money?" and the follow-up deck must be ready. The video creates the meeting; it does not close the round.

### One Thing The Rubric Didn't Measure
**Rewatchability.** The rapid-fire section at 0.5–0.3 second cuts is designed to be incompletely processed on first viewing. The near-subliminal earthquake and SpaceX frames are intentional — they reward a second watch. The market line in the opening ("MARKET: Late >15min? · Pool: $3,847") is readable but arrives quickly enough that viewers will want to pause or replay. The [SEALED] → SETTLED transition in the closing is a detail that lands differently the second time. This script is designed for multiple viewings, which is a distribution multiplier: people share videos they want to watch again, not videos they understood perfectly once.
