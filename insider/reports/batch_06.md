# Batch 06 — Prediction Market Mechanism Design & Elicitation Papers

## PM119 — Jose, Nau & Winkler (2008), "Scoring Rules, Generalized Entropy, and Utility Maximization"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Theoretical presentation on the mathematical connections between proper scoring rules, divergence measures (KL, power, pseudospherical), and utility maximization under the HARA family. Explores weighted scoring rules with non-uniform baseline distributions. Purely mechanism-design mathematics — no empirical data on insider trading, extraction rates, or adverse selection in prediction/betting markets.

---

## PM120 — Savage (1971), "Elicitation of Personal Probabilities and Expectations"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Foundational paper on proper scoring rules for eliciting subjective probabilities. Establishes the general characterization that any proper scoring rule corresponds to a convex function, with truthful reporting being optimal under risk neutrality. Seminal theoretical contribution but contains no empirical analysis of informed trading, insider extraction, or market maker losses.

---

## PM121 — Nolde & Ziegel (2016), "Elicitability and Backtesting: Perspectives for Banking Regulation"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Concerns backtesting of risk measures (VaR, Expected Shortfall, expectiles) in banking regulation. Proposes comparative backtests using strictly consistent scoring functions to rank forecasting methods. The paper is about financial risk measurement accuracy, not about informed/insider trading in prediction markets or adverse selection costs.

---

## PM122 — Schlag, Tremewan & van der Weele (2013), "A Penny for Your Thoughts: A Survey of Methods for Eliciting Beliefs"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Comprehensive survey of incentivized belief elicitation methods in experimental economics — proper scoring rules (quadratic, logarithmic, spherical), mechanisms for non-risk-neutral subjects, and practical implementation issues (order effects, hedging, presentation). Discusses theoretical conditions under which truthful reporting is optimal but provides no data on insider extraction rates, informed trader advantages, or adverse selection in markets.

---

## PM123 — Offerman, Sonnemans, van de Kuilen & Wakker (2009), "A Truth-Serum for Non-Bayesians"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Generalizes proper scoring rules from expected value maximization to non-expected utility theories (prospect theory, ambiguity aversion). Shows how risk attitudes distort classical proper scoring rules and proposes corrections. An experiment demonstrates feasibility. Purely about belief measurement methodology — no data on insider trading, informed trader extraction, or market maker losses.

---

## PM124 — Karni (2009), "A Mechanism for Eliciting Probabilities"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Introduces a direct revelation mechanism for eliciting subjective probabilities where truthful reporting is a dominant strategy — not merely a Nash equilibrium. The mechanism draws a random number r from uniform [0,1] and rewards the agent based on whether her report exceeds r. Elegant theoretical result but entirely about mechanism design, not about informed trading or adverse selection.

---

## PM125 — Prelec (2004), "Bayesian Truth Serum for Subjective Data"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Introduces the Bayesian Truth Serum (BTS) scoring instrument for non-verifiable claims (where prediction markets cannot operate). Each respondent provides an endorsement and a prediction of the sample distribution; the mechanism rewards "surprisingly common" answers. Truthful reporting is incentive-compatible in large samples. Discusses prediction markets as a comparison point but provides no quantitative data on insider extraction, informed trader advantages, or adverse selection.

---

## PM126 — Miller, Resnick & Zeckhauser (2005), "Eliciting Informative Feedback: The Peer-Prediction Method"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Proposes the peer prediction method for eliciting honest feedback when ground truth is unobservable (e.g., product reviews on eBay, Netflix). Agents are scored based on how well their report predicts another agent's report, using proper scoring rules and a common prior. Truthful reporting is a strict Nash equilibrium. The paper addresses incentive design for honest reporting, not informed/insider trading dynamics in markets.

---

## PM127 — Witkowski & Parkes (2012), "Peer Prediction without a Common Prior"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Extends the peer prediction method to settings where agents hold subjective private priors (rather than sharing a common prior). Introduces the "shadow" peer prediction mechanism exploiting temporal structure — agents report a belief before receiving their signal and a signal after. Provides strict incentive compatibility. Purely theoretical mechanism design; no data on insider trading, extraction rates, or adverse selection.

---

## PM128 — Radanovic & Faltings (2014), "Incentives for Truthful Information Elicitation of Continuous Signals"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Proposes the Divergence-based Bayesian Truth Serum for eliciting continuous private signals from agents. Extends BTS to small populations and non-binary signals. The information score penalizes agents whose information report agrees with a peer but whose prediction report diverges. Theoretical contribution to incentive-compatible information elicitation; contains no empirical data on informed trading or insider extraction in prediction markets.

---

## PM129 — Dasgupta & Ghosh (2013), "Crowdsourced Judgement Elicitation with Endogenous Proficiency"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Addresses crowdsourcing settings where agent proficiency is endogenous (depends on strategic effort choice). Proposes a mechanism where agents are rewarded for agreement with a reference agent but penalized for "blind agreement" using a cross-task statistic. Full-effort truthful reporting is the maximum-payoff Nash equilibrium. Focused on effort incentivization in crowdsourcing, not insider trading or adverse selection in betting/prediction markets.

---

## PM130 — Abernethy & Frongillo (2012), "A Characterization of Scoring Rules for Linear Properties"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Provides a full characterization of proper scoring rules for linear properties (expectations under a distribution), showing any such rule can be written as a Bregman divergence. Applies results to prediction market design — establishes equivalence between scoring rules for linear properties and automated market makers (connecting to Hanson's LMSR and Chen-Pennock cost-function framework). Purely theoretical; no empirical data on insider extraction or informed trader advantages.

---

## PM131 — Shi, Conitzer & Guo (2009), "Prediction Mechanisms That Do Not Incentivize Undesirable Actions"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Studies "principal-aligned" prediction mechanisms — scoring rules designed to avoid incentivizing agents to take real-world actions that harm the principal (e.g., terrorism prediction markets incentivizing attacks, or in-house markets incentivizing sabotage). Characterizes all principal-aligned proper scoring rules and proves an O(n) "overpayment" result: principal-aligned mechanisms cost n times as much as non-aligned ones. Interesting for market design but provides no data on insider extraction rates or informed trader advantages.

---

## PM132 — Boutilier (2012), "Eliciting Forecasts from Self-interested Experts"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Develops scoring rules for settings where experts have inherent interest in the principal's decision (e.g., a division head misreporting demand to influence R&D allocation). Introduces "compensation rules" that, combined with the expert's utility for decisions, induce proper scoring rules. Derives bounds on expert incentive to misreport and principal utility loss under uncertainty about expert preferences. Addresses strategic misreporting but from a mechanism design perspective — no quantitative data on insider extraction or adverse selection in prediction/betting markets.

---

## PM133 — Berg, Neumann & Rietz (2005), "Searching for Google's Value: Using Prediction Markets to Forecast Market Capitalization Prior to an IPO"

NO RELEVANT DATA on insider extraction rates per se. The paper uses Iowa Electronic Markets to forecast Google's post-IPO market capitalization. Key finding: traders accurately forecast post-IPO value weeks before the IPO, for very little payment — evidence against asymmetric-information theories of IPO underpricing that require large payments to overcome information gaps. Google's 15% underpricing (~$300M left on table) is attributed to intentional exchange for future benefits rather than winner's curse or information asymmetry. While the paper discusses information aggregation and asymmetric information, it does not quantify insider extraction rates, adverse selection costs, or informed trader advantages in the prediction market itself.

---

## PM134 — Ho & Chen (2007), "New Product Blockbusters: The Magic and Science of Prediction Markets"

NO RELEVANT DATA. Practitioner-oriented article describing how to set up prediction markets for new product demand forecasting (Hewlett-Packard and similar corporate applications). Explains market design principles (incentive, indicator, improvement, independence, crowd), recommends at least 50 participants and $500 average compensation per participant. Discusses how informed individuals naturally receive more weight through larger bets. No quantitative data on insider extraction rates, adverse selection, or market maker losses.

---

## PM135 — Gillen, Plott & Shum (2019), "A Pari-mutuel Like Mechanism for Information Aggregation: A Field Test Inside Intel"

Marginally relevant. This paper reports a long-running field test of a pari-mutuel Information Aggregation Mechanism (IAM) inside Intel for sales forecasting. Key design observations relevant to insider dynamics:
- Participants were selected by information level: (i) Street, (ii) Intel general, (iii) Intel specific, (iv) Intel technical. "Prediction" groups of 5-10 people from categories (iii) and (iv) produced the best forecasts. Groups with many participants from (i) or (ii) "tended to produce predictions that mirrored public knowledge, drowning out the informed signals from insiders."
- The IAM's expected-value forecast outperformed Intel's official internal forecast in 63% of runs. Optimal combination of forecasts heavily weighted the IAM positively and the official forecast negatively.
- Ticket prices increase over time to discourage free-riding on others' information — directly addressing the strategic timing problem of informed vs. uninformed participants.
- No quantitative estimates of insider extraction rate or adverse selection costs. The paper demonstrates that insiders drive forecast accuracy but does not measure the "tax" imposed on uninformed participants.

---

## PM136 — Almenberg, Kittlitz & Pfeiffer (2009), "An Experiment on Prediction Markets in Science"

Marginally relevant. Laboratory experiment testing prediction markets for scientific hypothesis testing under three information settings: (1) all public, (2) all private, (3) private then public. Key findings on informed trader dynamics:
- Participants with private information gained statistically significant profit from their information advantage. The correlation between "support" (how much a test result supports the true hypothesis) and net profit was significant in both Setting 2 (private info, p<0.04) and Setting 3 (private-then-public, p<0.01).
- Markets with private information (Setting 2) showed higher mispricing than public-information markets — 3 of 6 private-info markets failed to aggregate information entirely (prices 50-80% away from correct probabilities).
- Familiarity with Bayes' rule was positively correlated with profit in the private-info setting (p=0.05).
- Subsidizing market maker turned zero-sum into positive-sum, so informed traders profited without necessarily extracting from uninformed — the market maker absorbed losses.
- No quantitative extraction rate. The paper confirms informed traders earn profits from private information but does not isolate the adverse selection cost to uninformed participants.

---

## PM137 — Dreber, Pfeiffer, Almenberg et al. (2015), "Using Prediction Markets to Estimate the Reproducibility of Scientific Research"

NO RELEVANT DATA on insider extraction. The paper uses prediction markets to forecast whether 44 psychology studies would replicate in the Reproducibility Project: Psychology. Markets correctly predicted 71% of replication outcomes (vs. 58% for survey). Estimated median prior probability of tested hypotheses being true was only 8.8% — implying ~43% of "statistically significant" findings are false positives. Mean final market prices of 55% indicated participants expected roughly half of studies to replicate. While the paper demonstrates prediction market accuracy and Bayesian inference about scientific hypotheses, it contains no data on informed trader extraction rates, adverse selection, or market maker losses.
