## 1. FOUNDATIONAL THEORY — Market Microstructure & Information Economics

The bedrock. These papers built the language everyone else borrows.

| # | Paper | Authors | Year | Journal | ~Citations | Key Contribution |
|---|-------|---------|------|---------|-----------|-----------------|

| 3 | Bid, Ask, and Transaction Prices in a Specialist Market with Heterogeneously Informed Traders | Glosten, Milgrom | 1985 | JFE | 8,500+ | Spread exists because of informed traders. Sequential trade model. Adverse selection priced into every quote. |

| 6 | Price, Trade Size, and Information in Securities Markets | Easley, O'Hara | 1987 | JFE | 3,800+ | Trade size conveys information. Large trades signal informed trading. |
| 7 | Time and the Process of Security Price Adjustment | Easley, O'Hara | 1992 | JF | 3,200+ | Time between trades is informative. No-trade intervals signal absence of private info. |
| 8 | Insider Trading: Should It Be Prohibited? | Leland | 1992 | JPE | 2,800+ | Welfare analysis. Insider trading can improve price efficiency but redistributes wealth. The unresolvable tension. |
| 9 | Information and the Cost of Capital | Easley, O'Hara | 2004 | JF | 4,000+ | Private info is a non-diversifiable risk factor. More informed trading = higher cost of equity. |

| 13 | Information in Securities Markets: Kyle Meets Glosten and Milgrom | Back, Baruch | 2004 | Econometrica | 600+ | Unifies the two foundational models. Shows Kyle and GM are limiting cases of a general framework. |
